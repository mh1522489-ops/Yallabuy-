    Sony WH-1000XM5 Sound Cutting Out: Causes and Fixes

    If your Sony WH-1000XM5 keeps cutting out, stuttering, dropping audio for a few seconds, or randomly losing sound while still showing as connected, the problem can be frustrating. The good news is that a sound-cutting-out problem does not automatically mean your headphones are defective.

    8 min read
    Updated Set 2026

    YallaBuy Transparency: YallaBuy has not personally tested every scenario described in this guide on a WH-1000XM5 unit. The information below is based on Sony's official documentation, independent sources, and owner reports. We clearly distinguish between official information and user experiences rather than presenting owner reports as laboratory testing.

    What Does "Sound Cutting Out" Actually Mean?

    "Sound cutting out" can describe several different problems. For example, you might experience:

    • Audio cutting out for a fraction of a second.
    • Repeated Bluetooth stuttering.
    • Audio disappearing for several seconds.
    • Music stopping while the headphones remain connected.
    • Audio cutting out when you move away from your phone.
    • The headphones working normally on your phone but cutting out on Windows.
    • Problems appearing only when two devices are connected.
    • Audio interruptions when using LDAC.
    • Audio disappearing after pausing and resuming a video.
    • The headphones working normally with a cable but not over Bluetooth.

    These situations can have completely different causes. That's why the first step should not be a factory reset. The first step should be figuring out where the problem is happening.

    Why Does the Sony WH-1000XM5 Keep Cutting Out?

    Think of the audio connection as a chain: Headphones → Bluetooth → Playback Device → Operating System → App. A problem anywhere along that chain can cause interruptions. Some of the most common possibilities include:

    1. Bluetooth instability

    Bluetooth performance can be affected by distance, obstacles, and interference. Sony specifically recommends reducing the distance between the headphones and the connected device and removing obstacles when Bluetooth audio becomes unstable.

    2. Wireless interference

    Bluetooth operates in the 2.4GHz spectrum, which is also used by various Wi-Fi and wireless devices. In a crowded wireless environment, interference can become more noticeable.

    3. Multipoint

    The WH-1000XM5 supports connecting to two devices simultaneously. That's convenient when switching between your phone and computer, but it also means the headphones are managing multiple Bluetooth connections. Sony explains that the first connected device can continue communicating with the headphones and potentially interfere with switching audio to the second device.

    4. Windows Bluetooth problems

    A particularly important scenario is when the WH-1000XM5 works perfectly with your phone but cuts out on your Windows PC. In that situation, the headphones aren't necessarily the problem. Bluetooth drivers, adapters, antennas, Windows audio settings, and the PC's wireless environment can all be involved.

    5. Bluetooth hardware

    Desktop PCs can be especially sensitive to Bluetooth hardware and antenna placement. A weak Bluetooth adapter or poorly positioned antenna can produce an unstable connection even when the headphones themselves are working normally.

    6. Connection-quality settings

    The WH-1000XM5 supports several Bluetooth codecs and connection features, including LDAC. Higher-quality Bluetooth transmission can sometimes be less forgiving when the wireless environment is already unstable. For troubleshooting, connection stability should take priority over maximum theoretical audio quality.

    The Fixes

    Fix 1: Move Your Device Closer to the Headphones

    Start with the simplest possible test. Place your phone or computer close to the WH-1000XM5. Ideally, during the test keep the devices close together, remove unnecessary obstacles, avoid placing your phone behind your body, keep other Bluetooth devices away temporarily, and avoid testing in an extremely crowded wireless environment. Then play the same music or video for several minutes.

    If the cutting-out disappears when the device is very close to the headphones, that is useful evidence. It suggests that distance, signal strength, interference, or the Bluetooth hardware may be contributing to the problem.

    Fix 2: Try "Priority on Stable Connection"

    If your main goal is uninterrupted audio, connection stability is more important than pushing Bluetooth quality to its limits. Sony provides a Priority on Stable Connection option for situations where Bluetooth playback is unstable. The idea is simple: Maximum quality isn't always the same thing as maximum stability. If your headphones are constantly cutting out, try the stable-connection setting and see whether the behavior changes.

    Fix 3: Disable Multipoint Temporarily

    If your WH-1000XM5 is connected to both your phone and computer, temporarily disconnect one of them. For example, disconnect the headphones from your phone, keep only your computer connected, play music continuously, and monitor the headphones for several minutes. If the problem disappears, reconnect the second device and test Multipoint again. Multipoint itself isn't a bad feature, but when troubleshooting, reducing the number of active connections makes it easier to identify the source of the problem.

    Fix 4: Restart Your Phone or Computer

    This sounds basic, but don't skip it. Bluetooth depends on the operating system, drivers, background services, and applications. A temporary software problem can sometimes affect Bluetooth audio without indicating a hardware failure. Sony recommends restarting the connected device as part of Bluetooth troubleshooting.

    Fix 5: Close Unnecessary Applications

    If you're using the headphones with a phone or computer, temporarily close applications that you don't need. This is particularly useful if the cutting-out problem appears while your device is under heavy load. For example, on a computer you may have a browser with many tabs, a game, Discord, or cloud synchronization running. The purpose is simply to test whether the problem changes when the system has fewer things running.

    Fix 6: Test the WH-1000XM5 With Another Device

    This is one of the most useful diagnostic tests. Try another phone, tablet, or computer. Just connect the headphones to another device and reproduce the same type of audio. If the problem disappears, the headphones may be working normally, while the original device could be responsible (drivers, adapter, OS settings, etc.). If the problem continues on multiple completely different devices, the headphones themselves become more suspicious.

    Sony WH-1000XM5 Sound Cutting Out on Windows

    Windows deserves special attention. You may find that your WH-1000XM5 works perfectly with your phone but constantly stutters on Windows. If that's happening, don't immediately assume your headphones are defective. Check your Bluetooth Driver, your Bluetooth Adapter (especially on Desktop PCs), and your Bluetooth Antenna. Some Wi-Fi/Bluetooth motherboards use external antennas; if they aren't connected properly or are positioned poorly, Bluetooth performance can suffer.

    Could LDAC or Wi-Fi Be Causing the Problem?

    LDAC: LDAC is designed to provide higher-quality Bluetooth audio transmission. However, if your Bluetooth connection is already unstable, increasing the amount of data being transmitted isn't necessarily going to improve reliability. If disabling LDAC or switching to a more stable connection mode eliminates the cutting out, that's useful evidence.

    Wi-Fi Interference: Wireless interference is one of the easiest factors to overlook. Imagine you're using your WH-1000XM5 next to a busy Wi-Fi router, around several Bluetooth devices, or in an apartment with many nearby Wi-Fi networks. The wireless environment may simply be crowded. Try moving to another room or location. If the problem suddenly disappears, that's a valuable clue.

    When Should You Suspect a Hardware Problem?

    Don't assume a hardware failure simply because the audio cuts out once. Instead, look for a consistent pattern. If Phone A, Phone B, and Laptop all cut out, in different locations, with Multipoint disabled, Stable Connection enabled, and Firmware updated—and it still cuts out—the possibility of a problem with the headphones becomes much more significant. If the headphones are still under warranty, contacting Sony or the retailer is preferable to attempting an unofficial repair.

    The Fastest Troubleshooting Checklist

    1. Move closer: Put the playback device close to the headphones.
    2. Use one device: Disconnect the second Bluetooth device.
    3. Disable Multipoint: Test the headphones with only one connected device.
    4. Try Stable Connection: Prioritize connection stability over maximum Bluetooth quality.
    5. Restart the device: Restart your phone or computer.
    6. Close unnecessary apps: Especially on a computer under heavy load.
    7. Test another device: This is one of the most important steps.
    8. Check firmware: Use Sony | Sound Connect.
    9. If you're using Windows: Check Bluetooth drivers, adapter quality, and antenna placement.
    10. Consider a reset: Only after the simpler tests.
    11. Test again: Don't assume the problem is solved after one successful connection.
    12. Contact Sony if necessary: If the problem continues across multiple devices, professional support becomes the appropriate next step.
