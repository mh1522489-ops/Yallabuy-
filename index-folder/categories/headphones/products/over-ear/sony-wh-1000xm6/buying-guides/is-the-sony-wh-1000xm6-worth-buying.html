Quick verdict

The Sony WH-1000XM6 is worth buying for most people who want one pair of over-ear headphones for travel, commuting, open offices and calls, as long as they pay somewhere near the lower end of its recent price range and accept one real risk: owner reports of headband and hinge area breakage.

Independent testers agree on the main strengths. RTINGS calls the noise isolation , SoundGuys rates the ANC as , and What Hi-Fi? says call quality is The weaknesses are also consistent. Battery life is good but not the best in the class (SoundGuys), comfort splits reviewers, and owner reports describe a plastic headband ring that has cracked on some pairs.

Best for

  • Frequent flyers and commuters
  • Open-office workers wanting quiet focus time
  • People who take a lot of calls
  • Anyone who wants app control and multipoint

Not ideal for

  • People who wear headphones for many hours and need deep, plush earcups
  • Buyers who prioritize battery life above all else
  • iPhone owners who want hi-res wireless audio (no LDAC)
  • Anyone who tends to be rough on gear
Current value: good at roughly $380 to $400, harder to justify at the $460 list price. More on that in the value section below.

What changed from the WH-1000XM5

Sony fixed things that XM5 owners complained about, and it also raised the price.

The folding design came back. The XM5 only folded flat, while the XM6 can fold up small, which illustrated with a side-by-side photo. For anyone who packs headphones in a bag, that matters more than most spec changes.

Sony also redesigned the hinge with a metal component embedded in the plastic housing, which in May 2025. That SoundGuys article was about the XM5's reputation. In its poll of more than 2,200 readers, 25% said their XM5 hinge had broken. The poll is self-selected, so it shows how badly the problem was perceived and not how many units failed. I come back to whether the redesign worked in the durability section.

Sony says the new HD Noise Canceling Processor QN3 is seven times faster than the one it replaces and that 12 microphones feed the noise cancellation "Seven times faster" describes processing speed. It does not mean seven times more noise reduction, and no tester I found measured it that way.

RTINGS retested frequency response and noise isolation after firmware update 2.0.2 based on community feedback, which is a reminder that firmware can shift results a little.

Noise cancellation

This is the main reason to buy the XM6, and the evidence supports it.

What testers found. RTINGS rates the headphones as outstanding for noise isolation and says they are impressive for travel and great for office work. SoundGuys, which tested the headphones for five days on a unit supplied by Sony, calls the ANC Its product page scores noise isolation and ANC at 7.6 out of 10, which it says puts the headphones in the top 10% of the 86 over-ears it has measured with that method What Hi-Fi? calls the ANC excellent.

What Sony claims. Sony says an Adaptive NC Optimizer adjusts cancellation to surrounding noise, air pressure and how the headphones are worn. That is a description of a feature, and none of the testers I found isolated its effect.

What owners say. Owners on retailer pages describe good results against traffic, chatter and low-frequency hum. One Adorama reviewer who wears glasses said the cancellation still worked, and another said they chose the Sony over a Bose model for sound while believing the Bose was somewhat better at cancellation. Those are individual opinions.

YallaBuy analysis. Three independent outlets rate the ANC at or near the top of the category, and that is enough to call it a clear strength. Whether it beats the Bose QuietComfort Ultra (2nd Gen) is less settled. The head-to-head sources I found for that question were either single owners or low-authority aggregator sites, which I excluded. Treat any claim that one brand has a decisive ANC lead with caution, and weigh fit and comfort at least as heavily as small differences in measured isolation.

Seal quality matters for every ANC headphone. A tester who gets a good seal can hear a different result from the next person. RTINGS lists frequency response consistency as only passable, which fits that picture, since it reflects how much sound changes with fit and positioning. If you wear glasses, a heavy beard or thick hair, buy from a seller with a straightforward return policy.

Sound quality

Reviewers like the sound, with some differences in emphasis.

What Hi-Fi? says the sound can match anything at this level and describes it as spacious and musical. SoundGuys says the headphones sound very good, and its product page gives the audio quality a 4.8 out of 5 on its SoundMDAQS scale, again in the top 10% of the 44 over-ears it has scored that way RTINGS rates reproduction accuracy as good. Tom's Guide says the XM6 sounds different from earlier Sony headphones, and Sony says it tuned them with input from mastering engineers.

The sources agree the sound is good. They say less about its character, and none of them calls the XM6 a neutral studio headphone, so I would not assume it is one. If you want a flatter or warmer sound, the 10-band EQ in the Sony Sound Connect app gives you room to adjust, according to .

There is one platform limit. LDAC, Sony's high-bitrate Bluetooth codec, is listed in the specifications, but Expert Reviews lists "no hi-res audio support for iPhone owners" as a drawback. iPhones use AAC over Bluetooth, so they will not get LDAC. Sony's headphones also do not play audio over USB-C, according to launch coverage by iPhone owners who care about hi-res audio would need the 3.5 mm cable.

Gaming is another boundary. RTINGS says the headphones are very good for wired gaming and not widely usable for wireless gaming. If a headset for consoles or PC is part of your decision, a dedicated gaming headset serves you better.

Comfort and fit

Comfort is where reviewers disagree, and it is also where your own head shape matters most.

The positive side: Tom's Guide gave its verdict as "incredible comfort" after three weeks with the headphones. What Hi-Fi? calls comfort levels great and says the redesigned headband did not make its presence felt during testing. Expert Reviews says wearing glasses did not unduly affect comfort or noise cancellation for its reviewer. Sony lists a weight of about 254 g.

The cautious side: in its early impressions, SoundGuys described the earcups as stubbornly shallow with thin padding, calling it the same comfort complaint that affected the XM5. That article predates a long testing period, so it is a first reaction and not a verdict. SoundGuys' later full review does not appear in the portion I could read, so I can't say whether its view changed.

YallaBuy analysis. The disagreement is plausible rather than contradictory. Shallow earcups matter more if your ears sit close to the driver housing, and they matter more over a long flight than over a 30-minute commute. Headphones at 254 g are not heavy, so weight is unlikely to be the issue, but pad depth could be. If you have larger ears or you wear headphones for several hours at a time, try them in person before committing, or buy where returns are easy. Some roundups say the Bose QuietComfort Ultra (2nd Gen) fits more comfortably, but the ones I found were low-authority sites, so I am not relying on that claim. Trying both on is the only dependable way to settle it.

Battery life

Sony rates the headphones for up to 30 hours with ANC on and up to 40 hours with ANC off. Those are manufacturer figures.

On the independent side, SoundGuys tests with music peaking at 75 dB SPL and ANC on, and it describes the XM6's battery life as , noting that JBL and Sennheiser models last much longer. Its product page lists 30 hours of wireless playback and labels that below average for the category. Tom's Guide says battery life is on par with the closest competition and consistent with what Sony promises. RTINGS lists considerable battery life among the pros.

A word on conflicting numbers. I saw two specific measured figures (31.75 hours and 37 hours 14 minutes) on a low-authority aggregator site that attributed them to RTINGS and SoundGuys. I could not confirm them on the original pages, so I am not using them. The practical takeaway is the same either way: real-world results land near Sony's rating, and different test protocols give different numbers.

Hypothetical example, not a test result. If you listen for two hours a day with ANC on, a 30-hour rating works out to about two weeks between charges. A long-haul flyer using ANC for 12 hours at a stretch would use roughly 40% of a full charge per leg. Real use varies with volume, calls and codec choice.

YallaBuy analysis. Thirty hours covers any single flight and most workweeks. It becomes a weakness only against competitors with far larger batteries. Battery capacity also affects long-term wear, since fewer charge cycles help, which SoundGuys points out when comparing the Sony to longer-lasting JBL and Sennheiser models. If you routinely forget to charge, or if you travel for days without outlets, a longer-lasting model is a better fit.

Calls and microphones

Sony says the headphones use six microphones with AI-based beamforming for calls. What Hi-Fi? says call quality is class-leading. RTINGS rates the microphone system as very good.

Not every reviewer agreed completely. Expert Reviews lists call quality that struggles in loud environments as a con. These results are compatible: a microphone can be strong in an office or a quiet street and still degrade next to a train or in heavy wind, and testers do not use identical conditions. If you take many calls in genuinely noisy places, such as on a loud platform, expect good but not flawless results.

Features and software

The feature list is mature. Multipoint lets the headphones stay connected to two devices at once, which suits people who move between a laptop and a phone. The wired option works with the unit turned off, according to Sony's specifications. An ambient mode lets outside sound in, and What Hi-Fi? says you can turn off the touch panel in the app if you keep brushing it by accident. DSEE Extreme, which Sony says upscales compressed audio, returns from the XM5.

The Sound Connect app handles EQ, ANC and ambient settings, and SoundGuys notes that most of the software and firmware features you would want in a top-tier ANC headphone are present. SoundGuys also reported that Sony replaced a mic cover on the ANC assembly with a grille, fixing an issue it had flagged earlier. It adds that the ANC microphone protrudes about 2 mm from the housing, which is a cosmetic detail.

Durability: what owners are reporting

This section is the one to read before you buy, because the evidence is mixed and the stakes are real.

The background. The XM5 had a hinge problem. SoundGuys' reader poll suggested a high rate of failures, and the headphones are the subject of a federal class action, Kanter v. Sony Corporation of America, that alleges the XM5's hinge mounting points fracture under normal use. Those are allegations in a lawsuit, not findings.

What the XM6 changed. Sony added a metal hinge component and brought back a folding mechanism, and SoundGuys' first impression was that the reinforcement .

What owners have reported since. Reports appeared within months. covered the first reported broken hinge in July 2025 and said it was unclear whether the problem was widespread. By mid-2026 the pattern had shifted. reported that The Walkman Blog had found at least 13 Reddit reports of the plastic ring above the hinge breaking after prolonged use, which leaves the adjustable headband unable to stay together. Notebookcheck's own text says this is not nearly as widespread as the XM5 hinge failures and that it is too early to estimate how many pairs will be affected. Android Authority described the same ring failure pattern in August 2026, and SoundGuys wrote that complaints about the headband 'appear to be growing. A SoundGuys piece on the XM6 and the XM5's flaw adds that some posts show failures at the slider or hinge, that headphones extended to maximum length put more stress on the mechanism, and that Reddit users have begun recommending third-party protection plans over Sony's warranty.

On warranty. Owners report that Sony has declined to repair some of these breaks for free, and Notebookcheck repeats that account. I did not find a statement from Sony on its claims policy for this issue, so I can't say how consistent the denials are.

How to read this. The evidence supports a few careful statements. Owners have reported breakage of the plastic ring and related parts, and several reputable outlets have documented the reports. The reports have grown over time. Nobody has established a failure rate, and Reddit reports skew toward people who had a problem. That means the XM6 hinge redesign appears to have helped, since reports are focused on the surrounding plastic, but the headphones are not free of structural complaints. It is also unknown whether this is a defect in certain units or a flaw that will eventually affect a larger share.

YallaBuy analysis. I would treat durability as a real, unquantified risk. The practical response is to reduce your exposure. Buy from a retailer with a generous return window, consider an extended protection plan from the seller, keep the headband closer to a mid-range setting when you can, and store the headphones in the supplied case. If you want a pair you can be rough with, none of the mainstream flagship options is clearly safer on current evidence, and I would not pay a premium price for these headphones if you expect a multi-year lifespan without protection.

Other limitations

Several smaller points are worth knowing.

The price rose relative to what some buyers expected. Bose and Sony both sit in the same price band, so there is no meaningful price advantage in the flagship tier.

Sony does not support aptX HD, according to launch coverage cited by ChannelNews, which matters mostly to Android users with phones that favor that codec. LDAC is available instead.

The headphones are not designed for sports. RTINGS describes them as decent for sports and fitness but not built for it. There is no IP rating listed in the specifications I found.

Finally, the headphones are over a year old. Tom's Guide expects Sony to release a new model in 2027 or 2028, though that is the reviewer's expectation and not an announcement. I found no confirmed successor.

Alternatives and who should choose them

Alternatives only help if they solve a problem the XM6 does not.

Sony WH-1000XM5

The previous generation is cheaper on sale. Digital Citizen reported it at $199.99 on Sony's own store as of early October 2026, compared with $379.99 for the XM6. It lacks folding, and its hinge is the subject of the class action and the SoundGuys reader poll. Makes sense only if the price gap is large and you will use a protection plan.

Bose QC Ultra (2nd Gen)

A Best Buy listing showed it at $449 alongside the Sony. It is the closest direct rival on price and category, but I could not confirm head-to-head ANC or comfort results from reliable independent tests. Try it alongside the Sony if you can, especially if earcup depth worries you.

Sennheiser / JBL models

SoundGuys says these models offer much better battery life than the XM6. Choose them if charging less often matters more to you than top-tier noise cancellation.

Apple AirPods Max 2

T3 describes Apple's updated model, with an H2 chip and Apple's claim of up to 1.5 times more effective noise cancellation. That figure is a comparison with Apple's own earlier model and not an independent result. Makes sense mainly for people deep in the Apple ecosystem, and T3 notes the price is high.

Who should buy the XM6

  • You fly or commute often and want some of the strongest independently tested noise cancellation available.
  • You work in an open office and want quieter focus time, plus good call quality.
  • You switch between a laptop and a phone and want multipoint.
  • You want folding headphones that pack small.
  • You can buy near $380 to $400, or you are covered by a generous return policy.

Who should skip it

  • You wear headphones for many hours at a stretch and have found shallow earcups uncomfortable in the past.
  • Battery life is your first priority, since Sennheiser and JBL models last much longer according to SoundGuys.
  • You use an iPhone and want hi-res wireless audio, because LDAC is not available there.
  • You want a headset for wireless gaming. RTINGS says it is not widely usable for that.
  • You are hard on your gear and cannot buy protection, given the reported breakage.

Should XM5 owners upgrade?

For most XM5 owners, probably not. Sony improved noise cancellation, brought back folding, and Tom's Guide and What Hi-Fi? describe the XM6 as a refinement of a strong formula. Those are real gains, but the XM5 remains a capable headphone, and an upgrade at $380 or more is a lot to pay for refinement. It makes more sense if your XM5 has a failing hinge, you travel often and want the folding case, or you can sell the old pair to offset the cost. Given the reported headband ring issue on the XM6, I would not upgrade expecting a durability fix.

Value for money

Product quality and current value are separate questions. The XM6 is a very good headphone. Whether it is a good purchase depends on price and risk.

At about $380 to $400, the headphones cost a modest premium over the previous generation's discounted price, and you get what several independent testers rate as top-tier ANC, strong calls, and a mature app. That is a defensible price for the frequent traveler or office worker.

At the $460 list price, the case is weaker. The headphones are over a year old, prices have fallen repeatedly, and a durability question remains. A rational buyer waits for a sale or picks a competitor.

Open-box and used pairs at $229 to $320 are a real option if you accept the warranty and return terms. A used or open-box pair also carries the same durability question, with less protection.

Final verdict

The Sony WH-1000XM6 is one of the strongest all-round noise-canceling headphones you can buy in 2026, according to multiple independent testers. It earns that on noise cancellation, calls, features and sound. It falls short on battery life relative to some rivals, comfort for some head shapes, and a durability record that is still unfolding.

Buy it if you travel or work in noisy spaces, you can get it near $380 to $400, and you will buy with return protection. Skip it, or look at a Bose model, if long-session comfort or battery life drives your decision. Wait if the price is near $460 and you are not in a hurry.
