Quick Verdict

If you already own a working Sony WH-1000XM4, you probably do not need to upgrade to the WH-1000XM5.

The XM5 offers real improvements, especially in microphone quality, call performance, and some areas of noise cancellation. Independent testing also suggests that its real-world battery performance can be better than the XM4 under certain testing conditions.

However, the XM4 remains a very capable ANC headphone. It also has an important advantage that the XM5 removed: the XM4 folds into a much smaller travel case.

There is also a recurring owner-reported concern involving the XM5's headband hinge. This should not be interpreted as an official failure rate — Sony has not published one — but the issue appears repeatedly across independent owner discussions and retailer reviews.

And in 2026, there is another factor: Sony has already released the WH-1000XM6, which introduces a redesigned folding mechanism and further improves noise cancellation.

  • You do not care about having a compact folding case.

What Actually Changed?

1. Noise Cancellation

The XM5 makes a genuine improvement in some areas of ANC. Sony increased the number of microphones from four to eight and added the V1 processor alongside the QN1.

Independent testing from SoundGuys found the XM5 handled certain types of wind noise and low-frequency rumble more effectively than the XM4. Other reviewers have also reported stronger attenuation in some high-mid and treble frequencies. Independent Testing

However, this does not mean the XM4 suddenly has poor ANC. The XM4 was already one of the strongest noise-cancelling headphones when it launched, and it remains competitive today.

The XM5 has better ANC in some conditions, but the difference is not large enough by itself to make every XM4 owner upgrade.

2. Sound Quality

Sony changed the driver from a 40mm unit in the XM4 to a smaller 30mm carbon-fiber composite driver in the XM5. A smaller driver does not automatically mean worse sound.

What Hi-Fi?'s testing found the XM5 delivered a clearer and more open presentation compared with the XM4. The publication attributed some of this performance to the rigidity of the new carbon-fiber diaphragm. Independent Testing

This is a good example of why specifications should not be judged in isolation.

The XM5 can sound better to some listeners, but the improvement is not necessarily large enough to justify an upgrade for every XM4 owner.

3. Battery Life

This is one area where official specifications and independent testing tell different stories.

Sony's rated battery life: Both headphones are rated for 30 hours with ANC enabled. The XM4 is rated for up to 38 hours with ANC off, and the XM5 up to 40 hours with ANC off. According to Sony's specifications, ANC battery life is identical. Manufacturer Specs

Independent testing: SoundGuys measured approximately 19 hours with ANC for the XM4 and ~31 hours 53 minutes with ANC for the XM5, under the publication's own controlled testing conditions. Independent Testing

This does not mean every XM5 owner will get exactly 31 hours and 53 minutes. Battery life depends on volume, codec, ANC usage, temperature, and other factors.

The official specifications suggest similar ANC battery life, while independent testing indicates that the XM5 can have a meaningful real-world advantage under certain conditions.

4. Design and Portability

This is one area where the XM4 is arguably better. The XM4 folds into a compact shape, making it easier to put into a backpack or carry-on bag.

The XM5 changed the hinge and no longer folds into the same compact configuration. The ear cups can rotate, but the headphones remain substantially larger when stored.

For frequent travelers, this can be more important than a small improvement in ANC.

If portability matters to you, the XM4 has a clear advantage.

5. Microphone and Call Quality

This is one of the strongest reasons to choose the XM5. Independent reviews and recurring owner feedback generally rate the XM5's microphone and call performance higher than the XM4. Owner Feedback

Sony introduced additional microphones, beamforming, and AI-based noise reduction to improve voice pickup in noisy environments.

If you regularly use your headphones for Zoom meetings, Google Meet, phone calls, remote work, or voice calls while traveling — the XM5's microphone improvement can be meaningful.

Heavy call users have a much stronger reason to upgrade than people who mainly listen to music.

6. Build Quality and Hinge Reliability

This is the area where the XM5 deserves additional caution.

Across owner discussions, including Slickdeals threads, retailer reviews, and other online communities, there are recurring reports of the XM5's headband hinge cracking or separating from the ear cup. Owner Feedback

Some owners report the problem after extended use, including reports around the 12–24 month period.

However, it is important to distinguish recurring reports from a confirmed failure rate. Sony has not published a statistic showing what percentage of XM5 units experience hinge failure. Therefore, YallaBuy does not describe this as a proven failure rate or claim that every XM5 has a defective hinge.

The XM5 has a recurring owner-reported hinge durability concern that prospective buyers should be aware of.

This concern is particularly relevant because the XM5 introduced a different hinge design from the folding mechanism used on the XM4. Sony's later WH-1000XM6 returned to a folding design, which makes the XM5's hinge situation even more relevant when comparing generations.

The reports do not prove that most XM5 headphones will fail, but they are frequent enough across independent owner discussions to deserve consideration before buying.

What About the XM6?

This changes the buying decision in 2026.

Newer Generation Available

Sony released the WH-1000XM6 in May 2025 as the successor to the XM5. According to coverage from Notebookcheck and 9to5Toys, the XM6 uses a newer QN3 processor and a 12-microphone system. It also returns to a folding hinge design. Press Coverage

This matters because the XM6 addresses two of the biggest concerns surrounding the XM5: the non-folding design and the recurring owner-reported hinge concern. The XM6 also improves noise cancellation compared with previous generations.

That does not automatically make the XM6 the best choice for everyone. Price remains important. But if the XM5 is selling close to the XM6's price, the XM5 becomes much harder to recommend.

Before buying the XM5 in 2026, compare its current price with the XM6. See our full WH-1000XM5 vs XM6 comparison.

Value for Money

The WH-1000XM4 has become much more attractive as its price has fallen from its original $349.99 launch price. If you can find a new XM4 at a substantial discount, it can offer excellent value for someone who does not need the latest microphone or ANC improvements.

The WH-1000XM5 sits in a more complicated position. It is newer than the XM4 and offers genuine improvements, but it is no longer Sony's current flagship because the XM6 has replaced it. That means the XM5 makes the most sense when its price is significantly below the XM6.

Small price difference between XM5 and XM6 → consider the XM6.
Large XM5 discount → the XM5 becomes more interesting.
Very cheap XM4 → the XM4 remains excellent value.

Prices change frequently, so check the current price before making the final decision.

Should You Upgrade From XM4 to XM5?

For most people who already own a working XM4: No — not necessarily.

The XM5 is better in several measurable or observable areas, particularly microphone performance and some aspects of ANC.

But the XM4 already provides strong ANC, good sound quality, long battery life, comfortable fit, multipoint connectivity, and a compact folding design.

The improvement from XM4 to XM5 is therefore more of an incremental upgrade than a complete transformation.

If your XM4 works perfectly, keeping it and saving your money can be the better decision.

If your XM4 is damaged, its battery has deteriorated, or you strongly value call quality, the XM5 becomes more attractive.

And if you're prepared to spend considerably more, compare the XM5 against the newer WH-1000XM6 before buying.
