Samsung Galaxy Buds4 Pro Review: Are They Worth It in 2026?

An independent, data-driven analysis of Samsung's fourth-generation flagship earbuds — based on lab tests, not marketing.

March 15, 2026 12 min read Updated Aug 2026
Transparency note: YallaBuy has not personally tested this product. This article is based on official Samsung specifications, independent testing from specialized tech publications (SoundGuys, GSMArena, TechRadar, RTINGS, TechGearLab), and real user feedback from public forums and communities. Every claim below is attributed to its source.
Quick Verdict

The Galaxy Buds4 Pro (model number SM-R640) is Samsung's fourth-generation flagship earbuds, announced on February 25, 2026 at the Galaxy Unpacked event alongside the Galaxy S26 series, and released on March 11, 2026, according to Wikipedia.

According to SoundGuys' independent review (based on actual lab testing), the earbuds received a rating of 8 out of 10, with strong performance in durability, portability, and comfort, but relatively weaker results in battery life and passive isolation.

Best for: Samsung Galaxy phone owners (especially S23 or newer) who want strong sound quality and deep ecosystem integration. Not ideal for: iPhone users, or anyone prioritizing the best possible battery life or noise cancellation in this price range. Main strengths: Strong sound quality, effective noise cancellation, sleek design, Auracast support. Main weakness: The ear tip locking system is difficult to remove and reattach — a recurring issue carried over from the previous generation, according to TechRadar.

Current value: Good for Galaxy phone owners; less compelling for everyone else due to ecosystem lock-in.

What Makes This Product Different?

According to Samsung's official product page, the Buds4 Pro's design is built on "global ear data" to improve fit and comfort, with a "real metal blade" made from aluminum instead of the more polarizing plastic design used on the Buds3 Pro, per GSMArena, which described the new design as "much better suited to the average Joe" compared to the previous generation.

The most concrete, measurable difference noted by independent testing is an improvement in active noise cancellation of about 5–10dB in the 500Hz to 1.1kHz frequency range compared to the Buds3 Pro, according to SoundGuys' lab test — a range where many everyday annoying sounds tend to fall.

Main Features

Feature
Adaptive Noise Cancellation (ANC 2.0)
What is it?
An active noise cancellation system that Samsung's official Amazon listing describes as adapting "in real time" to the surrounding environment.
How does it work?
According to SoundGuys' testing, it relies on a set of microphones to pick up outside noise and generate an inverse sound wave to cancel it out.
Why does it matter?
It's the most important factor for anyone using earbuds on public transit or in noisy environments.
Limitations: Per the same test, performance dips slightly in the 500Hz–1.1kHz range, which can let some annoying sounds through if the fit isn't secure.
Feature
Connectivity and Samsung Seamless Codec (SSC)
What is it?
Samsung's proprietary audio codec supporting up to 24-bit / 96kHz audio, according to the official product page.
How does it work?
It requires a Samsung phone running One UI 6.1.1 or newer (S23 and up), per SoundGuys — otherwise the earbuds default to standard SBC and AAC codecs only.
Why does it matter?
Because much of the earbuds' added audio value is specifically tied to owning a recent Samsung phone.
Limitations: There's no aptX or LDAC support, and there's no such thing as truly lossless audio over Bluetooth yet, per SoundGuys' FAQ section.
Feature
Microphones and Call Quality
What is it?
A system combining 6 microphones with a bone-conduction voice pickup unit, according to Samsung's official page and the independent outlet Recording Now.
How does it work?
It uses a deep neural network (DNN) to isolate the user's voice from background noise during calls, per Samsung.
Why does it matter?
Because call quality is often a weak point for wireless earbuds in general.
Limitations: In a listener poll conducted by SoundGuys on sample call recordings (roughly 1,956 votes), 44% rated the quality "good" and 25% rated it "excellent," while about 6% rated it poor or awful. SoundGuys also noted that performance degrades in windy conditions if the earbud isn't seated securely in the ear, since that disrupts the bone-conduction pickup.
Feature
Design and Comfort
What is it?
A "lollipop" stem-and-bud shape with flat metal-look edges and swappable silicone ear tips.
How does it work?
The earbuds ship with three additional tip sizes, per SoundGuys.
Why does it matter?
Long-term comfort is a decisive factor for most daily users.
Limitations: Both TechRadar and SoundGuys described the ear tip locking mechanism as difficult to remove and reattach, with TechRadar specifically warning of the "risk of breaking them" when swapping sizes — the same issue that reportedly delayed the launch of the Buds3 Pro, according to PhoneArena.

Performance (According to Independent Testing)

Note: This section is based entirely on independent test results, not on YallaBuy's own hands-on experience.

Battery life

SoundGuys ran a standardized battery test and measured 6 hours and 1 minute of continuous playback per earbud (61mAh cell per bud), with a 530mAh charging case adding roughly 3–4 extra charge cycles.

Separately, the independent outlet Recording Now reported somewhat different figures: 6 hours with ANC on (26 hours total with the case), or 7 hours with ANC off (36 hours total with the case). This discrepancy between the two sources could stem from differences in testing methodology (volume level, ANC on/off, or test conditions), so it's reasonable to treat roughly 6 hours per earbud as the figure both sources broadly agree on, while noting that the total figure with the case varies depending on how it was measured. Both SoundGuys and Recording Now agreed that battery life is "below average" or "average" compared to competitors like the AirPods Pro 3 and Sony WF-1000XM6, which are officially rated at 8 hours per earbud, per Recording Now.

Noise cancellation

Per SoundGuys' lab test, attenuation at the maximum setting averages around 84% across the audible spectrum, with weaker performance specifically in the 500Hz to 1.1kHz range.

Sound quality

The specialized outlet TechGearLab (which conducted lab testing of frequency response and soundstage) described the sound as "one of the best options for Android-specific" earbuds, with bass that is "powerful and warm" but can "get boomy on bass-heavy tracks," occasionally reducing vocal clarity in those specific cases.

Real-World Experience

According to a week-long hands-on field test by TechRadar, the earbuds made a good initial impression on sound and design, but the reviewer flagged "one huge problem" that remains: the difficulty of swapping ear tips, along with a sense of insecure fit even after trying the largest available tip size.

On the user feedback side, in discussions on the r/galaxybuds subreddit (compiled via the aggregator RedditRecs), one user noted that ambient sound felt "extremely muddy," as if their ears were "plugged," and also noted that the build quality felt "a little more plastic or cheap" compared to the Buds3 Pro despite being lighter. These are individual user observations, not formal test results, but they do echo TechRadar's note about fit stability in the ear.

Problems & Limitations

Combining recurring observations across multiple independent sources, the main weak points are:

Ear tip locking system: This issue appears in both TechRadar's full review and its separate hands-on field test, and SoundGuys also listed it under "what we don't like." Because it's documented by more than one independent source, it qualifies as a genuine recurring problem rather than an isolated complaint.
Modest battery life: Documented by two independent sources (SoundGuys and Recording Now), with a slight discrepancy in the exact numbers between them.
Ecosystem lock-in: Documented by both SoundGuys and GSMArena; features like SSC, Live Translation, and full Multipoint require a recent Samsung phone.
In-ear fit stability: Noted by TechRadar's field test, echoed by a single individual Reddit user comment — the evidence base here is thinner, so this is described more cautiously rather than as a confirmed widespread issue.

By contrast, there are currently no widespread reports (based on available sources at the time of writing) of manufacturing defects similar to what the Buds3 Pro faced at launch in 2024, which per PhoneArena included uneven gaps and deformation of the plastic shell — that was specifically a previous-generation issue.

Who Should Buy It?

Who Should Buy
  • Owners of a recent Samsung Galaxy phone (S23 or newer) who want to get the most out of exclusive features.
  • Anyone who wants strong sound quality with warm bass and doesn't mind some emphasis on bass-heavy tracks.
  • Anyone who needs high water and dust resistance (IP57) for workouts or outdoor use.
  • Anyone who values Auracast for shared listening in public spaces.
Who Should Not Buy
  • iPhone users: you'll lose most of the exclusive features, and there are alternatives built specifically for Apple's ecosystem.
  • Anyone prioritizing the longest possible battery life in this price bracket.
  • Anyone who frequently swaps or removes ear tips (for cleaning or sharing use), given the reported difficulty of doing so.
  • Anyone with a non-Samsung Android phone, since most of the advanced features will be unavailable.

Value for Money

At an MSRP of $249.99, the Galaxy Buds4 Pro sits in the same price bracket as top competitors like the AirPods Pro 3 and JBL Tour Pro 3. Per GSMArena's analysis, the buying value "depends heavily" on whether the user owns a recent Galaxy phone; without one, the competitive edge weakens due to codec limitations and the locking of features like true Multipoint and 360 Audio.

GSMArena also pointed out that the listed price is the official MSRP, not necessarily the market price, since deals and discounts appear fairly regularly — a point echoed by PhoneArena's reporting on discounts of up to 25% on Amazon during certain periods. As a result, the real-world value proposition can improve significantly for buyers willing to wait for a deal rather than purchasing at full price.

Final Verdict

The Galaxy Buds4 Pro are strong earbuds, but not flawless ones. Sound quality and active noise cancellation put them among the better options currently available, according to independent testing from SoundGuys and TechGearLab, and the redesign represents a tangible improvement in look and perceived build quality over the previous generation, per GSMArena.

That said, they're not the best fit for everyone. Battery life is modest compared to key competitors, the ear tip locking system remains a documented weak point across multiple independent sources, and much of the real value is specifically tied to owning a recent Samsung Galaxy phone.

Bottom line: If you own a recent Galaxy phone and want a flagship earbud tightly integrated with your ecosystem, this is a reasonable and strong choice — especially if bought at a discount from the official price. If you use an iPhone or a non-Samsung Android phone, alternatives like the AirPods Pro 3 or JBL Tour Pro 3 are better suited to your needs.
