AirPods Pro 2 vs AirPods Pro 3: The Core Differences

According to Apple's official announcement, AirPods Pro 3 launched at a $249 starting price — the same MSRP as its predecessor — but changes quite a bit under the hood. In practice, street prices for the Pro 3 have been spotted as low as $189 during sales events, making the real-world price gap between the two generations narrower than the list prices suggest. RTINGS' side-by-side testing found that the Pro 3 delivers stronger noise cancellation and longer continuous battery life than the Pro 2.

Note the battery trade-off: SoundGuys' testing clocked the Pro 3's earbuds at roughly 8 hours and 42 minutes on a single charge, a clear improvement over the Pro 2. But as GSMArena pointed out, the Pro 3's charging case has a smaller battery than the Pro 2's case, so total listening time with the case actually drops from 30 hours to 24 hours. If all-day battery life without a case matters more to you than raw case capacity, that distinction is worth knowing before you decide.

Active Noise Cancellation Upgrade: Is It Really Twice as Good?

This is the headline feature, and it's also the one generating the most search interest around "AirPods Pro 3 sound quality" and the ANC upgrade specifically. Apple's own marketing claims the AirPods Pro 3 delivers the world's best in-ear Active Noise Cancellation, roughly twice as effective as the Pro 2 and four times better than the original AirPods Pro.

RecordingNow's hands-on testing, based on the reviewer's own purchase of both models, reported that the ANC improvement held up in real-world use, alongside a noticeably better microphone for isolating voice against background noise and wind. SoundGuys similarly found the Pro 3 to be the stronger performer for ANC and calls, while noting that day-to-day Bluetooth connectivity feels nearly identical between the two generations since both use the same Bluetooth 5.3 standard with SBC and AAC codec support.

YallaBuy analysis: If you regularly commute on trains, planes, or in loud city environments, this is the single biggest reason to consider upgrading. If your listening happens mostly in quiet spaces, the Pro 2's ANC — which is already well-regarded — may be more than sufficient.

AirPods Pro H2 vs H3 Chip: Clearing Up a Common Misconception

A lot of people search for "AirPods Pro H2 vs H3 chip" assuming Apple moved to a new audio chip with the Pro 3. That's not accurate. According to Apple's own tech specifications and reporting from The Apple Wiki, the AirPods Pro 3 uses the same Apple H2 chip found in the Pro 2. The main silicon change is in the case, not the earbuds: the Pro 3's charging case adds Apple's U2 chip, which improves precision finding through the Find My app but doesn't affect audio processing. There is currently no H3 chip in any AirPods model.

Sound Quality, Spatial Audio, and Lossless Audio

Both models support Personalized Spatial Audio with dynamic head tracking. Macworld's review noted that early reactions to the AirPods Pro 3's sound signature have been mixed among adopters, with the reviewer describing the Pro 3 as punchier across genres compared to the Pro 2. A separate hands-on comparison from Elomymelo described the change more cautiously, characterizing it as a shift in tonal balance rather than a straightforward upgrade — some listeners may prefer the Pro 2's tuning.

On lossless audio: neither AirPods Pro 2 nor AirPods Pro 3 supports high-resolution codecs like LDAC or aptX over standard Bluetooth, as confirmed by SoundGuys' testing. If lossless streaming quality is your primary concern, that limitation applies equally to both generations, and an upgrade won't change it.

Fit, Comfort, and Build

Apple states the Pro 3's redesign was informed by more than 10,000 ear scans and 100,000 hours of testing, resulting in foam-infused ear tips in five sizes, including a new XXS option. The Medium article comparing the two models described the Pro 3's hybrid silicone-foam tips as creating a more secure seal during workouts, while the Pro 2 was described as feeling less intrusive for casual, all-day wear. Water resistance also improved, from IP54 on the Pro 2 to IP57 on the Pro 3, meaning the newer model is rated to handle more direct exposure to sweat and rain.

New Features Exclusive to AirPods Pro 3

  • Heart rate sensor: Built into the earbuds for workout tracking. Note that if you already wear an Apple Watch during exercise, this feature offers less added value, as one comparison from Medium pointed out.
  • Live Translation: Real-time conversation translation between supported languages, part of Apple Intelligence integration.
  • Improved microphone array: Independent testing from RecordingNow ranked the Pro 3's mic quality among the best in the wireless earbud category.

Adaptive Audio and the Hearing Aid Feature: What Carries Over

Adaptive Audio, which blends Transparency and Noise Cancellation dynamically, is available on both generations. The same is true of Apple's clinically-authorized Hearing Aid Feature. According to the National Hearing Institute, the FDA's authorization — the first of its kind for over-the-counter hearing aid software — covers both AirPods Pro 2 (with firmware 7B19 or later) and AirPods Pro 3, when paired with a compatible iPhone or iPad running iOS 18 or later. The FDA's review included a clinical study of 118 adults with mild to moderate hearing loss and found no adverse effects.

This means Pro 2 owners don't need to upgrade to access this feature — it's a strong reason to keep your current pair if hearing assistance is your main interest.

AirPods Pro 2 vs AirPods 4: The Budget Alternative

If price is the deciding factor, it's worth considering where the standard AirPods 4 fits in. Apple currently sells AirPods 4 at $129 and AirPods 4 with ANC at $179 — both cheaper than either Pro model. According to TechRadar's comparison, the Pro-series buds offer a true in-ear seal for stronger noise isolation and richer sound, while the AirPods 4 use an open-fit design that some users find more comfortable for all-day wear but that isolates far less outside noise. If you don't need ANC or an in-ear fit, AirPods 4 with ANC is a reasonable middle ground below the Pro tier.

AirPods Pro 1 vs 2 vs 3: A Quick Historical Note

For context on how far the Pro line has come: the original AirPods Pro launched in October 2019 with the Apple H1 chip. The AirPods Pro 2 arrived in September 2022 (Lightning case) and was updated with a USB-C case in September 2023, introducing the H2 chip. The AirPods Pro 3 launched in September 2025, keeping the H2 chip but adding the U2 chip to the case, according to The Apple Wiki's technical documentation. Each generation has focused on ANC, fit, and case charging improvements rather than a fundamentally different audio chip.

Is AirPods Pro 2 Still Worth Buying in 2026?

Now that it's officially discontinued by Apple, according to Wikipedia's AirPods Pro entry, AirPods Pro 2 is only available through third-party retailers and Apple's refurbished channel, not Apple's own current lineup. That said, independent reviewers haven't written it off. RecordingNow's long-term review, updated for 2026, still called it an excellent value package, especially at discounted pricing around $150–$199, and favorably compared its sound quality to headphones costing many times more. If you can find it at a meaningful discount below AirPods Pro 3 pricing, it remains a legitimate buy — just be aware that as stock diminishes, availability and warranty support may become less predictable over time. Remember that prices for both models change daily, so the value equation can shift quickly.

Trade-In Value: What to Expect

If you're planning to upgrade and want to offset the cost, know that Apple does not currently offer an official trade-in program for AirPods, according to discussions on Apple's own community forums. Instead, third-party buyback marketplaces like BankMyCell aggregate offers from multiple resellers; as of mid-2026, AirPods Pro 2 trade-in quotes vary by condition, working battery status, and whether the case matches the original set. If you're weighing an Apple audio event purchase against selling your old pair, expect more value from a private sale (eBay, Facebook Marketplace) than from any formal trade-in path, since none currently exists for AirPods.

AirPods Pro 3 Rumors and What Might Come Next

Beyond the current Pro 3, Apple watchers are already looking ahead. MacRumors has reported that Apple is working on AirPods with built-in cameras, potentially unveiled alongside future iPhone hardware, with a design expected to resemble the AirPods Pro 3 but with small camera modules added. These remain unconfirmed rumors rather than announced products, so they shouldn't factor into a purchase decision today — but they're worth knowing about if you'd rather wait for the next major redesign than buy into the current generation.

Final Verdict

There's no universal right answer to "should I upgrade from AirPods Pro 2." The AirPods Pro 3 is a genuine, evidence-backed improvement in ANC strength, microphone quality, fit security, and earbud-only battery life — confirmed independently by RTINGS, SoundGuys, and hands-on reviewers. But it's not a reinvention: it uses the same H2 chip, the same Bluetooth codecs, and many of its best software features (Adaptive Audio, the Hearing Aid Feature) already exist on the Pro 2.

If ANC performance, workout tracking, or call clarity are priorities for you, the upgrade is well justified — especially if you can catch the Pro 3 at a sale price closer to $189. If you're a casual listener who bought AirPods Pro 2 for solid sound and reliable noise cancellation, there's little urgency to replace a pair that's still performing well and is now considerably cheaper. Keep an eye on daily price fluctuations for both models; the right answer may change depending on what's on sale on any given day.
