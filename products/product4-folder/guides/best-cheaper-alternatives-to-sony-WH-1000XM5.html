Why Look Beyond the Sony WH-1000XM5?

The Sony WH-1000XM5 earned its reputation as one of the most complete noise-cancelling headphones on the market: strong active noise cancellation (ANC), a comfortable fit, and reliable call quality. But it carries a flagship price, and it's no longer Sony's newest model — the WH-1000XM6 has since taken over as the current flagship, which has pushed WH-1000XM5 pricing down and created room for a wave of genuinely competent, much cheaper alternatives.

If you're searching for cheaper alternatives to the WH-1000XM5, you're probably trying to answer one of a few questions: Can I get 80–90% of the experience for half the price or less? Which budget or mid-range ANC headphones have independent testing behind their claims? Is it smarter to buy an older or lower-tier Sony instead of switching brands? And what specific trade-offs — comfort, call quality, codecs — come with going cheaper?

It's also worth being upfront about what this guide is not. It's not a claim that any of these seven alternatives beats the WH-1000XM5 outright — independent testing generally still favors Sony's flagship on overall polish and consistency. What it does answer is a narrower, more useful question: if you specifically like what the WH-1000XM5 represents but don't want to pay flagship pricing, which alternatives have real evidence behind them, and what do you give up with each one?

Quick Verdict: The Short Answer

  • Best overall value: Sony WH-CH720N — Sony's own sound tuning and ANC processor at roughly a quarter of the WH-1000XM5's original price.
  • Closest to the WH-1000XM5 experience: Sony WH-1000XM4 — the direct predecessor, still sold new and frequently discounted.
  • Best noise cancellation per dollar: Anker Soundcore Space One — unusually strong ANC for a sub-$120 headphone.
  • Best sound quality for the price: Sennheiser Accentum Wireless — reviewers consistently single out its tuning.
  • Best feature set and customization: JBL Tour One M2 — extensive EQ, hearing personalization, and adaptive ANC.
  • Best known premium brand at a mid-range price: Bose QuietComfort Headphones (2023).
  • Best true budget pick: 1MORE SonoFlow / SonoFlow SE — often under $60, with independent testers repeatedly flagging it as outperforming its price class.

None of these headphones beats the Sony WH-1000XM5 in every category. Each one wins for a specific kind of buyer — which is why this guide is broken down by trade-off rather than a single universal winner.

Key Terms Worth Understanding Before You Compare

ANC vs. passive isolation

Passive isolation is the physical blocking of sound by the ear cup material and seal. Active noise cancellation uses microphones to detect incoming sound and generates an inverse wave to cancel it electronically. ANC is far more effective against low, steady noise (engine hum, HVAC) than against sudden or high-pitched sound (voices, clattering dishes). This is why the Sennheiser Accentum Wireless scores well against mid-and-high-frequency noise while lagging specifically in the low-frequency range that matters most on airplanes.

Bluetooth codecs (SBC, AAC, LDAC, aptX)

A codec determines how audio is compressed for wireless transmission. SBC is the baseline every headphone supports but the lowest quality. AAC works well specifically with Apple devices. LDAC (Sony) and aptX/aptX Adaptive (Qualcomm) allow higher-resolution streaming — but only if your source device supports the same codec. An iPhone cannot use LDAC even if the headphones support it. Before paying more for codec support, confirm your phone or laptop actually supports the matching codec.

Bluetooth multipoint and latency

Multipoint lets a headphone stay connected to two devices simultaneously and switch audio between them without re-pairing — increasingly common even in budget headphones on this list. Latency is the delay between audio being sent and heard; above roughly 150–200ms it becomes noticeable as out-of-sync video. This is why the Sony WH-CH720N's measured ~290ms latency and the Bose QuietComfort's ~183ms lag are called out specifically — they matter far more to video-callers and gamers than to music listeners.

1. Sony WH-1000XM4 — The Closest You Can Get to the WH-1000XM5

The WH-1000XM4 was Sony's flagship before the XM5 replaced it, and Sony continued selling it afterward as a lower-priced option. TechRadar's coverage of the WH-1000XM5 explicitly frames the XM4 as "the biggest threat" to its successor, noting that although it's "not the last word in audio quality," the differences in sound and noise cancellation between the two models are "pretty small" — and that its own review called the XM4 "a better option for most people" due to the price gap.TechRadar

It has been reported selling as low as roughly $170, compared to an original list price closer to $350, and continues to see steep discounts years after launch. Because Sony has now released two newer generations (XM5 and XM6), retailers have strong incentive to keep discounting XM4 stock — but exact pricing shifts weekly.

2. Sony WH-CH720N — Sony's Sound and ANC Processor at a Fraction of the Price

Sony markets the WH-CH720N as its lightest wireless noise-cancelling headphone, built around the same Integrated Processor V1 chip used in the flagship WH-1000XM5, but stripped of higher-end extras like LDAC, a folding hinge, wear detection, and a carrying case.

This is where sources genuinely diverge. RTINGS gave the WH-CH720N a noise isolation score of 6.3/10 — for comparison, the WH-1000XM6 scores 9.7 on the same scale — indicating its ANC is meaningfully behind Sony's flagship tier. At the same time, RTINGS measured battery life at roughly 40 hours 11 minutes with ANC on, about 15% longer than Sony's own 35-hour rating.RTINGS TechGearLab ranked it lower (15th of 26) when judged strictly against its ~$180 list price, noting "cheaper options" were a better buy at that price — a comment about value at list price, not raw performance. Reviewers who tested it at its common $80–$100 street price describe it as a genuine bargain. In short: the verdict depends heavily on what you actually pay.

Buyer Profiles: Which Alternative Fits Your Situation?

  • "I want the closest thing to a WH-1000XM5 for less." Go with the Sony WH-1000XM4 — same sound signature, same app, and a gap reviewers consistently describe as small.
  • "I fly often and need the strongest possible ANC on a budget." The Anker Soundcore Space One or Bose QuietComfort — both independently verified; the Bose carries a stronger RTINGS isolation score but costs more.
  • "Sound quality matters more than blocking every last bit of noise." The Sennheiser Accentum Wireless, with the caveat of its weaker low-frequency ANC.
  • "I want the lightest possible headphone." The Sony WH-CH720N wins clearly at 192g — accept the trade-offs in ANC depth and codecs.
  • "I like to tinker with EQ and customize everything." The JBL Tour One M2's app depth and Personi-Fi are unmatched here, ideally at a discount.
  • "I just want something cheap that works well enough." The 1MORE SonoFlow or SonoFlow SE — repeatedly called the best value in the budget ANC category.

Design, Comfort, and Build Quality Compared

Weight and ear cup design vary more across this group than spec sheets suggest, and comfort is where reviewer impressions matter more than any single number. The Sony WH-CH720N is the clear outlier at 192 grams — the lightest here, including the WH-1000XM5 itself — though at least one review flagged shallow ear cup depth for larger ears. The Sennheiser Accentum (≈221g) is second-lightest with a firm but comfortable clamp; its main drawback is the lack of a case and a non-folding design.

The Bose QuietComfort (≈236g) and Anker Space One (≈258g) sit mid-pack. Bose's design has changed only incrementally across generations — familiar to existing owners, but not meaningfully innovated for several cycles. The JBL Tour One M2 (≈278g) is the heaviest, yet multiple reviewers describe it as comfortable in practice due to generous padding — weight alone doesn't predict comfort; padding, clamp distribution, and headband design matter just as much. The 1MORE SonoFlow family (≈238–246g) was called "incredibly comfortable" by TechRadar, though the newer HQ51 variant has been reported as clamping harder and running warmer.

On build materials, none of these alternatives use premium materials found on $400+ headphones — expect plastic housings across the board, with differences in hinges, headband padding, and foldability. The WH-1000XM4, Space One, Tour One M2, and SonoFlow fold for storage; the WH-CH720N and Accentum Wireless do not.

App and Software Ecosystem

Every headphone here pairs with a companion app, but depth and reliability vary considerably — an area where owner feedback carries more weight than marketing. Sony's Headphones Connect app (WH-1000XM4 and WH-CH720N) is mature and widely used, with EQ presets, ANC/Ambient customization, DSEE upscaling controls, and firmware updates, maintained across many product generations. Anker's Soundcore app is feature-rich (custom EQ, adaptive ANC modes) though some owners describe occasional Bluetooth quirks. Bose's app focuses on simplicity and the savable ANC presets; JBL's Headphones app is the deepest of the group — the source of both its appeal to power users and the "overwhelming" first-setup complaint from casual buyers. Sennheiser's app covers EQ and firmware; 1MORE's is the most frequently criticized, with a recurring "wonky" theme in owner and reviewer feedback.

The Bottom Line

If you want the closest overall experience to the WH-1000XM5, buy the WH-1000XM4. If your budget is fixed under $120, buy the Anker Soundcore Space One for ANC or the Sennheiser Accentum Wireless for sound. If you're spending under $100, the Sony WH-CH720N (comfort) and 1MORE SonoFlow (value) are the credible picks. And if you can wait for a sale and want the strongest noise cancellation of any alternative here, the Bose QuietComfort Headphones have the lab data to back it up. Whatever you choose, confirm the live price before buying — in this category, the discount is often the deciding factor.
