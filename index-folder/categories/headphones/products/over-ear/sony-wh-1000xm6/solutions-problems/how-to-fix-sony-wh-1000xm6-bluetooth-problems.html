How to Fix Sony WH-1000XM6 Bluetooth Problems

If your Sony WH-1000XM6 drops out, refuses to pair, goes silent on a laptop, or plays nothing after a phone call, the cause is usually one of about eight things, and most can be fixed in a few minutes without sending anything back. This guide lists those causes, gives the fixes in the order that makes sense to try them, and ends with habits that stop the problems from returning.

The fastest fixes, in order

Try these before reading anything else. Each one takes under two minutes, and the first three solve a large share of cases according to Sony's own troubleshooting flow.

  1. Turn the headphones off and on, and restart the phone, laptop or TV you are connecting to.
  2. Move the source device close, with a clear path between it and your head. Sony lists distance, interference, obstructions and low battery as the four primary causes of connection drops.
  3. In the Sony | Sound Connect app, set the Bluetooth connection mode to prioritize a stable connection. Sony's own pairing troubleshooting page recommends this when radio interference is likely.
  4. Switch off "Connect to 2 devices simultaneously" (multipoint) and test with a single device.
  5. If sound stutters or calls misbehave on a phone or PC, switch the LE Audio setting to "Classic Audio only." This is a Sony-documented fix for missing ringtones and failed calls.
  6. Delete the headphones from the source device's Bluetooth list, then pair again, choosing the name that does not start with "LE-".
  7. Update the headphone firmware through the app.
  8. Reset, then initialize if needed (steps are in the fix ladder below).

If none of that works, skip to the section on when the headphones themselves are the problem.

Why the XM6 has more Bluetooth failure points than older headphones

The WH-1000XM6 is not a simple Bluetooth device. According to SoundGuys' lab review, it uses a Bluetooth 5.3 radio and supports LDAC, AAC, SBC and LC3, with multipoint available. MusicTech's review lists the same codecs and notes support for Bluetooth LE for low-latency modes.

That list matters for troubleshooting because the headphones can reach your phone in several different ways:

  • Classic Bluetooth with SBC or AAC, the universal, conservative path.
  • Classic Bluetooth with LDAC, which sends much more data and is Android-only on the XM6 according to SoundGuys, which says iOS users cannot use LDAC or LE Audio features.
  • Bluetooth LE Audio with the LC3 codec, which Sony added in firmware and has expanded since.
  • Any of the above combined with multipoint, where two devices are connected at once.

YallaBuy analysis: each of those paths has its own bugs and its own compatibility gaps with the phone, laptop or TV on the other end. A headphone that only used SBC would have fewer ways to fail. The XM6 gives you more capability, and the cost is that a problem can hide in a setting you did not know existed. Most of the fixes in this guide amount to steering the headphones onto the simplest path that still does what you need.

There is also a version question. Sony has changed Bluetooth behavior through firmware more than once. Notebookcheck reported that version 2.0.2 (July 2025) added a Low Latency connection method alongside Stable Connection and Sound Quality, and reported again that version 3.0.0 (October 2025) extended LE Audio to more features. If you read older forum advice, it may describe a menu that no longer looks the same.

The causes, one at a time

Cause 1: Radio environment, distance and obstructions

This is the least exciting cause and often the correct one. Bluetooth shares the 2.4 GHz band with Wi-Fi, microwave ovens and many other devices.

Sony's support documentation on skipping sound says interference can occur when a Wi-Fi device in use near the headphones shares that band, and suggests keeping the headphones as far as possible from microwave ovens, ticket gates and other sources of electromagnetic radiation. It also says obstructions between headphones and source weaken the link, and it advises moving a phone out of a bag or pocket, removing a case that blocks signal, or placing the devices closer together.

Sony's pairing troubleshooting page names where interference tends to occur: crowded places, near store cash registers, microwaves, traffic lights, automatic doors, and crowded trains and stations. Another Sony page puts the normal range at about 10 meters, shorter around thick walls or metal structures, and says drops at long distance are normal behavior.

What this means in practice: if the headphones drop only in specific places (a station platform, a gym full of phones, one corner of your house next to the router), the headphones are probably fine. Changing where you stand, or which pocket the phone is in, costs nothing and proves the point.

Cause 2: A connection mode that favors sound quality over stability

The XM6 offers connection priorities in the Sony | Sound Connect app. Sony's firmware notes for version 3.1.5 describe three Bluetooth connection methods on some smartphones: Prioritize Sound Quality, Prioritize Stable Connection and Low Latency.

What Hi-Fi? says in its XM6 settings guide that choosing stability is worth considering if you often use the headphones in a city with tall buildings and heavy wireless traffic, and that its reviewers never experienced problems during testing and would otherwise leave the headphones on sound quality.

SoundGuys adds a useful detail for Android: the headphones default to a "best effort" LDAC setting when connected to an Android phone, and the reviewer found that forcing LDAC to its 660 kbps option gave the best blend of stability and performance. SoundGuys also says multipoint forces you to accept a lower-bitrate codec.

YallaBuy analysis: LDAC moves far more data than AAC or SBC, which gives it more to lose when the radio environment is noisy. Sony does not publish a precise per-device description of what "Prioritize Stable Connection" changes, so treat the exact codec behavior as something that can vary by phone. The practical test is simple: switch to stable, listen for a day, and see if the dropouts stop. If they do, you have your answer, and you have lost a small amount of audio fidelity that many listeners will struggle to hear on compressed streaming files anyway.

Cause 3: Multipoint conflicts

Multipoint lets the XM6 stay connected to two devices at once, typically a phone and a laptop. It is one of the headphone's selling points, and it is also the setting behind a recurring group of complaints.

Sony's position. In its article on no sound from the second device, Sony says to confirm "Connect to 2 devices simultaneously" is on, then stop playback on the first device and close its music app. Sony adds that on some devices, silent streaming can continue on the first device after the second connects, which keeps the headphones attached to the first and stops audio from switching.

Owner reports. Reviews on Adorama's product pages include several complaints in this area. One March 2026 review on the silver model's page describes laptop audio vanishing entirely after the phone briefly made a sound, and says toggling Bluetooth or reconnecting from the PC did nothing. Another review on a separate Adorama listing says any other audio playing stops meeting audio and mic use in Zoom, Teams or WhatsApp until the headphones are reconnected, and a different reviewer on that page says the headphones struggle on Teams on a laptop whenever there is a second audio source.

These are individual retailer reviews. They show that the problem exists for some owners, but they cannot tell us how many. Positive reports also exist: another reviewer on the same page says switching between two remembered devices is smooth. The honest summary is mixed evidence with unknown prevalence.

YallaBuy analysis: the pattern across those reports (laptop plus phone, a meeting app, a second audio source) is consistent with Sony's own note about a background audio stream holding the connection. That is an inference, not a confirmed diagnosis. It does suggest that if you only need multipoint some of the time, turning it off for important calls is a sensible precaution.

Cause 4: LE Audio and Classic Audio fighting each other

LE Audio is the newer Bluetooth audio standard behind the LC3 codec. It is meant to improve efficiency and enable features like low-latency gaming and shared listening, but it brings its own compatibility problems.

Sony has published at least four support articles specifically about the XM6 and LE Audio:

  • Ringtone not heard or calls cannot be answered. Sony recommends restarting the app or device first. If that fails, open Sound Connect and set the LE Audio connection setting to "Classic Audio only (the conventional connection method)."
  • Cannot switch to the other paired device in the app when two devices are connected over LE Audio. Sony says to switch the connection from the Bluetooth settings of the device you want to use.
  • Voice chat heard only through the smartphone while connected over LE Audio.
  • Volume changing automatically when the connection moves between Classic Audio and LE Audio.

Owner experience on computers is less happy. A poster on Slickdeals wrote that enabling LE Audio in Windows is only possible when the PC maker's driver indicates support, even if the Bluetooth chip is rated for version 5.2 or higher, and returned the headphones over it. A reviewer on Adorama complained about the same limitation and noted that Sony does not include a USB dongle. These are single reports, not test results, but they match SoundGuys' point that iOS devices cannot use LE Audio with the XM6 and RTINGS' note that low latency requires a compatible phone or Bluetooth LE dongle.

YallaBuy analysis: if your use is music and calls and you do not need low latency, the simplest course is to set LE Audio to Classic Audio only and see whether the problem goes away. You lose nothing you were using. If you do want LE Audio for gaming, check that both ends support it before you blame the headphones.

Cause 5: The "LE-" duplicate and stale pairing data

When you pair from a phone's or computer's Bluetooth menu, the XM6 can appear under two names. Sony's pairing article says to select the model name without the "LE-" prefix, and if only the LE- name shows, to wait a few seconds for the plain name to appear. Sony's computer troubleshooting page goes further: pairing a computer with the LE-named entry puts it in Low Energy mode, which may cause the connection to fail or the sound to be inaudible.

Stale pairing records are the second half of this cause. Sony's guidance on pairing with many devices says Bluetooth headphones can normally be paired with up to eight devices but connect to one at a time (multipoint aside), and that when you have paired several devices, turning on a different one may not always connect. Sony's fix is to make a manual connection from the Bluetooth settings of the device you want, or to find which other device the headphones are currently attached to and disconnect it there.

The pairing page also warns that headphones that automatically reconnect to the last device can block a connection from a different one. If you are switching from your phone to a laptop, disconnect the phone first.

Cause 6: Firmware and app bugs

Sony fixes Bluetooth bugs through firmware, which makes updates the single most useful maintenance habit. Sony Canada's downloads page listed firmware 3.1.5 (released June 30, 2026) as the current version when this guide was checked, with a Sound Connect app update dated September 30, 2026.

Sony's release notes for 3.1.5 list these fixes: recording and playback failing in some iPhone apps, and call audio becoming inaudible when music or video plays during a call. Android Authority notes the update also includes earlier changes and added Gaming Audio Profile support, so those fixes may not all be new in 3.1.5. Check Sony's page for the exact list.

Two cautions from the same sources. Sony says not to charge the headphones or turn them off during an update, and not to update where Wi-Fi is weak or where 2.4 GHz devices crowd the area. SoundGuys-owned reporting on the update adds that Sony suggests disconnecting other Bluetooth devices such as smartwatches first. Also, one secondary source, basic-tutorials.com, reports that Sony says the firmware cannot be rolled back after updating. That means an update is a one-way decision, which is another reason to read the release notes.

Cause 7: The computer is sending sound somewhere else

On Windows, "connected" does not mean "selected." Sony's computer troubleshooting article (Windows 11 steps) says to open Settings, then System, then Sound, scroll to Advanced, open More sound settings, and on the Playback tab select the headphones and choose Set as Default Device. For microphone problems, do the same on the Recording tab.

If that fails, Sony's sequence is: turn the headphones off and on, restart the computer, reset the headphones, initialize them, and try another app or computer. Sony adds that after initialization you should delete the pairing from the computer and pair again.

A related note on call quality: a Slickdeals owner wrote that sound quality on classic Bluetooth drops sharply whenever the microphone is active, which is why LE Audio looked attractive to them. This is an owner explanation, not a lab finding, but it is a reminder that thin-sounding calls on a PC are not necessarily a connection fault.

Cause 8: Low battery

Sony puts battery charge among its four primary causes of connection drops. SoundGuys measured 37 hours and 14 minutes in its standardized battery test, so a dead battery is unlikely to be a surprise mid-day, but it is worth ruling out if drops cluster at the end of a long travel stretch or after weeks without charging.

The fix ladder

Work down this list. Stop when the problem is gone. The steps are ordered from least disruptive to most disruptive, because the later ones erase your pairings and settings.

Level 1: Quick resets (about 2 minutes)

  1. Power the headphones off and on.
  2. Turn the source device's Bluetooth off and on, or restart the device.
  3. Move closer and reposition the source device (out of a bag, out of a back pocket, away from the router).
  4. If you use noise cancelling in a crowded place, note that Sony's skipping article suggests turning noise cancelling off while diagnosing interference.

Level 2: App settings (about 5 minutes)

Open Sony | Sound Connect and check each of these. Menu names can differ between app versions and phone platforms, so use the labels below as a guide rather than an exact path.

  1. Connection mode. Set it to prioritize a stable connection. Sony says this is a fix for interference-prone places.
  2. Multipoint. Turn off "Connect to 2 devices simultaneously" (found under All device settings, then Connection, according to Sony's multipoint article). Test with one device.
  3. LE Audio. If you have call or ringtone problems, change the LE Audio connection setting to Classic Audio only.
  4. Update. If the app offers a firmware update, install it using the precautions in Cause 6.

Change one setting at a time and give each a real test. If you change three things at once and the problem disappears, you will not know which one mattered, and you will not know what to change back.

Level 3: Re-pair the headphones (about 5 minutes)

Sony's recommended sequence when pairing fails is to restart the playback device, delete the headphones' Bluetooth information from it, and pair again.

  1. On the phone or computer, open Bluetooth settings and remove ("forget") the XM6.
  2. Restart that device.
  3. Put the headphones in pairing mode. SoundGuys describes this as holding the power button until you hear "PAIRING."
  4. Select the entry named WH-1000XM6 without the "LE-" prefix. If only the LE- entry appears, wait a few seconds.
  5. Test before reconnecting anything else.

Level 4: Reset, then initialize

Sony publishes both procedures in its WH-1000XM6 reset article.

Reset. Connect the headphones to a power source with the supplied USB-C cable. Press and hold the power button and the NC/AMB (noise cancelling and Ambient Sound) button together for about 5 seconds. The charging indicator turns off and lights up again. Sony says a reset keeps your pairing information and settings.

Initialize (factory reset). Press and hold the power button and the NC/AMB button for about 10 seconds. Sony warns that this restores the factory defaults and deletes all pairing information. Sony also says that if you updated the software after buying the headphones, the update is kept through initialization. Sony's article does not specify whether the headphones should be on, off or charging for this step. Check the Help Guide for your unit if the first attempt does not work. For comparison, Sony's page for the related 1000X The Collexion says to turn the headphones off with no USB cable connected first, but do not assume the XM6 is identical.

After initializing, delete the old pairing from every device that had one, then pair again as in Level 3. Skipping that deletion is a common reason a factory reset appears to do nothing.

Level 5: Test with a second device

Sony's flow includes a diagnostic step worth taking seriously. If pairing fails, try a different phone, Walkman or other device. Sony says that if pairing works on the second device, the original device may be malfunctioning and you should consult its manufacturer. If it also fails on other devices, the headphones may be malfunctioning and service may be required.

This single test separates "my laptop's Bluetooth is the problem" from "my headphones are the problem," and it saves a lot of wasted resets.

Device-specific advice

Android phones

  • Android defaults to a best-effort LDAC setting according to SoundGuys. If dropouts appear mostly with high-quality streaming, set a stable connection mode in Sound Connect.
  • The Low Latency and LE Audio options are described by Sony as available on "some smartphones," so expect the menu to differ by phone model.
  • If you changed Android's developer-option Bluetooth audio settings in the past, reset them. SoundGuys notes that developer options are one place the LDAC setting can be forced.

iPhone

  • SoundGuys states that LDAC and LE Audio features are unavailable with iOS devices at the time of its review, so settings about those will not help you. The headphones connect with AAC or SBC.
  • One Adorama reviewer rated the XM6 four stars and reported connectivity issues on iOS even with a single connection. That is one report. Sony's 3.1.5 notes list a fix for recording and playback problems in some iPhone apps, so update before troubleshooting further.
  • Re-pairing and a connection-mode change are the two most useful steps.

Windows laptops and desktops

  • Check the default playback and recording devices (Sony's steps in Cause 7).
  • Pair with the plain model name, not the LE- entry.
  • Do not expect LE Audio to work just because the Bluetooth adapter claims version 5.2 or newer. Owner reports on Slickdeals and Adorama suggest the PC maker's driver is what decides, and a Bluetooth LE dongle is one workaround that Redditors and RTINGS mention for compatibility. Treat any specific dongle as unverified until you check its LE Audio support.
  • For meetings, turn off multipoint if another device keeps taking over the audio.
  • Sony publishes a separate guide on using Bluetooth headphones for video calls on a computer.

Macs

  • Sony's own documentation focuses on Windows, so Mac advice here is thinner. One blog post on Medium argues that high-quality Bluetooth microphone audio on Macs is available only with AirPods and possibly Beats, which would explain thin call audio on the XM6. It cites a Reddit thread and is a single-author claim, not independent testing, so treat it as a lead rather than a fact.
  • Holding Option and clicking the Bluetooth or sound icon in the menu bar lets you choose input and output devices separately, according to that post. Using the Mac's built-in microphone with the headphones as output is a common way to avoid the call-quality drop.

TVs and game consoles

  • Sony maintains a BRAVIA Connectivity Guide for pairing with its own TVs.
  • Third-party TV problems exist, but the reports I found concern older Sony headphones. Samsung community threads describe pairing failures between Samsung TVs and the WH-1000XM4 and WH-1000XM5. I did not find XM6-specific Samsung reports, so do not assume the same cause. The step that has worked for other models is the same re-pair and reset sequence above, plus checking the TV's own firmware.
  • Consoles: one third-party source notes the XM6 has no direct Bluetooth connection to PS5 or Xbox without an adapter or TV connection. That claim comes from a retailer-style answer page, not Sony, so check your console's current Bluetooth audio support before buying an adapter.

How to avoid these problems in the first place

This is the section to bookmark. Everything here follows from the causes above.

  1. Keep firmware and the app current, but read the notes first. Firmware is how Sony fixes Bluetooth bugs. Update in a calm environment, with the phone close, Wi-Fi stable, no charging, and other Bluetooth devices disconnected.
  2. Decide whether you need multipoint. If you use the XM6 with one device most days, leave multipoint off. Turn it on when you actually need it. This removes the most commonly reported source of laptop-and-phone conflicts.
  3. Choose your connection priority on purpose. Use stable connection in crowded or high-interference places, and sound quality when you are at home or in quiet surroundings.
  4. Pick Classic Audio or LE Audio deliberately. If you do not use low-latency gaming or Auracast, Classic Audio only is the most conservative setting, and Sony documents it as a fix for call problems.
  5. Pair with the plain name every time. Ignore the LE- entry unless you know you want it.
  6. Prune your paired devices. Remove phones, tablets, TVs and laptops you no longer use. Sony's general guidance says connecting to a particular device can become unreliable when many have been paired.
  7. Disconnect before switching. If you move from phone to laptop, disconnect the phone first, or use the device's Bluetooth settings to force the new connection.
  8. Keep the phone in a better spot. Sony says to take the device out of bags and pockets if the signal is weak. A front pocket or a hand-held phone often does better than a bag, in YallaBuy's analysis, though Sony only says to try moving it.
  9. Keep the battery from running flat. Sony lists battery charge as a primary cause, and lithium cells age faster when they spend a lot of time at very low charge, according to SoundGuys' care guidance.
  10. Keep the 3.5 mm cable in the case. SoundGuys notes the XM6 includes a 3.5 mm cable and that USB-C audio is not supported. When wireless fails at an inconvenient moment, wired playback is the fallback that skips every Bluetooth variable.

When the headphones themselves are the problem

Sony's own test is the most reliable one: if they will not pair with any device after a reset and initialization, the headphones may be malfunctioning and may need service.

Before contacting Sony, gather the following. It makes the conversation shorter.

  • The firmware version, shown in the Sound Connect app.
  • Whether the problem occurs on every device or only one.
  • Which of the fixes above you tried and what happened.
  • Your proof of purchase and purchase date.

Check the warranty terms and the retailer's return window in your region, since both vary. Sony's support site has a Product Repair page. There is no single failure rate for XM6 Bluetooth faults in anything I could find, so do not assume a defect before you have run the second-device test.

If the problem is a conflict with one device (a particular laptop, a particular TV), returning the headphones will not fix it. That is a point for the source device's maker, or for a different setup such as a Bluetooth adapter.

What this guide could not confirm

  • How common these problems are. Sony does not publish failure or complaint rates, and the owner reports above come from a handful of retailer and forum posts. The evidence shows that multipoint and LE Audio issues exist for some owners, not that they affect most.
  • Independent lab testing of connection drops. RTINGS and SoundGuys test latency, codecs and features, but I did not find a lab test measuring dropout frequency. What Hi-Fi? says it never experienced connection problems in its testing, and GSMArena reported no noticeable latency with AAC and LDAC.
  • Exact menu paths. Sony changes Sound Connect between releases, so labels may differ on your phone.
  • Exact codec behavior in each connection mode. Sony's sources do not spell it out per device.
  • Anything that changed after October 25, 2026. Newer firmware may have changed these behaviors.
