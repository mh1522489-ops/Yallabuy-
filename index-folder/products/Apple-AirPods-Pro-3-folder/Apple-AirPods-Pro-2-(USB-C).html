Price & Availability

The official launch price was $249. After Apple introduced the AirPods Pro 3 at roughly the same price in September 2025, it discontinued sales of the 2nd generation model through its own stores and website. According to a Macworld Macworld report, Amazon continued selling the AirPods Pro 2 at around $199 as part of Apple's product lineup on the platform.

The product has also seen seasonal price drops, including a reported all-time low of $179 during one of Amazon's major sales events, and a separate Black Friday/Cyber Monday price of $189, according to Tom's Guide Tom's Guide.

What Actually Changed with the USB‑C Version?

According to Apple's official announcement, the difference between this version and the previous Lightning model comes down to three things: the USB‑C port replacing Lightning, improved dust resistance rated IP54 (versus IPX4 on the Lightning version), and support for Lossless Audio when paired with the Apple Vision Pro.

Everything else — including sound quality, noise cancellation, and physical design — is identical to the Lightning version launched in 2022, according to a review from Inverse Inverse. Even the newer software features, such as Adaptive Audio and Conversation Awareness, are available to owners of the older Lightning model through the iOS 17 update, per the same source.

In short: anyone who already owns the Lightning AirPods Pro 2 won't gain much by upgrading. But first-time buyers with a USB‑C iPhone will find this version more practical for unifying their charging cables.

Main Features

Active Noise Cancellation (ANC)

Apple claims up to twice the noise cancellation of the first-generation model Apple. Independent testing from RTINGS RTINGS confirmed a clear improvement in noise isolation across the frequency spectrum compared to the previous generation, and found the earbuds outperform Apple's own AirPods 4 in this area. SoundGuys SoundGuys similarly ranked the ANC performance among the best in its class compared to competing earbuds.

That said, RecordingNow RecordingNow — which describes itself as an independent publisher that purchases the products it tests — noted that performance still falls short of larger over-ear headphones like the Bose QuietComfort Ultra or AirPods Max.

Transparency Mode and Adaptive Audio

This feature lets users hear their surroundings without removing the earbuds, and Adaptive Audio automatically switches between this mode and noise cancellation depending on the environment. CNN Underscored CNN Underscored called this the standout upgrade in the update, but noted the system sometimes leans too heavily on Transparency mode in noisy environments like subway platforms, resulting in an occasionally unpleasant amount of ambient sound.

Battery Life

Apple claims up to 6 hours of continuous listening Apple. An independent test by TechGearLab TechGearLab, which played music at 75% volume until the earbuds died, measured an actual result of 8.5 hours — higher than Apple's stated figure. The same outlet reported that the charging case provides roughly four additional full charges, bringing the total to about 34 hours before the case itself needs recharging.

By contrast, Headphones Addict Headphones Addict noted that battery life is good overall but lower than some direct competitors in the same price range — an observation that has come up in more than one independent review.

Water and Dust Resistance

The IP54 rating (based on the official IEC 60529 standard) means good resistance to sweat and light rain, but it does not mean the earbuds are submersible, a point RecordingNow RecordingNow made explicitly.

Fit and Stability During Exercise

RTINGS RTINGS testing noted that some users found the earbuds could shift or loosen during high-intensity workouts like HIIT, though they remained comfortable and stable enough for moderate exercise once a good tip fit was found. This observation also appeared in another independent tech review site, making it a pattern seen across more than one independent source rather than an isolated complaint.

Call Quality

Tom's Guide Tom's Guide described call quality as a relative weak point compared to other aspects of the product. This partly contrasts with microphone improvements noted in other reviews following later firmware updates, suggesting the experience may vary depending on firmware version and usage environment.

Performance: Who Tested What?

It's worth being explicit that all performance data in this review comes from independent testing organizations, not hands-on testing by YallaBuy:

RTINGS ran lab tests on noise isolation, fit stability, and resistance ratings, comparing results directly against other Apple products and competitors.

TechGearLab measured actual battery life through continuous playback at a fixed volume.

SoundGuys, Tom's Guide, and What Hi‑Fi provided comprehensive reviews covering sound quality, comfort, and smart features.

When figures differ between these sources — as with battery life — it usually comes down to differences in testing methodology: the volume level used, whether features like ANC or head tracking were enabled, and the type of content played. For that reason, Apple's "up to 6 hours" figure is best treated as an approximate baseline rather than a fixed number.

User Feedback: Recurring Patterns, Not Absolute Judgments

Based on independent reviews and feedback that recurs across multiple sources, the most consistent patterns are:

  • Some users have reported the earbuds shifting during intense physical activity. This has come up in more than one independent source, making it more of an actual design limitation than an isolated complaint.
  • Android users lose a significant part of the product's value. Features like precise Find My tracking, personalized spatial audio, and battery optimization through iOS are unavailable or limited outside the Apple ecosystem, as confirmed by multiple reviews from SoundGuys and Headphones Addict.
  • No sufficient evidence was found in the sources reviewed to describe any technical fault (such as pairing issues or early battery degradation) as a "confirmed widespread problem," so YallaBuy is not including any such claim without adequate documentation.

Comparison with Competitors and Alternatives

Official Successor

Apple AirPods Pro 3

Launched in September 2025 at roughly the same price ($249), the AirPods Pro 3 replaced the 2nd generation in Apple's official store. According to Macworld Macworld, the newer model adds Live Translation and a heart-rate sensor, along with a redesign intended to improve fit. However, the same report noted that the 2nd generation's charging case actually has longer battery life, comes with a charging cable in the box, and is now roughly $50 cheaper at its best discounted price.

In other words: buyers who don't specifically need the heart-rate sensor or Live Translation may currently get better value from the 2nd generation at a discounted price.

Competitor

Google Pixel Buds Pro

According to a comparison published by Headphones Addict Headphones Addict, the Pixel Buds Pro outperform the AirPods Pro 2 in battery life (roughly 8 hours per charge versus Apple's stated figure), in low-frequency noise cancellation, and in supporting multipoint connectivity across all devices rather than only within one ecosystem. This makes them a better fit for Android users or anyone using devices across different platforms.

Apple Alternative

AirPods 4 (with Active Noise Cancellation)

Per RTINGS' RTINGS comparative review, the AirPods Pro 2 remains the better choice for most people thanks to a more predictable fit and interchangeable ear tips, while some users may prefer the AirPods 4's open-ear design for greater environmental awareness and a looser fit. Note that the AirPods Pro 2 supports wireless charging, unlike the AirPods 4, which charges via USB‑C only.

Problems & Limitations

  • Heavy reliance on the Apple ecosystem significantly limits the product's value for Android users, a point confirmed by more than one independent review.
  • Call quality was described in one independent review as falling short of expectations for the price.
  • Some users experienced earbuds shifting during high-intensity workouts.
  • Apple's stated battery life (6 hours) is lower than some direct competitors in the same price range, even though one independent test measured a higher real-world figure.
  • The product no longer has direct sales support from Apple, meaning warranty and support may go through third-party retail channels depending on where it's purchased.

Who Should Buy It?

✓ Ideal For

  • iPhone users looking for strong noise cancellation without paying full price for the AirPods Pro 3
  • Existing Apple device owners who want to take advantage of seamless device switching and spatial audio
  • Anyone who prefers unifying their charging cable via USB‑C with their other modern devices

✗ Not Ideal For

  • Android users, since they'll miss out on most of the smart features that justify the relatively high price
  • Regular high-intensity athletes who need absolute in-ear stability
  • Anyone prioritizing the longest possible battery life in this price range, since some alternatives outperform it specifically in this area
  • Anyone who wants Apple's newest features (like the heart-rate sensor or Live Translation) — in that case, the AirPods Pro 3 is the better fit

Final Verdict

The Apple AirPods Pro (2nd generation) with USB‑C remains a technically strong earbud, with noise cancellation backed by multiple independent test sources and excellent integration with the Apple ecosystem. But it's no longer Apple's newest offering, and official sales have shifted to the AirPods Pro 3.

The right choice depends on three factors: Do you actually use an iPhone? Do you genuinely need the third generation's exclusive features? And is the product available at a clearly discounted price relative to its original cost when you're reading this? If the answer is "yes" to the first question and "no" to the second, this remains a sensible option — especially when found at a solid discount.
