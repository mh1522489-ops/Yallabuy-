Focal Bathys Review: An Honest Look at Focal’s Audiophile Wireless ANC Headphones

The Focal Bathys is a premium wireless noise-cancelling headphone built around one idea: sound quality comes first.

By Mahmoud Hamdy • January 15, 2026 • 25 min read

Quick verdict:

The Focal Bathys is a premium wireless noise-cancelling headphone built around one idea: sound quality comes first.

It is not the best choice if your priority is maximum noise cancellation, the lightest possible design, the strongest call quality, or the most convenient smart features. Sony and Bose are generally stronger choices for those priorities.

But if your priority is high-quality music reproduction while still having Bluetooth, ANC, USB-C DAC playback, multipoint connectivity, a premium build, and enough battery life for daily use, the Bathys remains a compelling headphone.

The most important thing to understand about the Bathys is that Focal did not simply try to make another mainstream ANC headphone. The company tried to bring some of its high-end audio philosophy into a wireless, portable product.

That creates both the Bathys' biggest strength and several of its biggest weaknesses.

YallaBuy Verdict

The Focal Bathys makes sense if:

  • You care about sound quality more than maximum ANC.
  • You listen to music critically rather than simply using headphones as background audio.
  • You want a more balanced and detailed presentation than many mainstream ANC headphones provide.
  • You want USB-C DAC playback.
  • You want Bluetooth multipoint.
  • You like physical controls.
  • You are comfortable paying a premium for audio quality.
  • You don't mind a relatively heavy headphone.
  • You are willing to accept that some competitors offer a more complete everyday feature set.

The Focal Bathys makes less sense if:

  • You mainly need headphones for airplanes and want the strongest possible ANC.
  • You make a lot of calls in noisy environments.
  • You want something extremely lightweight.
  • You want a highly polished smart-headphone ecosystem.
  • You want automatic features and extensive software customization.
  • You are looking for maximum value rather than maximum audio quality.
  • You are sensitive to headphone weight or headband pressure.
  • You expect a $600–$700+ headphone to be excellent at absolutely everything.

Our bottom line

The Bathys is not a “best for everyone” headphone.

It is better described as a music-first premium wireless headphone that happens to include ANC and modern wireless features.

That distinction matters.

If you buy it because you want the best noise cancelling, you may be disappointed.

If you buy it because you want the best possible combination of high-end sound + wireless convenience + USB DAC functionality, the Bathys becomes much more interesting.

1. What Is the Focal Bathys?

The Focal Bathys is a closed-back over-ear wireless headphone with active noise cancellation.

Focal designed it as a portable headphone for listeners who want something more serious than a conventional consumer ANC model.

It sits in an unusual part of the headphone market.

On one side, you have headphones from companies such as Sony and Bose that focus heavily on:

  • ANC
  • comfort
  • calls
  • battery life
  • smartphone integration
  • adaptive features
  • convenience

On the other side, you have traditional audiophile headphones that focus heavily on:

  • driver performance
  • tonal balance
  • detail
  • dynamics
  • imaging
  • amplification
  • wired listening

The Bathys attempts to sit between these two worlds.

It provides the convenience of a modern wireless headphone while retaining a strong emphasis on audio reproduction.

That is the basic idea behind the product.

And it is also the reason why evaluating the Bathys using only the usual “ANC headphone” checklist can lead to the wrong conclusion.

3. Design and Build Quality

The Bathys immediately looks different from many mainstream ANC headphones.

It doesn't try to disappear.

This is important because Focal has historically been a company associated with high-end audio equipment, and the Bathys carries some of that visual identity into a portable wireless product.

The earcups are relatively large, the construction combines different materials, and the overall appearance is more obviously “high-end audio” than “generic travel headphone.”

There is also the illuminated Focal logo.

Some people will love it.

Others will think it makes the headphone unnecessarily flashy.

That's a matter of taste.

But there is a bigger issue:

The Bathys is not a lightweight headphone.

Focal lists the weight at approximately 350 grams.

That's considerably heavier than some mainstream ANC competitors.

Weight doesn't automatically mean uncomfortable.

But it does mean that comfort becomes more dependent on your head shape, tolerance for pressure, and how long you wear the headphones.

This is one area where reviews disagree.

RTINGS describes the Bathys as very comfortable, while other testing has found that the weight and headband pressure can become noticeable during longer sessions.

That's exactly why YallaBuy would not describe the Bathys simply as “extremely comfortable” without qualification.

The more accurate conclusion is:

The Bathys can be comfortable for long listening sessions, but its relatively high weight means comfort is more individual than with some lighter competitors.

4. Comfort: One of the Most Important Things to Consider

Comfort is particularly important with expensive headphones.

You can EQ a headphone.

You can change the codec.

You can adjust ANC.

You cannot change the fact that a headphone is physically heavy on your head.

The Bathys uses relatively deep earcups and substantial padding.

For many listeners, this produces a comfortable fit.

The earcups provide enough space around the ears for a proper over-ear fit, which is preferable to having your ears pressed directly against the internal driver structure.

However, the headband can become more noticeable over time.

This creates an interesting contradiction in the Bathys reviews.

Some reviewers find it comfortable enough for long sessions.

Others experience pressure or fatigue after several hours.

That doesn't necessarily mean one group is wrong.

Headphone comfort is highly dependent on:

  • head size
  • head shape
  • hair
  • glasses
  • clamping force
  • ear size
  • preferred listening position
  • sensitivity to weight

Therefore, if you are buying the Bathys online without trying it first, this is one of the biggest things to keep in mind.

YallaBuy advice

If you know that heavy headphones bother you, do not buy the Bathys purely because its sound reviews are excellent.

A headphone that sounds fantastic but becomes physically uncomfortable after two hours isn't a good headphone for you.

5. The 40 mm Aluminium/Magnesium Drivers

This is where the Bathys becomes particularly interesting.

Focal uses 40 mm Aluminium/Magnesium drivers with the company's distinctive “M-shaped” dome design.

Focal says these drivers are designed and manufactured in France.

The driver technology is one of the reasons the Bathys is positioned differently from conventional wireless ANC headphones.

But don't fall into the marketing trap of assuming that exotic driver materials automatically guarantee better sound.

The real question is:

How does the headphone sound?

And this is where the Bathys generally performs very well.

6. Sound Quality: The Main Reason to Buy the Bathys

If there is one reason to consider the Bathys over a conventional flagship ANC headphone, it is sound quality.

Independent testing consistently identifies the Bathys as one of the stronger-sounding wireless ANC headphones.

RTINGS describes its sound reproduction as impressive and relatively balanced.

SoundGuys also places sound quality among its major strengths.

That doesn't mean everyone will prefer it.

Sound is subjective.

But there is a consistent theme across independent reviews:

The Bathys is designed more around accurate and detailed music reproduction than around a heavily consumer-oriented bass presentation.

7. Bass

The Bathys does not completely eliminate bass.

There is still enough low-end energy to make music enjoyable.

But the bass generally feels more controlled than exaggerated.

That distinction matters.

Some mainstream headphones intentionally emphasize bass because it makes the first few minutes of listening immediately exciting.

The Bathys takes a somewhat different approach.

Bass tends to serve the music rather than dominate it.

That can be excellent for:

  • acoustic music
  • jazz
  • classical
  • vocals
  • rock
  • electronic music
  • well-recorded pop

But if your favorite listening style is:

“Give me huge bass and make everything shake.”

the Bathys may not be your ideal choice.

You can adjust the sound using the app's EQ, but the underlying character remains different from a strongly bass-oriented headphone.

8. Midrange

The midrange is one of the Bathys' strongest areas.

Vocals and instruments can sound clear and well separated.

This is especially noticeable with recordings where several instruments occupy the same general frequency range.

A poorly tuned headphone can make these elements blend together.

The Bathys tends to preserve more separation.

This can make:

  • vocals easier to follow
  • guitars more distinct
  • piano more articulate
  • acoustic instruments more natural
  • complex arrangements easier to understand

For someone who listens to music actively rather than passively, this can be a major advantage.

9. Treble

The treble is more complicated.

The Bathys has plenty of detail and energy in the upper frequencies.

That contributes to its sense of clarity.

But additional treble energy can also make certain recordings sound brighter.

If a recording is already aggressive or poorly mastered, the Bathys may expose that.

This is not necessarily a flaw.

In fact, one argument in favor of a detailed headphone is that it doesn't hide weaknesses in the source material.

But listeners who prefer a smooth, warm and relaxed presentation may prefer a different headphone.

10. Detail Retrieval

This is another area where the Bathys earns its price.

The headphone can reveal small details in recordings that are easier to miss with more consumer-oriented headphones.

You may notice:

  • subtle background instruments
  • room information
  • guitar texture
  • vocal details
  • percussion attacks
  • separation between instruments

But this doesn't mean the Bathys magically creates information that wasn't in the recording.

The headphone is simply good at presenting the information that is there.

This is an important distinction.

11. Soundstage

The Bathys is a closed-back headphone.

Therefore, don't expect the huge open-back soundstage of a dedicated audiophile open-back model.

Still, the presentation can feel spacious for a closed wireless headphone.

Instrument placement is also one of its strengths.

The result is more engaging than simply having a flat wall of sound directly inside your head.

However, anyone buying the Bathys specifically because they want an enormous soundstage should adjust their expectations.

The Bathys is impressive for its category, not a replacement for a large open-back audiophile headphone.

12. Imaging and Instrument Separation

Imaging is another reason audiophile listeners may prefer the Bathys.

Good imaging helps you understand where sounds are positioned.

For example:

  • a guitar can occupy a clear location
  • vocals can remain centered
  • percussion can sit further toward the edges
  • background instruments can remain distinguishable

This becomes particularly valuable with complex music.

The Bathys' strong channel matching and generally consistent frequency response contribute to this.

For critical listening, this is more important than simply saying:

“The headphones sound detailed.”

13. USB-C DAC Mode: The Feature That Makes the Bathys Different

One of the Bathys' most interesting features is USB-C DAC playback.

Instead of using the headphone purely as a Bluetooth device, you can connect it directly to a compatible source using USB-C.

This allows the Bathys to operate as a digital audio device with its internal DAC and amplification.

Focal lists up to 42 hours of battery life in USB-DAC mode, compared with up to 30 hours through Bluetooth.

This is unusual in the mainstream ANC category.

It makes the Bathys particularly attractive to people who listen to:

  • lossless music
  • high-resolution music
  • music from a computer
  • local music libraries
  • high-quality streaming services

The USB DAC also makes the Bathys more versatile as a desktop headphone.

You can essentially use one headphone in several ways:

  • Bluetooth → everyday wireless listening
  • 3.5 mm → traditional wired connection
  • USB-C → digital audio/DAC mode

That versatility is one of the strongest arguments for the Bathys.

14. Is USB DAC Actually Useful?

Yes—but not for everyone.

If you mostly listen to Spotify through your phone over Bluetooth, you may not notice enough difference to justify buying the Bathys specifically for its DAC.

But if you use a computer as a major listening source, USB DAC becomes much more interesting.

It can also simplify your setup.

Instead of:

Computer → external DAC → amplifier → headphones

you can potentially use:

Computer → USB-C → Bathys

The Bathys handles the digital-to-analog conversion and amplification internally.

This is one reason the headphone appeals to listeners who want audiophile-oriented functionality without carrying a complete desktop audio chain.

15. Bluetooth Performance

The Bathys supports:

  • SBC
  • AAC
  • aptX
  • aptX Adaptive

Bluetooth 5.1 is also supported.

The inclusion of aptX Adaptive is particularly useful for compatible Android devices and other supported sources.

However, Bluetooth codecs are often misunderstood.

A higher-quality codec does not automatically make a headphone sound better in every situation.

The actual experience also depends on:

  • source device
  • operating system
  • codec implementation
  • signal quality
  • recording quality
  • headphone DSP
  • listener sensitivity

So don't buy the Bathys simply because you see “aptX Adaptive” on the specification sheet.

Buy it because the entire audio system is good.

16. Multipoint Connectivity

The Bathys supports Bluetooth multipoint.

This is a practical feature rather than an audiophile feature.

For example, you may connect:

  • phone
  • laptop

at the same time.

This makes the Bathys more useful for people who move between work and personal listening.

You can listen to music from your computer while remaining connected to your phone for calls.

This is one of those features that doesn't make the headphone sound better, but it makes living with it easier.

And that matters.

17. Active Noise Cancellation

This is where the Bathys becomes more complicated.

The Bathys has ANC.

But it is not a class-leading ANC headphone.

Independent testing has consistently found that its noise cancellation is good enough to be useful but not as strong as the best modern Sony, Bose, or Apple implementations.

SoundGuys found the low-frequency reduction meaningful and described it as roughly comparable to the Sennheiser Momentum 4 in its testing, while also noting that Sony and Apple outperform it.

RTINGS also reports strong overall noise isolation.

Other testing has been more critical.

The conclusion is therefore not:

“The Bathys has bad ANC.”

That would be inaccurate.

The better conclusion is:

“The Bathys has competent ANC, but ANC is not the reason to choose it over the competition.”

18. What Does That Mean in Real Life?

On:

  • trains
  • buses
  • offices
  • cafés
  • general city environments

the ANC can make a meaningful difference.

It reduces background noise and allows you to listen at a lower volume.

That's useful.

But if your primary goal is to sit on an airplane and eliminate as much engine rumble as possible, there are better choices.

Sony and Bose have invested heavily in ANC processing and microphone systems.

Their flagship models are designed specifically around this problem.

Focal's priorities are different.

That's the trade-off.

19. Transparency Mode

Transparency mode allows outside sound to pass through the headphones using the external microphones.

This is useful when you need to:

  • hear someone speaking
  • listen for an announcement
  • hear your surroundings
  • interact without removing the headphones

Independent reviews have generally viewed the Bathys' transparency mode positively.

But, as with almost all transparency systems, it is not identical to simply having nothing on your ears.

There can be an artificial quality to reproduced environmental sound.

Still, it is a useful feature and adds practical flexibility.

20. The Bathys Does Not Behave Like a Traditional Passive Headphone

This is an important detail that many buyers may overlook.

The Bathys is fundamentally an electronic headphone.

Its listening modes depend heavily on its internal electronics and DSP.

That means you shouldn't think of it as:

“A normal wired Focal headphone that also happens to have Bluetooth.”

It is more complicated than that.

The internal electronics are part of the headphone's intended sound and operation.

This is one of the compromises that comes with combining high-end audio and modern ANC technology.

21. Microphone and Call Quality

The Bathys includes eight microphones and Focal's Clear Voice Capture technology.

On paper, that sounds impressive.

In practice, microphone performance is acceptable to good depending on the environment, but it isn't the Bathys' strongest competitive advantage.

RTINGS describes the microphone as reasonable, while noting that the recorded voice isn't completely natural.

SoundGuys found it usable for calls and conferencing.

That distinction matters.

If you occasionally answer calls:

Bathys = fine.

If you spend hours every day in noisy conference calls:

There are better options.

This is another area where mainstream flagship ANC headphones can be more practical.

22. Gaming

The Bathys can work for casual gaming.

But it is not designed as a competitive gaming headset.

Wireless latency can be noticeable depending on the connection.

Using a wired connection can improve some aspects, but there are still limitations.

Another issue is microphone functionality depending on the connection.

RTINGS notes that microphone support isn't available through the analog connection, while USB connection can provide microphone functionality on compatible devices.

Therefore:

Casual gaming

Fine.

Story-driven gaming

Very good if you value sound quality.

Competitive gaming

Not our first recommendation.

Gaming + voice chat + maximum convenience

A dedicated gaming headset may make more sense.

23. Movies and TV

The Bathys can be excellent for movies because its strong separation and detailed presentation help dialogue and sound effects remain distinct.

But again, there are trade-offs.

If you care about:

  • spatial audio
  • head tracking
  • ecosystem integration
  • virtual surround features

some competitors may provide a more sophisticated experience.

The Bathys instead focuses more heavily on conventional high-quality stereo reproduction.

That isn't necessarily worse.

It's simply a different philosophy.

24. Battery Life

Focal rates the Bathys for approximately:

30 hours in Bluetooth mode

35 hours using the mini-jack

42 hours in USB DAC mode

These are strong numbers.

Battery life is not one of the Bathys' weaknesses.

In fact, battery performance is one of the more convincing parts of its overall package.

You can use it for multiple days without constantly thinking about charging.

Focal also supports quick charging.

That makes it practical for travel.

25. But Battery Life Isn't Everything

A buyer should never choose headphones based only on battery numbers.

Imagine two headphones:

Headphone A

  • 50 hours
  • average sound
  • excellent ANC

Headphone B

  • 30 hours
  • excellent sound
  • good ANC

The correct choice depends on the person.

For a frequent traveler, A might be better.

For someone who listens critically at home and in the office, B could be better.

The Bathys belongs firmly in the second category.

26. App and EQ

The Bathys works with Focal's companion software and provides EQ functionality.

This is important because it gives you some ability to adapt the sound to your preferences.

A listener who finds the default tuning slightly bright can make adjustments.

Someone who wants more bass can also increase the low end.

But don't expect an infinite sound-design system.

The app is primarily there to manage the headphone and adjust its sound rather than create an enormous ecosystem of smart features.

This is another area where Sony's software approach is more extensive.

Focal's philosophy remains relatively straightforward:

Make the headphone sound good first.

27. Physical Controls

The Bathys uses physical controls rather than relying entirely on touch gestures.

For some users, this is a major advantage.

Physical buttons provide tactile confirmation.

You don't need to remember exactly where on the earcup you should tap.

You can operate them while wearing gloves.

And you are less likely to trigger accidental commands.

The downside is that physical controls can make the design look less minimalist than touch-controlled headphones.

Again, this is preference rather than an objective flaw.

28. Portability

The Bathys includes a carrying case.

That's important because this is not a particularly small headphone.

The earcups are substantial and the overall design occupies more space than lightweight travel models.

If you travel frequently, the case is useful protection.

But don't expect the Bathys to disappear into a tiny bag.

This is a serious over-ear headphone.

29. Durability

The Bathys feels like a premium product.

The combination of materials, earcups, headband and metal components gives it a more substantial character than many plastic-heavy mainstream headphones.

However, “premium” does not mean indestructible.

A $700-class headphone is still a portable electronic device with:

  • hinges
  • batteries
  • microphones
  • moving components
  • leather or leather-like surfaces
  • internal electronics

Treating it carefully is important.

One particularly useful feature is that the ear pads are replaceable.

Focal's documentation explains that the pads can be removed and replaced using their clips.

That's a positive sign for long-term ownership.

  • Is the battery serviceable?
  • Does the software remain supported?
  • Is the construction robust?
  • Is the case included?
  • Are replacement parts available?

The replaceable ear pads are a positive.

That is good news for anyone intending to keep the headphone for years.

However, like all wireless headphones with built-in batteries, battery aging remains an unavoidable long-term consideration.
