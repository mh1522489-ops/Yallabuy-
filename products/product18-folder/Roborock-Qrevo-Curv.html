If you are looking for a robot vacuum that can do much more than simply pick up dust, the Roborock Qrevo Curv is one of the more interesting premium options to consider.

This robot vacuum is designed for people who want to automate as much floor cleaning as possible. It can vacuum, mop, return to its dock, empty its dustbin, refill its water, wash its mop pads with heated water, and dry them with warm air.

At $849/$1500, however, this is not a cheap robot vacuum. That means it needs to do more than just look impressive on paper. It needs to provide reliable everyday cleaning and reduce the amount of work you have to do.

The Qrevo Curv has some genuinely impressive strengths, especially on hard floors. Its FlexiArm system helps the robot clean closer to walls and corners, while its AdaptiLift chassis can help it move over relatively tall thresholds.

But it is not perfect.

Independent testing has found that its carpet performance is only adequate, its pet-hair performance on carpets can be disappointing, and its obstacle avoidance is not as reliable as you might expect from a premium robot vacuum.

So, is the Roborock Qrevo Curv worth $849?

Let's take a closer look.

Design and Build Quality

The Qrevo Curv has a modern, premium robot vacuum design.

Like many robot vacuums in this category, it uses a circular body designed to move around furniture and navigate different areas of your home.

One of the more interesting design features is the FlexiArm system.

Traditional round robot vacuums can struggle to clean directly against walls and in corners because their circular body leaves a small gap.

Roborock's FlexiArm design attempts to solve this problem by extending cleaning components beyond the main body.

This can help the robot reach areas that conventional circular robot vacuums may leave behind.

Independent testing found the Qrevo Curv's hard-floor pickup performance to be excellent, with the FlexiArm side brush helping it clean close to walls and corners.

That is one of the most useful features of this robot in real-world cleaning.

Vacuuming Performance

A robot vacuum can have a huge list of features, but none of them matter if it cannot actually clean your floors.

Fortunately, this is one area where the Qrevo Curv performs very well.

Hard Floors

If your home has hardwood, tile, laminate, vinyl, or other hard flooring, the Qrevo Curv is particularly impressive.

Independent testing found that it performs exceptionally well at picking up debris from bare floors.

It can handle different sizes of debris, while the FlexiArm side brush helps improve cleaning near walls and corners.

This makes the Qrevo Curv especially attractive for homes that are mostly made up of hard flooring.

For everyday messes such as:

  • Dust
  • Crumbs
  • Food particles
  • Dirt
  • Hair
  • Larger debris

the Qrevo Curv can be a very capable cleaner.

Carpet Performance

This is where the story becomes more complicated.

The Qrevo Curv can clean carpets, but it is not one of the strongest robot vacuums available for deep carpet cleaning.

Independent testing found that it can handle medium and large debris on carpets reasonably well, but it struggles more with fine particles embedded in carpet fibers.

This means the Qrevo Curv is better suited to homes with mostly hard floors and some carpeting than homes that are almost entirely carpeted.

If your house has thick carpets everywhere, you may still need a traditional vacuum cleaner from time to time.

That is not necessarily a deal-breaker.

Most robot vacuums have some limitations with deep carpet cleaning.

But at $849/1500, it is something buyers should know before purchasing.

Pet Hair Performance

If you have pets, this section is particularly important.

The Qrevo Curv can pick up pet hair from hard floors quite well.

However, its performance becomes less impressive when pet hair gets embedded deeply into carpets.

Independent testing found that the robot can remove hair sitting on top of carpet but has more difficulty pulling hair from deeper inside carpet fibers.

Therefore, I would not recommend buying the Qrevo Curv purely because you have a shedding dog or cat and expect it to completely replace your traditional vacuum.

It can certainly help reduce the amount of hair on your floors.

But homes with heavy shedding and lots of carpet may still need manual vacuuming.

Mopping Performance

Mopping is one of the strongest areas of the Qrevo Curv.

The robot uses rotating mop pads to scrub the floor instead of simply dragging a wet cloth behind it.

This can provide better cleaning for everyday stains.

The FlexiArm mop system also helps the robot reach closer to walls and corners.

Independent testing found the Qrevo Curv's stain-cleaning performance to be among its strongest characteristics.

This is particularly useful in kitchens, dining areas, and other places where food and drink spills are common.

The robot can also detect certain dirt situations and perform additional cleaning passes, although these features are not always perfectly consistent.

Heated Mop Washing

One of the biggest advantages of the Qrevo Curv is its multifunction dock.

After the robot finishes mopping, it can return to the dock and have its mop pads washed.

The dock uses heated water for mop cleaning, with the manufacturer specifying water temperatures up to approximately 75°C / 167°F for the washing process.

After washing, the mop pads are dried using warm air.

This matters because leaving wet mop pads sitting inside a dock can create unpleasant odors and hygiene problems.

The drying system helps reduce that problem.

Automatic Dust Emptying

Another useful feature is automatic dustbin emptying.

When the robot returns to its dock, the station can transfer collected dirt from the robot into a larger dust bag.

This means you don't have to manually empty the robot after every cleaning cycle.

For people with busy schedules, this is one of the features that can make a premium robot vacuum genuinely convenient.

You will still need to replace the dust bag eventually, but you should not have to think about the robot's small dustbin after every run.

Automatic Water Management

The dock also helps reduce the amount of manual work involved with mopping.

It can refill the robot's clean-water supply and collect dirty water after cleaning.

That means the robot can handle multiple cleaning cycles without requiring you to refill its internal tank every time.

However, the dock itself still requires maintenance.

You will eventually need to:

  • Refill the clean-water tank
  • Empty the dirty-water tank
  • Replace the dust bag
  • Clean parts of the dock
  • Maintain the mop system

So "maintenance-free" would be an inaccurate description.

It is better to think of the Qrevo Curv as low-maintenance rather than maintenance-free.

AdaptiLift Chassis

Another interesting feature is Roborock's AdaptiLift system.

The chassis can adjust the height of the robot's wheels to help it move over thresholds and other obstacles.

Roborock advertises the system as being capable of handling thresholds up to around 4 cm.

Independent testing found that the robot could clear thresholds around 3.8 cm in testing.

This can be useful in homes where different rooms have raised thresholds.

A robot that gets stuck at a doorway is not very useful, so this feature can make a real difference in certain homes.

However, performance can vary depending on the surface and situation.

Navigation and Mapping

The Qrevo Curv uses LiDAR-based navigation to map your home.

This allows it to understand the layout of rooms and plan cleaning routes.

The Roborock app also gives you a large amount of control over how the robot cleans.

You can customize cleaning preferences, select rooms, choose cleaning modes, adjust suction power, and configure mopping settings.

This level of control is one of Roborock's strengths.

However, navigation is not perfect.

Independent testing found that the robot's obstacle avoidance can be inconsistent.

Obstacle Avoidance

This is one of the biggest weaknesses I would mention in an honest review.

The Qrevo Curv has cameras and sensors designed to identify obstacles.

However, independent testing found that it does not always recognize smaller objects such as socks and power cords reliably.

It can sometimes bump into objects or even run over things left on the floor.

This means you should still prepare your floor before running the robot.

For example, don't assume that the robot will always recognize:

  • Charging cables
  • Small socks
  • Toys
  • Small objects
  • Pet-related messes

The more organized your floor is, the better your experience will probably be.

App and Smart Features

The Roborock app is another strong part of the overall experience.

Instead of simply pressing a button and letting the robot run, you can customize how it cleans your home.

Depending on your setup, you can control things such as:

  • Rooms
  • Cleaning schedules
  • Suction levels
  • Mop water levels
  • Cleaning routes
  • Carpet behavior
  • Cleaning intensity

The Qrevo Curv also supports different carpet cleaning options.

For example, you can choose to clean carpets first, avoid carpets, or use deeper carpet-cleaning settings.

This level of customization is useful for homes with mixed flooring.

Noise

Nobody wants a robot vacuum that sounds like a traditional vacuum cleaner running constantly.

Fortunately, the Qrevo Curv is relatively quiet during normal operation.

Independent testing described the robot itself as remarkably quiet, although the docking station can become considerably louder when it empties the dustbin.

So you may barely notice the robot during normal cleaning, but the automatic emptying process can be much louder.

  2. Very good mopping — The rotating mop pads and FlexiArm mop make it a strong choice for hard-floor mopping.
  3. Highly automated dock — The dock can empty dust, refill water, collect dirty water, wash mop pads, and dry them.
  4. Heated mop washing — The dock uses heated water to clean the mop pads, helping improve the cleaning process.

Cons of the Roborock Qrevo Curv

  1. Carpet cleaning is only average — It can handle larger debris on carpets, but fine debris embedded in carpet fibers can be difficult for it.

  5. The dock still needs maintenance — Although the dock automates many tasks, you still need to empty tanks, replace bags, and clean components.

The Qrevo Curv makes the most sense for certain types of buyers.

It is a good choice if:

You have mostly hard floors.

You want strong mopping performance.

If mopping is important to you, the Qrevo Curv is much more attractive than a basic robot vacuum.

You hate cleaning the robot manually.

The multifunction dock removes much of the repetitive maintenance.

You have multiple rooms with thresholds.

The AdaptiLift chassis can be helpful in homes with raised transitions between rooms.

You want an advanced app.

Roborock provides extensive customization through its software.

Who Should NOT Buy the Roborock Qrevo Curv?

Despite its impressive feature list, this robot is not for everyone.

You may want another option if:

Your home is mostly thick carpet.

The Qrevo Curv's carpet performance is not strong enough for me to recommend it primarily as a deep-carpet cleaner.

You have a lot of pet hair embedded in carpet.

The robot can struggle with hair buried in carpet fibers.

Your floor is usually covered with cables and small objects.

The obstacle avoidance system is not reliable enough to assume the robot will avoid everything.

You have very low furniture.

The robot may not fit underneath some couches, cabinets, or other furniture because of its height.

You want the cheapest robot vacuum possible.

At $849/1500, the Qrevo Curv is clearly aimed at people who value automation and convenience more than simply getting the lowest possible price.

Is the Roborock Qrevo Curv Worth $849/$1500?

This is the most important question.

At $849/1500, I would not call the Qrevo Curv a perfect robot vacuum.

It has real weaknesses.

The carpet performance is not outstanding, pet hair embedded in carpets can be difficult, and the obstacle avoidance system could be more reliable.

But that does not mean it is a bad purchase.

In fact, for the right home, it can be an excellent robot vacuum.

If your home has mostly hardwood, tile, laminate, or other hard flooring, the Qrevo Curv becomes much more attractive.

Its excellent hard-floor pickup, strong mopping performance, FlexiArm system, automated dock, heated mop washing, and customizable cleaning options create a very convenient package.

The important thing is to buy it for the right reasons.

Don't buy it simply because it has a high suction number or a long list of smart features.

Buy it because you want automated everyday floor cleaning and because your home's flooring matches what the robot does best.

Roborock Qrevo Curv vs. a Traditional Vacuum

A robot vacuum will never completely replace every type of vacuum cleaner.

A traditional vacuum is still better for:

  • Stairs
  • Furniture
  • Tight corners
  • Deep carpet cleaning
  • Cars
  • Upholstery
  • Areas the robot cannot reach

What the Qrevo Curv offers is convenience.

You can let it handle routine floor cleaning while using a traditional vacuum occasionally for areas that need deeper or more specialized cleaning.

For many people, that combination makes more sense than expecting one machine to do everything.

Final Verdict

The Roborock Qrevo Curv is a premium robot vacuum with a strong focus on automation.

At $849/1500, it is expensive, but it provides a feature set that goes far beyond basic robot vacuuming.

Its strongest areas are hard-floor cleaning, mopping, automated maintenance, and cleaning close to walls and corners.

Its weaknesses are mainly carpet performance, embedded pet hair, and inconsistent obstacle avoidance. Independent testing supports this mixed picture: the Curv performs exceptionally well on bare floors and stains, while its carpet and obstacle performance are less impressive.

My overall take

Buy it if your home is mostly hard floors and you want maximum automation.

Think twice if your home is mostly carpet or you have lots of pet hair embedded in carpets.

At $849/1500, the Qrevo Curv should not be treated as a perfect robot vacuum.

It is better described as a highly automated premium cleaner that performs particularly well on hard floors and mopping, but has some important limitations.

And that honest distinction is important when you're spending this much money.

Final Rating: 4/5

For the right home, the Roborock Qrevo Curv can dramatically reduce the amount of routine floor cleaning you have to do.

For homes dominated by hard flooring, it is one of the more compelling options in its category.

But if you need exceptional deep-carpet cleaning or flawless obstacle avoidance, you should consider other models before making the purchase.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to the most common questions about the Roborock Qrevo Curv — based on the official spec sheet, independent testing references, and how the robot performs in real homes.

Is the Roborock Qrevo Curv worth $849/1500?

For buyers who want a premium, highly automated robot vacuum and mop with excellent hard-floor performance, the Qrevo Curv is a strong option. It is not ideal as a deep-carpet cleaner, for very cluttered homes, or for buyers who need class-leading obstacle avoidance at this price point, because independent testing found only adequate carpet performance and inconsistent object recognition.

How well does the Roborock Qrevo Curv clean hard floors?

It is excellent on hard floors. Independent testing found exceptional hard-floor pickup across different debris sizes, and the FlexiArm side brush helps it reach closer to walls and corners than many traditional round robot vacuums.

What is the FlexiArm system?

FlexiArm is Roborock's extendable cleaning system. It allows both the side brush and the mop pad to extend beyond the robot's main body to reach areas that traditional circular robot vacuums typically miss, such as corners, edges, and around furniture legs.

What is the AdaptiLift chassis?

AdaptiLift is Roborock's adjustable chassis system that lets the robot lift its wheels to clear raised thresholds. Roborock advertises the ability to handle thresholds up to around 4 cm, and independent testing found it could clear around 3.8 cm.

Is the Qrevo Curv good for pet hair?

It picks up pet hair from hard floors quite well, but independent testing found it can struggle with hair embedded in carpet fibers. Homes with heavy shedding and lots of carpet will likely still need occasional manual vacuuming.

How well does the obstacle avoidance work?

The Qrevo Curv uses camera-based obstacle detection, but independent testing has found that it does not always reliably recognize smaller objects such as socks and power cables. It can bump into items or run over them, so tidying the floor before a run is still recommended.

Does the dock wash and dry the mop pads?

Yes. The multifunction dock can wash the mop pads with heated water (up to around 75°C / 167°F according to Roborock) and then dry them with warm air. It also auto-empties the dustbin, refills clean water, and collects dirty water.

What maintenance does the Qrevo Curv still need?

Even with the automated dock, you will still need to refill the clean-water tank, empty the dirty-water tank, replace the dust bag, clean the dock occasionally, and check the mop system. The Qrevo Curv is best described as low-maintenance rather than maintenance-free.
