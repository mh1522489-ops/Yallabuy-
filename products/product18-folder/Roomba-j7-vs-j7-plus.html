If you are looking for a smart robot vacuum that can clean your floors without needing your attention all the time, the iRobot Roomba j7 and Roomba j7+ are two models that are still worth considering.

At first look, the two robots seem almost identical. They have the same basic robot design, the same cleaning system, the same smart navigation technology, the same obstacle detection, and many of the same software features.

So, what is the real difference?

The Roomba j7+ comes with a Clean Base automatic dirt disposal station, while the standard Roomba j7 comes with a normal charging station.

That small difference can make a surprisingly big difference in everyday use.

The j7 is cheaper and gives you most of the important smart features. The j7+ costs more, but it can automatically empty its dustbin into the Clean Base, meaning you do not need to manually empty the robot after every few cleaning runs.

But is the j7+ actually worth paying more for?

And which one is better for homes with pets, carpets, children, furniture, cables, and everyday mess?

In this detailed Roomba j7 vs j7+ comparison, we will look at almost everything you need to know before buying either robot, including cleaning performance, navigation, obstacle avoidance, pet hair, carpets, hard floors, battery life, maintenance, smart features, advantages, disadvantages, and who should or should not buy each model.

They are fundamentally the same cleaning platform.

The real question is: Do you want automatic dirt disposal or not?

What Is the Roomba j7?

The Roomba j7 is a smart robot vacuum designed around one major idea: the robot should understand the environment around it instead of blindly driving around your home.

It uses a camera-based navigation and obstacle recognition system called PrecisionVision Navigation.

This allows the robot to identify objects such as cables, shoes and pet waste and react to them.

That is important because one of the biggest problems with older robot vacuums was not necessarily cleaning performance.

It was getting stuck.

A robot could start cleaning while you were away, encounter a charging cable, sock, toy or another object, and then spend the rest of the cleaning cycle sitting there waiting for help.

The j7 was designed to reduce that problem.

iRobot says the j7/j7+ can recognize obstacles and send an image to the app so the user can decide how the robot should handle the object in the future.

The standard j7 comes with a Home Base charging station rather than the automatic dirt disposal station.

This means that when the dustbin becomes full, you need to empty it yourself.

For some people, this is completely fine.

For others, especially people with pets or large homes, it can become annoying.

What Is the Roomba j7+?

The Roomba j7+ is essentially the j7 with a much more convenient charging station.

Instead of simply charging the robot, the Clean Base Automatic Dirt Disposal also collects the dirt from the robot's internal dustbin.

After the robot finishes cleaning, it returns to the Clean Base.

The station then automatically pulls the dirt and debris from the robot into a disposable bag.

iRobot says the Clean Base can hold approximately 60 bins worth of dirt, dust and hair, allowing you to go weeks without manually emptying the robot.

That is the biggest advantage of the j7+.

The robot itself is not dramatically more powerful than the j7.

The j7+ is simply more autonomous.

And that difference matters more than it may sound.

The Biggest Difference: Self-Emptying

Let's imagine two people.

Person A runs the robot three times per week.

Person B runs the robot every day because they have pets and want to keep hair under control.

For Person A, manually emptying the dustbin may not be a big deal.

For Person B, it can become another small household task.

This is where the j7+ makes more sense.

The Clean Base automatically empties the robot after cleaning.

According to iRobot, the bags can hold up to 60 bins of dirt and debris.

That does not mean you will always get exactly 60 cleaning sessions.

The actual time depends on how much dirt, dust and hair your home produces.

A home with two dogs will fill the bag much faster than a small apartment with one person.

But the concept is still useful.

You clean the robot's brushes and sensors when needed, while the Clean Base takes care of the daily dustbin emptying.

Can You Turn the Roomba j7 Into a j7+?

This is one of the most useful things to know about the comparison.

The standard j7 can be paired with a compatible Clean Base Automatic Dirt Disposal system.

iRobot specifically states that the Clean Base is sold separately for the j7 and is compatible with j and i series products.

So buying the j7 does not necessarily mean you are permanently giving up the self-emptying feature.

You can potentially buy the compatible Clean Base later.

However, this changes the value calculation.

If you already know that you want automatic dirt disposal, buying a j7 and then purchasing a Clean Base separately may not be the best financial decision.

In that situation, comparing the total cost of the j7 plus the Clean Base against the price of a j7+ package makes more sense.

Cleaning Performance

Now we reach one of the most important parts of this comparison.

Does the j7 clean better than the j7+?

Not really.

Because the two robots use the same fundamental cleaning system, you should not buy the j7+ expecting dramatically stronger vacuuming performance.

Both use iRobot's three-stage cleaning system, including:

  • Dual Multi-Surface Rubber Brushes
  • Edge-Sweeping Brush
  • Dirt Detect technology
  • Automatic cleaning patterns
  • Smart navigation

iRobot describes the system as providing 10x the Power-Lifting Suction compared with the Roomba 600 Series cleaning system.

The important thing here is that "10x" is a comparison with the older 600 Series system.

It should not be interpreted as meaning the j7 has 10 times the suction power of every other modern robot vacuum.

That distinction is important when comparing robot vacuums.

Dual Multi-Surface Rubber Brushes

One of the best parts of the j7 platform is its dual rubber brush system.

Instead of using traditional bristle brushes, the Roomba j7 uses two rubber rollers designed to work across different floor types.

These rollers move in coordination to collect dirt, dust, crumbs and hair.

They also have an advantage for pet owners because hair is generally less likely to become deeply tangled than with traditional bristle-style rollers.

However, "less likely to tangle" does not mean "never needs maintenance."

Long human hair and pet hair can still collect around the rollers and their ends.

Independent testing has found that hair can still accumulate around the brushroll area, so occasional maintenance remains necessary.

That is normal for robot vacuums.

No matter how advanced the robot is, it still needs basic maintenance.

Hard Floor Cleaning

The Roomba j7 is a strong choice for homes with hardwood, laminate, tile and other hard flooring.

Its dual rubber rollers can pick up everyday debris such as:

  • Dust
  • Crumbs
  • Pet hair
  • Small food particles
  • Dirt
  • Cat litter

The robot also has an edge-sweeping brush designed to help collect debris close to walls and corners.

However, you should not expect it to perfectly replace a powerful traditional vacuum for every type of heavy mess.

If someone drops a large amount of flour, cereal or other debris on the floor, a normal vacuum may still be faster.

The Roomba j7 is best understood as a maintenance cleaner.

Run it frequently and it helps prevent dirt from building up.

That is where robot vacuums are most useful.

Carpet Cleaning

Carpet is a more complicated story.

The j7 can clean carpets and rugs, but its performance depends on carpet thickness, debris type and how much dirt is present.

Independent testing has shown that the j7+ can perform well across different surfaces, although its performance is not perfect for every type of debris.

It is especially useful when you run it regularly.

If your carpet receives small amounts of dust and hair every day, frequent robot cleaning can make a noticeable difference.

But if your carpet is very thick and heavily dirty, a full-size upright vacuum can still have an advantage.

This is one reason I would not recommend buying the j7 specifically because you expect it to replace a powerful traditional vacuum.

Think of it as an automated everyday cleaner.

Pet Hair Performance

Pet owners are one of the main groups that can benefit from the Roomba j7.

The rubber rollers are designed to deal with hair better than many traditional brush designs.

The robot also has one major feature that is particularly interesting for pet owners: pet waste avoidance.

This is actually more important than it sounds.

Imagine leaving your home and telling the robot to clean while your dog or cat is inside.

If there is an accident on the floor, a normal robot vacuum could drive directly through it.

The result could be extremely unpleasant.

The j7 uses its camera-based PrecisionVision system to recognize hazards such as pet waste and avoid them. iRobot even offers its P.O.O.P. — Pet Owner Official Promise — for qualifying solid cat or dog waste under its stated terms.

This is one of the biggest reasons to choose the j7 platform over an older robot.

But Is the j7 Perfect for Pet Hair?

No.

This is where an honest review needs to be realistic.

The j7 is good for routine pet-hair cleaning, but it is not automatically the best robot vacuum for every pet owner.

Independent testing has found that its pet-hair pickup can be only moderate on some carpets. Tom's Guide, for example, recorded a pet-hair score of 82.5 in its testing of the j7+.

So if your main priority is removing huge amounts of pet hair from thick carpet, you should compare the j7 against newer robot vacuums before buying.

But if your priority is:

"I want my robot to clean regularly and avoid my pet's accidents and random objects."

The j7 becomes much more attractive.

Obstacle Avoidance

This is arguably the biggest reason to buy a j7 or j7+.

The robot uses a front-facing camera and software to identify objects in its path.

Instead of simply following a map and hoping nothing is there, it can react to objects it sees.

The system can identify things such as:

  • Shoes
  • Cables
  • Pet waste
  • Other household obstacles

When the robot encounters an object, the app can show an image and ask you what should happen with that obstacle in the future.

This creates a more intelligent cleaning experience.

Why Obstacle Avoidance Matters So Much

Robot vacuum marketing often focuses on suction power.

But in everyday life, navigation can be just as important.

A robot with excellent suction is not very useful if it constantly gets stuck.

Imagine a living room with phone chargers, shoes, children's toys, pet bowls, cables, and furniture.

An older robot may repeatedly run into these objects.

The j7 is designed to recognize many of them and change its behavior.

Independent testing has found the j7+'s obstacle avoidance to be one of its strongest features. RTINGS describes the system as highly consistent at identifying hazards, especially on bare floors.

However, there is a trade-off.

The robot can sometimes keep a relatively large distance from an object to make sure it does not touch it.

That means you may get a small uncleaned area around some objects.

RTINGS specifically notes this conservative behavior as one limitation of the camera-based obstacle avoidance system.

So: better safety can sometimes mean slightly less coverage around obstacles. That is a reasonable trade-off for many homes.

Navigation and Smart Mapping

The j7 and j7+ also support iRobot's smart mapping features.

The robot can learn the layout of your home and create maps that allow you to control where it cleans.

For example, you can create rooms and tell the robot to clean:

  • Kitchen
  • Living room
  • Bedroom
  • Hallway

You can also create keep-out areas when necessary.

This makes the j7 much more practical than a basic robot that simply moves randomly around the house.

App Experience

The iRobot app is one of the most important parts of owning the j7.

From the app, you can control cleaning schedules, rooms and other settings.

You can also receive notifications when the robot encounters objects.

This is especially useful when the robot is running while you are away.

Instead of wondering why the robot stopped, the app can help you understand what happened.

For people who enjoy smart-home technology, this is one of the strongest aspects of the j7 platform.

Automatic Recharge and Resume

Another useful feature is automatic recharge and resume.

If the robot does not have enough battery to complete a cleaning job, it can return to the charging station.

After charging, it can continue the cleaning job from where it stopped.

iRobot describes this as Smart Recharge and Resume.

This is particularly useful in larger homes.

You do not have to manually find the robot and restart it.

Battery Life

Battery life is one area where the j7 is not class-leading by today's standards.

Some testing sources report around 75 minutes of runtime under their testing conditions, while real-world results can vary depending on floor type, navigation, obstacles and cleaning conditions.

This is important because robot-vacuum battery claims can be misleading.

A robot may have a certain theoretical runtime, but actual cleaning time depends on what it is doing.

If it has to navigate around many obstacles, travel between rooms and make complicated movements, the cleaning session can behave differently.

The good news is that the j7 does not necessarily need to clean your entire home on one charge.

The recharge-and-resume system is designed to handle longer cleaning jobs.

Design and Size

The j7 and j7+ robot itself is relatively compact.

According to iRobot, the robot measures approximately 13.3 inches wide × 3.4 inches high.

It weighs approximately 7.49 pounds.

The 3.4-inch height is useful because it allows the robot to travel under many sofas, beds and cabinets.

But before buying, measure the clearance under your furniture.

A few centimeters can make a big difference.

Who Should NOT Buy the Roomba j7?

The j7 may not be the best choice if:

  • You hate emptying robot dustbins.
  • You have several pets.
  • Your home gets dirty very quickly.
  • You want completely hands-off cleaning.
  • You have a very large house.
  • You expect class-leading deep carpet cleaning.
  • You want the newest robot vacuum technology available in 2026.

That last point is important. The j7 is an older generation of robot vacuum technology. It can still be useful, but newer competitors have introduced stronger navigation, higher suction ratings and more advanced docking systems. So the j7 makes the most sense when its price is attractive.

Who Should NOT Buy the Roomba j7+?

You probably do not need the j7+ if:

  • You live in a small apartment.
  • You rarely use the robot.
  • You don't mind manually emptying the dustbin.
  • You are trying to spend as little as possible.
  • You already own a compatible Clean Base.
  • You want the newest and most powerful robot vacuum technology.

In those cases, the standard j7 may provide better value.

Which One Is Better for Apartments?

For a small apartment, the standard j7 may make more sense.

Why? Because the dustbin may not fill as quickly, and you may not mind emptying it.

If you only clean a few times per week, buying the j7+ primarily for self-emptying may not provide enough additional value.

Best choice: Roomba j7 — especially if it is available at a noticeably lower price.

Which One Is Better for Large Homes?

The j7+ makes more sense for a larger home.

A larger home usually means more floor area, more dust, more hair, more cleaning sessions, and more frequent dustbin emptying.

The Clean Base reduces the amount of manual work.

Best choice: Roomba j7+

Which One Is Better for Homes With Pets?

Both are strong choices.

If your main concern is avoiding pet waste, both models have the same important obstacle-avoidance technology.

If your concern is convenience, however, the j7+ is better.

Best choice: Roomba j7+

Which One Is Better for Families With Children?

I would also lean toward the j7+.

Children often leave things on the floor: toys, socks, shoes, cables, small objects.

The j7 platform is designed to recognize many obstacles rather than simply driving into everything.

The self-emptying feature also means less daily maintenance.

Best choice: Roomba j7+

Final Verdict: Which One Should You Actually Buy?

This is where the comparison becomes very simple.

If the two robots are close in price: buy the j7+. The automatic dirt disposal is worth having.

If the j7 is significantly cheaper: buy the j7. You get almost all of the important robot features without paying for the Clean Base.

If you have pets: I would lean toward the j7+, not because it cleans dramatically better, but because pet owners benefit from frequent cleaning and automatic emptying.

If you have a small apartment: I would lean toward the j7. You probably do not need the larger self-emptying station.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to the most common questions about the Roomba j7 and Roomba j7+ — based on iRobot's official specifications, independent testing references, and how the robots perform in real homes.

Is the Roomba j7+ better than the j7?

The j7+ is more convenient, but it is not dramatically better at vacuuming. Both use the same core robot cleaning and obstacle-avoidance technology. The main difference is the included Clean Base automatic dirt disposal system.

Does the Roomba j7 avoid pet poop?

Yes. The j7 and j7+ use PrecisionVision Navigation to identify and avoid hazards including solid cat or dog waste. iRobot also offers its P.O.O.P. promise under specific terms.

Does the Roomba j7+ empty itself?

Yes. The j7+ returns to its Clean Base and automatically transfers dirt from the robot into the disposal bag.

How long can the j7+ go without emptying?

iRobot says the Clean Base can hold up to approximately 60 bins of dirt and debris. Actual time varies depending on how dirty your home is and how often the robot runs.

Can I add a Clean Base to the Roomba j7?

Yes. iRobot states that the Clean Base is sold separately for the j7 and is compatible with j-series products.

Is the Roomba j7 good for pet hair?

It is good for regular pet-hair maintenance, particularly because of its dual rubber brushes. However, independent testing shows that its pet-hair performance on carpet is not class-leading.

Is the Roomba j7 good for carpets?

Yes, it can clean carpets, but it is better viewed as an everyday maintenance robot rather than a replacement for a powerful upright vacuum on very thick or heavily soiled carpet.

Is the Roomba j7+ noisy?

The robot itself is not necessarily the biggest issue. The Clean Base can be quite loud for the short period when it automatically empties the robot. Tom's Guide lists the loud Clean Base as one of the j7+'s drawbacks.

Does the j7 have smart mapping?

Yes. The j7 supports iRobot's smart mapping system and can learn your home's layout.

Does the j7+ have stronger suction than the j7?

Not in the way most buyers would expect. The j7 and j7+ share the same core cleaning platform. The main upgrade in the j7+ is the Clean Base rather than a fundamentally different vacuum motor.

Which one should I buy?

If you want the lowest cost and don't mind emptying the bin, choose the Roomba j7. If you want less maintenance and automatic dirt disposal, choose the Roomba j7+. For most homes with pets or frequent cleaning, the j7+ is the more convenient choice.
