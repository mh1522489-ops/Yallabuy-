Bose QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds (2nd Gen) Review: Still the Noise-Cancelling King?

Class-leading ANC, rich sound, and wireless charging now included — but the 6-hour battery remains unchanged. Our full independent analysis.

Mahmoud Hamdy August 2026 12 min read
Quick Verdict

The Bose QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds (2nd Gen) are the refreshed version of Bose's flagship earbuds, launched in September 2025 at $299 — the same price as the original model. According to multiple independent testing outlets, including TechRadar, CNN Underscored, and TechGearLab, the standout strength is active noise cancellation (ANC), which TechRadar described as the best in the earbuds category at the time of its review. On the other hand, battery life remains limited (6 hours from the buds themselves), and the price sits at the premium end of the market.

What Makes This Product Different?

The 2nd Gen isn't a radical redesign or a sonic overhaul — it's a set of practical, everyday-use improvements. According to Bose's official product page, the key additions are:

  • SpeechClarity: an AI-based technology that filters out surrounding noise and wind during calls.
  • ActiveSense: an adaptive version of Aware Mode that detects sudden loud sounds and applies noise cancellation proportional to their loudness and duration, without fully drowning out music.
  • Cinema Mode: a new preset using Bose's TrueSpatial technology to turn standard stereo audio into a more theater-like experience for video content.
  • Default wireless charging: a feature missing from some 1st Gen configurations, now standard on the case in this generation, per PhoneArena.
  • A new wax guard to help protect the earbuds from damage, per PhoneArena.

These improvements amount to an incremental update rather than a generational leap — a view echoed by multiple independent reviews, including SoundGuys, which called the refresh "somewhat unnecessary" for existing 1st Gen owners.

Main Features

Active Noise Cancellation (ANC)

What is it? A system that uses internal and external microphones to detect ambient noise and generate an inverse sound wave to cancel it out.

How does it work? Bose uses an updated algorithm that provides smoother transitions between noise-cancellation levels when sudden sounds occur, according to Bose's own description.

Why does it matter? It's the single feature most consistently praised across independent reviews. TechRadar rated it the best in its class at the time of testing, and CNN Underscored described it as capable of nearly "silencing" the surrounding environment in loud settings.

Limitations: There is no full "off" switch for noise cancellation — the lowest setting is the minimum achievable via the app, per TechGearLab. One user (an individual review on Adorama) reported that sudden loud sounds could produce a jarring, "gunshot-like" artifact — an isolated observation not echoed with the same severity in major independent reviews.

Sound Quality and CustomTune

What is it? A proprietary Bose calibration technology that tailors audio playback to the shape of each user's ear canal.

How does it work? The earbuds play a calibration tone at every power-on while worn, measuring how the ear's shape modifies that sound and adjusting audio and noise cancellation accordingly, per Bose's official description.

Why does it matter? It's one reason multiple reviews (TechRadar, CNN Underscored) described the sound as "rich" and "detailed."

Limitations: RecordingNow described the sound quality as "good but not great" relative to competitors, and TechGearLab noted a clear bass lean that won't suit every listener. SoundGuys also felt the app's EQ tools were limited for anyone wanting deeper customization.

Wireless Charging and Battery Life

What is it? A feature that lets the charging case be topped up on any Qi-compatible wireless charging pad, without needing a USB-C cable every time.

How does it work? The case itself includes internal wireless charging coils, now available by default in this generation, per PhoneArena.

Why does it matter? It was one of the most-requested features from 1st Gen owners, per Ecoustics and PhoneArena.

Limitations: Battery life from the buds themselves is unchanged at 6 hours — a point criticized by multiple independent sources, including TechGearLab and CNN Underscored, as insufficient for long, uninterrupted listening sessions without returning to the case.

Performance (According to Independent Testing)

YallaBuy does not own a test unit of this product. The following is a summary of results published by specialist outlets.
  • HotHardware ran a real-world usage test and recorded actual battery life of just over 5 hours — close to the official 6-hour figure.
  • CNN Underscored tested noise cancellation in genuinely loud environments (subway, busy office) and described the results as among the best the team has experienced in the compact-earbud category.
  • Mark Ellis Reviews tested call quality against the AirPods Pro 2 and found improvements present, but not enough to surpass Apple's clarity in noisy environments.
  • RecordingNow compared ANC between the 1st and 2nd Gen "back to back" and found only a minor edge for the 2nd Gen in consistency and smoothness — not enough of a difference to justify an upgrade on its own.

Real-World Experience

Based on user reports on platforms like Adorama and Slickdeals, feedback splits between strong overall satisfaction with comfort and sound quality, and scattered individual complaints. Notable recurring patterns include:

  • Numerous five-star reviews praising comfort and secure fit during exercise and daily use.
  • Some users reported difficulty finding the right ear-tip size, consistent with WhatHi-Fi's note about the limited three-size selection.
  • At least one individual report mentioned pairing difficulty with an Android phone.
  • Scattered reports on the Slickdeals forum mentioned static noise specifically in the left earbud, echoed by more than one user across both the 1st and 2nd Gen — but the sample size is small and doesn't currently rise to the level of a confirmed, widespread issue based on available evidence. Anyone concerned about this should check the retailer's return policy before purchasing.

Problems & Limitations

  • Battery life from the buds alone hasn't improved over the previous generation — the most frequently repeated criticism across independent reviews.
  • No option to fully disable noise cancellation.
  • A noticeably larger physical footprint than some competitors, like the AirPods Pro.
  • No LDAC codec support, per TechRadar — relevant specifically to Android users focused on high-resolution audio.
  • Some independent reviews (SoundGuys) questioned whether the upgrade is even necessary for existing 1st Gen owners, given how minor the day-to-day differences are.

Who Should Buy It?

✓ Ideal For

  • Anyone who spends significant time in loud environments (transit, open offices, flights) and wants the highest achievable level of sound isolation
  • Listeners who prefer rich, bass-forward sound and don't mind limited EQ customization
  • Anyone who wants built-in wireless charging without needing a cable every time
  • Smartphone users (iPhone or Android) who want reliable multipoint connectivity

✗ Not Ideal For

  • Anyone who needs several continuous hours of listening without returning to the case — 6 hours of continuous use may not be enough for some users
  • Existing 1st Gen QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds owners who don't specifically need default wireless charging or the minor call-quality improvements, since the practical difference is limited according to multiple independent reviews
  • Android users specifically focused on LDAC high-resolution audio support
  • Budget-conscious buyers looking for the lowest possible price in the good-ANC category, since cheaper alternatives exist that offer comparable performance in some respects

Alternatives

  • Apple AirPods Pro 3: A closer-priced alternative (around $249 at launch) with features exclusive to iPhone users, such as live translation, and Apple's own claims of major ANC improvements (a manufacturer claim not yet independently verified in full by YallaBuy). A strong choice for anyone who values deep integration with Apple's ecosystem.
  • Sony WF-1000XM5: A well-known competitor known for balanced sound, though some reviews, including RecordingNow, rated its comfort lower than Bose's.
  • Sennheiser Momentum True Wireless 4: A relatively cheaper option with solid sound quality, but without the same level of noise cancellation according to RecordingNow's comparisons.
  • Bose QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds (1st Gen): Per SoundGuys, this may be a more cost-effective choice for buyers who don't specifically need default wireless charging or the minor call-quality improvements in the 2nd Gen, especially if found at a discount.

Value for Money

At $299 at launch, these earbuds sit in the premium tier of the wireless earbuds market. The main justification for that price — according to nearly every independent review referenced in this report — is noise cancellation quality, which genuinely is among the best currently available. On the other hand, the practical gap versus the 1st Gen is narrow, and competitors like the AirPods Pro 3 offer a lower price with strong features for iPhone users specifically. So the real value depends heavily on how much noise cancellation matters to you relative to other factors like battery life or which operating system you use.

Final Verdict

The Bose QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds (2nd Gen) are a technically strong product, particularly in noise cancellation, where they outperform most competitors according to multiple independent tests. However, they aren't a necessary upgrade for existing 1st Gen owners, and limited battery life remains a genuine weak point for some users. If noise cancellation is your top priority and price isn't a major concern, these earbuds are a very strong choice. If budget or battery life matters more to you, it's worth comparing current prices against the AirPods Pro 3, or even the 1st Gen from the same Bose lineup, before making a final decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it worth upgrading from the 1st Gen to the 2nd Gen? +
According to multiple independent reviews (SoundGuys, RecordingNow), the practical differences are limited, centering mainly on default wireless charging and minor call-quality and ANC improvements. If the 1st Gen is available at a lower price and you don't specifically need those features, the upgrade may not be necessary.
How long does the battery actually last? +
Per Bose officially: up to 6 hours from the buds alone (4 hours with Immersive Audio on), and up to 18 additional hours from the case, for roughly 24 hours total. HotHardware's real-world test recorded a similar figure (just over 5 hours).
Does it support wireless charging? +
Yes — this is one of the most notable additions in this generation compared to some 1st Gen configurations, per PhoneArena and Ecoustics.
Can noise cancellation be fully turned off? +
There's no complete "off" mode, per TechGearLab — you can only reduce it to its lowest setting via the Bose app.
Is it suitable for sports and workouts? +
It carries an IPX4 water-resistance rating, suitable for sweat and everyday use conditions, but it is not rated for swimming or submersion.
