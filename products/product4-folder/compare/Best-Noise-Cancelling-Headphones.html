What's new and why it matters

The WH-1000XM6 is Sony's flagship successor to the widely reviewed WH-1000XM5, and multiple outlets frame it as a synthesis of the XM4 and XM5 generations. Independent Test TechRadar describes the WH-1000XM6 as combining the best design elements of its predecessors while leveling up the sonic and ANC capabilities of what came before, with clean touch controls, a comfortable fit, and a broad feature set. Sony markets the noise cancellation as best-in-class; independent reviewers are more measured. Independent Test GSMArena calls the WH-1000XM6 among the best ANC over-ear headphones available, citing better noise cancellation and audio quality than the outgoing model, though it flags the hinge mechanism as feeling less sturdy and the $499.99 price as a point of hesitation.

Noise cancellation

Independent Test Reviewers largely agree the XM6 is at or near the top of the class. YallaBuy Analysis: "Best noise cancellation" claims in this category are close-fought between Sony, Bose, and Apple; treat any single publication's ranking as one data point rather than a universal fact.

Battery life

Manufacturer Claim: Sony rates the WH-1000XM6 for up to 30 hours of playback with noise cancelling on, and up to 40 hours with ANC off. Independent Test: RTINGS measured approximately 31 hours and 45 minutes in its own testing, closely matching Sony's stated figure. That puts the XM6 in the middle of this list — well behind the Sennheiser Momentum 4's 60-hour rating, but in line with the Bose QC Ultra 2nd Gen.

Design and comfort

The XM6 brings back the foldable hinge design used in earlier XM-series models, along with a new magnetic-clasp carrying case and a six-microphone array for calls. Reviewer opinion on the hinge is mixed: while foldability is convenient for travel, Independent Test GSMArena notes the hinging mechanism makes the headset feel less sturdy than the non-folding XM5. This is an area to watch in long-term owner reports, though there is not yet enough independent long-term data to call it a confirmed reliability concern.

Audio and connectivity

The WH-1000XM6 uses a new 30mm driver paired with Sony's QN3 processor, which includes a "noise shaper" feature designed to smooth digital-to-analogue conversion and anticipate sudden sound changes. It supports LDAC for high-resolution playback over Bluetooth on compatible Android devices, plus AAC and SBC.

Sony WH-1000XM6 vs. the competition

Independent Test: In a direct comparison, SoundGuys found the Sony WH-1000XM6 wins on sound quality, battery life, and wireless features against the AirPods Max, with both tying on ANC performance, while Apple retains an edge in wired lossless playback over USB-C. SoundGuys also notes the WH-1000XM6 remains roughly $100 cheaper than the AirPods Max.

✓ Pros
  • Class-leading ANC in most independent reviews
  • Strong, detailed sound
  • Foldable and travel-friendly
  • Broad codec support (LDAC, AAC, SBC)
  • Reliable multipoint Bluetooth
• Cons
  • Price increased over the XM5
  • Battery life is merely average for the category
  • Hinge feels less rigid to some reviewers
  • Microphone quality drops outdoors in wind

Who should buy the Sony WH-1000XM6? Frequent travelers, commuters, and anyone who wants one headset that handles noise cancellation, calls, and music equally well. It's the safest all-around pick in this guide.

Who should skip it? Buyers who specifically want the longest possible battery life (look at the Sennheiser Momentum 4 instead) or who are fully invested in Apple's ecosystem (the AirPods Max 2 will integrate more deeply).

What's new in the 2nd Gen

Independent Test: Trusted Reviews highlights a longer 30-hour battery life, a new Cinema mode for video playback, USB audio support, and improved noise cancellation as the headline updates over the first-generation QC Ultra. Rolling Stone's testing found Bose made its most meaningful improvements in ease of use, battery life, and customization, while keeping the tried-and-true ANC performance and audio quality largely intact from the previous generation.

Battery life

Manufacturer Claim: Bose's own testing found up to 24 hours of playback with Immersive Audio (spatial sound) off, and up to 18 hours with it enabled — but multiple independent reviewers, including Trusted Reviews and Rolling Stone, cite a simpler "up to 30 hours" figure for standard use, which appears to be the number Bose promotes for typical listening without spatial audio engaged. YallaBuy Analysis: This is a case where sources report different numbers depending on which feature (Immersive Audio) is factored in — worth noting if spatial audio matters to you, since it noticeably shortens runtime.

Comfort and design

This category's biggest reputation is built on comfort. The 2nd Gen keeps a similar look to its predecessor, with metal yokes, a faux-leather finish on the earcups and headband, and matte plastic on the earcups. Owner and reviewer consensus across multiple publications consistently ranks Bose among the most comfortable over-ear headphones for long sessions, though a small number of reviewers, including Trusted Reviews, mention occasional hinge squeaks as a minor build quality complaint.

Connectivity

The 2nd Gen upgrades to Bluetooth 5.4 with support for SBC, AAC, and aptX Adaptive, and Independent Test Trusted Reviews found the wireless connection notably stable even in congested RF environments.

✓ Pros
  • Widely regarded as the most comfortable flagship
  • Effective, highly customizable ANC
  • New Cinema and Immersive Audio modes
  • USB-C audio input
• Cons
  • Price increase over the original QC Ultra
  • Battery life claims vary by feature use
  • Occasional hinge squeaks reported
  • Heavy reliance on the Bose app

Who should buy it? Anyone who prioritizes wearing comfort above all else — people with glasses, long commutes, or all-day desk use will likely appreciate the fit more than raw ANC scores.

Who should skip it? Buyers chasing the single longest battery life, or those unwilling to pay a premium over the still-capable first-generation QC Ultra, which remains available at a discount.

3. Apple AirPods Max 2 — Best for Apple Users

What actually changed from the original AirPods Max

This is the most important thing to understand before buying. Independent Test: Rolling Stone's review notes that outside of new colorways, the AirPods Max 2 is externally identical to the original, down to the ounce, with the same battery life and the same widely criticized flimsy case. The core internal upgrade is the shift from Apple's older H1 chip to the H2 chip — the same chip used in the AirPods Pro 3 — which Apple says delivers up to 1.5x more effective ANC.

The H2 chip enables several new software features: Adaptive Audio (automatically adjusting ANC and Transparency based on the environment), Conversation Awareness, Apple Intelligence-powered Live Translation, and Voice Isolation.

YallaBuy Analysis: If you already own the original AirPods Max, the case for upgrading is weak — you're paying full price largely for a chip upgrade and new software features rather than a redesigned product. If you're buying your first pair, the Max 2 is the better starting point since it includes the newer chip at no cost premium.

Battery life

Manufacturer Claim / Independent Test: Tom's Guide's side-by-side comparison confirms battery life is unchanged at 20 hours for both the original AirPods Max and AirPods Max 2 — the shortest rated runtime of any model in this guide.

Sound quality

Independent Test: What Hi-Fi? found the AirPods Max 2 offers better punch, dynamics, and precision than the original, with a high standard of build and finish and improved ANC, but notes that rival headphones in this price range have moved on considerably, offering greater insight, rhythmic precision, and dynamic punch. The publication also notes that the sound differences between the two generations are relatively small.

ANC and transparency mode

Owner Feedback / Independent Test: One reviewer with extensive headphone-comparison experience rates the AirPods Max 2's noise cancelling slightly above the AirPods Pro 3 and describes the transparency mode, powered by the H2 chip, as more natural-sounding and arguably best-in-class. This should be read as one reviewer's opinion rather than a settled industry consensus, since Sony and Bose also claim ANC leadership.

✓ Pros
  • Deep integration with iPhone, iPad, and Mac
  • Premium build quality
  • Improved ANC and transparency mode via H2
  • New Apple Intelligence features like Live Translation
• Cons
  • Most expensive model in this guide
  • Unchanged 20-hour battery life (shortest here)
  • Same case design criticized on the original
  • Minimal external redesign for "2nd generation"
  • Limited functionality on Android devices

Who should buy the AirPods Max 2? Buyers embedded in the Apple ecosystem who value seamless device switching and are willing to pay a premium for it — this is squarely an ecosystem purchase, not primarily a pure ANC or battery-life play.

Who should skip it? Android users get almost none of the ecosystem benefit and would get more ANC and battery performance per dollar from the Sony WH-1000XM6 or JBL Tour One M3. Current AirPods Max owners have little reason to upgrade based on the evidence above.

The headline feature: battery life

This is not a close contest. Manufacturer Claim: Sennheiser rates the Momentum 4 for up to 60 hours of playback with ANC enabled. Independent Test: Real-world testing from multiple reviewers consistently confirms 57–59 hours, more than double the roughly 30-hour rating of both the Sony WH-1000XM6 and Bose QC Ultra. Charging is also fast: Sennheiser states 5 minutes of charging delivers about 4 hours of playback, with a full charge taking around 2 hours.

YallaBuy Analysis: If battery anxiety is your main headphone frustration — forgetting to charge before a trip, running out mid-flight — the Momentum 4 solves that problem more effectively than anything else in this guide, including headphones released a full generation later.

Sound and design

The Momentum 4 uses 42mm drivers with a frequency response of 6 Hz–22 kHz, and supports SBC, AAC, and aptX Adaptive codecs over Bluetooth 5.2, adjustable through Sennheiser's Smart Control app. Independent Test: Tom's Guide's review praises great sound and effective noise cancelling, alongside reliable touch controls, but criticizes the "anonymous" plain styling and notes the earpads can make ears feel warm over long sessions.

Known limitations

Independent Test: Tom's Guide also reports flaky connectivity issues specifically tied to the Smart Control companion app — worth knowing if you plan to rely heavily on app-based EQ adjustments.

✓ Pros
  • Class-leading 60-hour rated battery life (confirmed)
  • Strong, balanced sound
  • Effective ANC
  • Fast charging
  • Frequently discounted below list price
• Cons
  • Plain, understated design compared to rivals
  • Earpads can run warm on long sessions
  • App connectivity glitches reported
  • Bluetooth 5.2 is a generation behind some competitors

Who should buy the Momentum 4? Long-haul travelers, people who regularly forget to charge headphones overnight, and anyone who values runtime over styling or the latest Bluetooth version.

Who should skip it? Buyers who want the most eye-catching design or the tightest ecosystem integration — this is a headphone built around one standout spec rather than an all-rounder flagship experience.

Why reviewers single out the sound

This is the pick for listeners who care more about audio fidelity than the absolute strongest ANC. Independent Test: ecoustics.com states the Px7 S3 delivers audiophile-grade sound, deep bass, and premium build quality that surpasses Bose, Sony, and Apple in clarity and detail. What Hi-Fi? similarly praises a full-bodied sonic character with impressive textural insight, calling it a serious marker at this price level.

Battery life

Manufacturer Claim: Bowers & Wilkins claims 30 hours of battery life on a charge, though the company doesn't specify whether this figure is with ANC on or off. Independent Test: Engadget's testing found the Px7 S3 tends to outlast its own stated figures — after 15 hours of mixed use with ANC mostly active, over 60% battery remained, suggesting Bowers & Wilkins' rating is conservative rather than optimistic.

Design and usability features

The Px7 S3 keeps physical buttons rather than moving fully to touch controls, and includes wear sensors in both earcups that trigger standby mode and auto-pause playback when the headphones are removed.

ANC performance

Owner Feedback / Independent Test: One long-term reviewer characterizes the Px7 S3's ANC as slightly improved and less intrusive than its predecessor, while still noting a mild "pressurized" sensation that some other ANC headphones also produce. It is generally regarded as competent but not class-leading — the Px7 S3's reputation is built on sound quality, not maximum noise suppression.

Why it stands out at this price

Independent Test: SoundGuys, reviewing the Tour One M3 head-to-head against Sony, Bose, and Apple, concluded that for its price the JBL delivers best-in-class battery life, more connectivity options than any competitor, matched ANC performance, and sound that rewards minimal EQ effort — while acknowledging Sony still leads on microphone quality and out-of-the-box sound.

Battery life

Here the sources genuinely disagree, and it's worth explaining why. Manufacturer Claim: JBL states 40 hours of playback with ANC on and 70 hours with ANC off. Independent Test: SoundGuys' own standardized testing measured 55 hours and 37 minutes with ANC on — well above JBL's own conservative claim. YallaBuy Analysis: As with the Bowers & Wilkins Px7 S3, this looks like a case of a manufacturer under-promising; independent lab conditions found notably more runtime than the official spec sheet states. Either figure — JBL's 40-hour claim or SoundGuys' 55-hour measurement — comfortably beats the Sony and Bose flagships.

Accessibility feature

Manufacturer Claim / Independent Test: SoundGuys highlights a Personal Sound Amplification (PSAP) mode that turns transparency mode into something closer to a hearing-assistance feature, working regardless of which phone brand you use — unlike Apple's comparable Hearing Aid feature, which is locked to its own ecosystem.

Design and build

Owner Feedback: One long-term reviewer notes average build quality for the price, with plastic-heavy earcup exteriors, though a metal core in the headband and leather-like cushioning keep it from feeling cheap. A separate review flags that the plastic build feels less premium for the price, and that touch controls can be overly sensitive.

✓ Pros
  • Strong value relative to Sony, Bose, and Apple
  • Tested battery life exceeds official claims
  • Matched ANC performance in direct testing
  • Unique cross-platform hearing-assistance mode
  • Ongoing firmware improvements noted
• Cons
  • Plastic-heavy build feels less premium
  • Touch controls reported as overly sensitive
  • Sound quality competent rather than exceptional
  • Optional Smart Tx transmitter adds cost

Who should buy the Tour One M3? Buyers who want flagship-adjacent ANC and battery performance without paying Sony, Bose, or Apple flagship prices — and anyone who specifically needs the cross-platform hearing-assistance mode.

Who should skip it? Build-quality-conscious buyers who want a more premium feel, or listeners chasing the most refined sound signature (the Px7 S3 or Sony WH-1000XM6 will serve that need better).

What you get at this price

Where the budget shows

Owner Feedback & Long-Term Concerns

Independent lab testing captures performance at launch; it doesn't always reveal how headphones hold up after months of daily use. Based on available owner and reviewer reports:

Sony WH-1000XM6

Some reviewers flag the new folding hinge as feeling less sturdy than the non-folding XM5, though there isn't yet enough independent long-term data to call this a confirmed defect rate — it's a pattern worth watching rather than a proven problem.

Bose QC Ultra (2nd Gen)

A small number of reviewers, including Trusted Reviews, mention occasional hinge squeaks. This appears to be an isolated-to-minor complaint rather than a widespread pattern based on current evidence.

Sennheiser Momentum 4

Reports of flaky Smart Control app connectivity come from at least one major publication (Tom's Guide); this is a software-side concern rather than a hardware durability issue.

Anker Soundcore Space Q45

Community reports on Head-Fi.org mention hiss with ANC engaged. This is a recurring theme across a handful of independent sources, so it's fair to describe as a known limitation rather than an isolated complaint, though it does not appear to affect all units.

As with any electronics category, treat scattered individual complaints with caution, and weigh confirmed patterns — reported across multiple independent sources — more heavily than single anecdotes.

Value for Money

Price alone doesn't determine value. Here's how the evidence stacks up:

The Anker Soundcore Space Q45 offers the strongest raw value if your budget is the primary constraint — you get real ANC, LDAC support, and long battery life for a fraction of flagship pricing, with the trade-off being ANC strength and mic performance that independent testers rate below the category leaders.

The JBL Tour One M3 offers the best value-to-performance ratio among near-flagship options, delivering ANC that SoundGuys found comparable to Sony and Bose at a noticeably lower price.

The Sony WH-1000XM6 and Bose QC Ultra (2nd Gen) justify their higher prices primarily through polish, app ecosystems, and (for Bose) comfort — not through a dramatic performance gap over the JBL.

The Apple AirPods Max 2 is the hardest to justify on pure specs alone; its value proposition is almost entirely about Apple ecosystem integration rather than ANC or battery performance relative to price.

The Sennheiser Momentum 4 delivers unusual value when found on sale below its $349.95 list price, given its battery life advantage is large enough to outlast a full generation of newer competitors.

Final Verdict

There is no single "best" noise-cancelling headphone — only the best headphone for a specific priority:

  • Want the most well-rounded flagship? Sony WH-1000XM6
  • Want to forget you're wearing headphones for hours? Bose QuietComfort Ultra (2nd Gen)
  • Live entirely inside Apple's ecosystem? AirPods Max 2 — but only if you don't already own the original
  • Traveling for days without reliable charging? Sennheiser Momentum 4
  • Care most about how music actually sounds? Bowers & Wilkins Px7 S3
  • Want near-flagship performance without the price? JBL Tour One M3
  • Working with a tight budget but still want real ANC? Anker Soundcore Space Q45

Whichever you choose, remember that prices on all seven models change frequently — check current availability and pricing on Amazon before making a final decision.
