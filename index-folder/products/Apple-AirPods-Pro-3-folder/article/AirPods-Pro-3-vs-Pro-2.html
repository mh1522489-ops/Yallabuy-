AirPods Pro 3 vs AirPods Pro 2: Is the Upgrade Actually Worth It?

⚡ Quick Verdict

The AirPods Pro 3 (released September 2025) are Apple's biggest AirPods Pro upgrade in years. Independent lab testing from SoundGuys found they cut outside noise by an average of 90%, compared with roughly 83% for the AirPods Pro 2, and they nearly doubled measured battery life — 8 hours 42 minutes versus 5 hours 43 minutes with ANC on. They also add a heart-rate sensor, IP57 water resistance, and a redesigned fit.

What Makes the AirPods Pro 3 Different?

The two biggest differentiators, backed by both Apple's claims and independent testing, are noise cancellation and battery life. Apple's marketing claims "up to 2x more Active Noise Cancellation" than the Pro 2, and independent lab data from SoundGuys supports a real, measurable jump: an average external noise reduction of about 90% for the Pro 3 versus about 83% for the Pro 2 — which SoundGuys and What Hi-Fi both describe as enough to match or beat some over-ear headphones, including Apple's own AirPods Max.

The second differentiator is biometrics. The Pro 3 adds an in-ear photoplethysmography (PPG) heart-rate sensor that the Pro 2 does not have at all. Independent testing (CNET Labs, plus a peer-reviewed treadmill study published in PLOS Digital Health) found the Pro 3's heart-rate readings landed close to Apple Watch and ahead of several non-Apple smartwatches.

Key Feature

Active Noise Cancellation

ANC uses microphones to detect ambient sound and generate an inverse waveform that cancels it out before it reaches your ear. Both models use the H2 chip, but the Pro 3 pairs it with new ultra-low-noise microphones, redesigned foam-infused ear tips, and updated computational audio processing.

For commuters, frequent flyers, or anyone working in noisy environments, stronger ANC directly improves daily usability. SoundGuys notes that ANC performance depends heavily on getting a proper seal — the Pro 3's firmer tips don't behave like memory foam, so a poor fit can undercut the lab-measured gains.

Key Feature

Battery Life

The Pro 3's larger measured runtime comes from a combination of a redesigned internal architecture and chip efficiency improvements. SoundGuys' independent test recorded 8 hours 42 minutes for the Pro 3 versus 5 hours 43 minutes for the Pro 2 with ANC on — a real-world-relevant improvement of roughly 50%, not just a marketing number.

Real battery life varies with volume, ANC setting, and age of the battery cells; both Apple's and SoundGuys' figures are single-test snapshots, not guarantees.

New — Pro 3 Only

Heart Rate Sensing

A built-in infrared PPG sensor measures blood flow in the ear canal during workouts, feeding data to the Fitness app. It fires infrared light at a high sampling rate and uses the onboard accelerometer to subtract footstrike vibration ("cadence lock") from the optical signal.

For buyers without an Apple Watch, it adds fitness tracking (Apple claims support for 50+ workout types) without another purchase. However, TechRepublic's review is explicit that the sensor is "not a clinical tool, a chest-strap replacement for precision training, or a smartwatch substitute" — its value is as a convenient secondary data source, not a medical-grade instrument.

Durability

Water and Sweat Resistance

The Pro 3 and its case carry an IP57 rating, up from IP54 on the Pro 2. What Hi-Fi calls this "one small but significant improvement," noting it means better protection against rain, sweat, splashes, and even brief full submersion. Apple states clearly that water resistance is not a permanent condition and can degrade with normal wear; it's not intended for swimming or water sports.

Live Translation and Hearing Health Features

Live Translation converts spoken conversation between supported languages in near real time; Hearing Test, Hearing Aid, and Hearing Protection are FDA-authorized accessibility features. According to Apple's own specification pages, these features are shared across both models — Live Translation and the Hearing Aid feature both work on AirPods Pro 2 and later with the latest firmware, paired with an Apple Intelligence-enabled iPhone on iOS 26+.

Important: Several of the headline "new" features attached to the Pro 3 launch are not actually exclusive to it. If Live Translation or the Hearing Aid feature is your main reason for upgrading, the Pro 2 already supports them with current firmware.

Performance (Independent Testing)

RTINGS and SoundGuys have both published dedicated review and comparison content for the Pro 3. According to SoundGuys' direct Pro 3 vs. Pro 2 upgrade comparison, lab measurements show the Pro 3 improving on the Pro 2 in two areas with clear, reproducible numbers: noise reduction (90% vs. 83%) and battery runtime (8:42 vs. 5:43). SoundGuys frames the Pro 3 as a "close to perfect" earbud overall, while also flagging "occasional connection quirks and channel-panning glitches."

What Hi-Fi's review praised the Pro 3 for "spacious, powerful, dynamic sound" and "improved ANC," while listing "battery life could be improved" and "strong competition" among its cons — a reminder that even a clear generational improvement over the Pro 2 doesn't automatically mean class-leading numbers against every rival on the market.

Real-World Experience (User & Independent Reports)

Recurring Issue — Static/Hiss with ANC: Multiple outlets (TechRadar, PhoneArena) reported that some AirPods Pro 3 owners experience a static, hiss, or white-noise sound when ANC is enabled and no audio is playing. Apple issued at least two firmware updates (November and December 2025) without users confirming the issue was fully resolved. This appears widespread enough in reporting to describe as a recurring concern, though it does not affect every unit.
Recurring Issue — Fit and Comfort: A USA Today review and several Reddit/forum threads describe discomfort with the redesigned, firmer ear tips, which Apple built with an internal foam layer for a more stable seal. Reports include tips falling out, ears feeling sore after extended wear, and difficulty finding a comfortable size — a contrast with some users' experience of the Pro 2's tips.
YallaBuy Analysis: Buyers sensitive to in-ear pressure or who had a marginal fit with previous AirPods Pro generations should try the Pro 3 in person or buy from a retailer with an easy return policy before committing, given the volume and consistency of fit-related reports across independent outlets.

Problems & Limitations

AirPods Pro 3: Static/hiss reports with ANC enabled (unresolved as of the most recent reporting); fit complaints tied to firmer ear tips; What Hi-Fi flags "strong competition" in the premium ANC earbud space.

AirPods Pro 2: No heart-rate sensor or built-in workout tracking; lower IP54 rating; shorter battery life than the newer model; increasingly positioned by Apple and retailers as the outgoing model.

Competitors like the Bose QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds (2nd Gen) and Samsung Galaxy Buds4 Pro may do better in specific areas — Bose has historically emphasized comfort and a plush passive seal, and Samsung's Buds4 Pro offer cross-platform (Android) compatibility that neither AirPods model matches.

Value for Money

At identical $249 launch prices, the Pro 3 objectively offers more hardware and more independently verified performance than the Pro 2 did at launch — better measured ANC, roughly 50% more measured battery life, a new sensor, and a higher IP rating. That said, value isn't just about specs on the day of release. Because the Pro 2 is the outgoing model, it is realistically available at a lower current price more often, which changes the calculation for budget-conscious buyers who don't need the newest features. The static-noise and fit reports around the Pro 3 are also worth weighing against the performance gains — a highly-rated spec sheet doesn't guarantee a comfortable, glitch-free experience for every buyer.

Final Verdict

If you're buying AirPods Pro for the first time, or upgrading from the original AirPods Pro or a non-Pro model, the AirPods Pro 3 is the stronger buy based on the independent evidence available: better lab-tested ANC, meaningfully longer measured battery life, and new fitness/health capabilities the Pro 2 lacks.

If you already own a Pro 2 and it still works well for you, the case for upgrading is weaker — several of the "new" software features (Live Translation, Hearing Aid) already reached the Pro 2, and the reported static and fit issues on the Pro 3 mean it isn't a universally smoother experience. Budget-focused buyers who don't need heart-rate tracking may get better overall value from a discounted Pro 2, provided current pricing supports that.
