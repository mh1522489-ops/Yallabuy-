What Are the Bose QuietComfort 45 Headphones?

The Bose QuietComfort 45 is a wireless over-ear noise-canceling headphone designed primarily around comfort and noise reduction.

The philosophy behind the product is easy to understand.

Bose did not try to make the QC45 the most futuristic headphone on the market. Instead, the company concentrated on the core QuietComfort formula:

lightweight construction + comfortable earcups + strong ANC + easy controls.

The QC45 uses Bluetooth 5.1 and supports wireless connectivity as well as a wired 3.5mm connection. Bose's official specifications list a rechargeable lithium-ion battery, a USB-C charging connection, built-in microphones, and a Bluetooth range of up to approximately 10 meters.

The headphones also work with the Bose app, which provides setup and control functions.

That simplicity is actually one of the QC45's biggest strengths.

You don't need to learn complicated gestures.

You don't need to remember ten touch controls.

You have physical buttons.

For many people, that is better.

Bose QuietComfort 45 Design

At first glance, the Bose QuietComfort 45 looks very familiar.

That is intentional.

Bose maintained the classic QuietComfort shape rather than completely redesigning the headphone.

The result is a relatively conservative-looking product that prioritizes practicality.

The earcups are large enough to surround most ears rather than pressing directly against them.

The headband is padded.

The ear cushions use a soft synthetic material.

And the overall construction is designed to remain relatively light.

Bose lists the headphones as an around-ear design with protein-leather ear cushions and a glass-filled nylon construction.

This combination helps explain why the QC45 feels more like a travel tool than a fashion accessory.

It is not trying to make a huge visual statement.

It is trying to disappear once you put it on.

And that is exactly what a QuietComfort headphone should do.

Build Quality

The QC45 feels solid enough for everyday use.

It does not feel like a luxury metal headphone, and that is important to understand.

Bose uses lightweight materials because reducing weight is part of the product's design philosophy.

This can initially make the headphones feel less premium than some heavier competitors.

But after several hours of use, the advantage becomes much clearer.

A heavy headphone can feel impressive in your hand.

A lightweight headphone feels impressive after wearing it for six hours.

The QC45 is designed for the second experience.

The folding mechanism also makes the headphones much easier to transport.

They can be folded into the included carrying case, which is particularly useful for travelers.

Bose QuietComfort 45 Noise Cancellation

Noise cancellation is the reason many people buy Bose headphones.

And the QC45 is very good at it.

Multiple independent reviews have praised its ANC performance. SoundGuys described its noise cancellation as outstanding, while RTINGS found that the QC45 performs very well at reducing external noise across different scenarios.

The biggest strength is low-frequency environmental noise.

That makes the QC45 particularly useful for:

  • Airplane engines
  • Bus engines
  • Train noise
  • Air-conditioning systems
  • Fans
  • Office HVAC noise
  • General background rumble

Put the QC45 on during a flight and the constant engine rumble can become dramatically less noticeable.

That does not mean the outside world disappears completely.

No ANC headphone can realistically eliminate every sound.

Human voices, sudden noises, high-frequency sounds, and nearby conversations can still get through.

But the overall noise floor becomes much calmer.

How Does the Bose QuietComfort 45 Noise Cancellation Work?

Active noise cancellation uses microphones to detect external sound.

The headphone's electronics then generate an opposing signal designed to reduce the perceived external noise.

The technology works particularly well with predictable continuous sounds.

That is why airplane engines are a perfect example.

An engine produces a relatively constant low-frequency sound.

The ANC system can detect and counteract much of it.

Random sounds are more difficult.

Someone suddenly shouting next to you is not the same kind of problem as an airplane engine.

The QC45 is therefore best understood as a tool for reducing environmental noise, not as an invisible wall around your head.

The Biggest ANC Problem: No True Off Mode

This is one of the most important weaknesses of the QC45.

You cannot simply switch the active noise cancellation completely off and continue listening in a traditional passive mode.

Instead, the available listening modes revolve around Quiet and Aware.

RTINGS and SoundGuys both identify the lack of a true ANC-off mode as a limitation.

Why does this matter?

Because there are situations where you might want:

Bluetooth + normal passive listening + no ANC processing.

For example, perhaps you are sitting in a quiet room and simply want to save battery.

The QC45 does not give you that exact option.

This is a small problem for some users and a major annoyance for others.

Bose QuietComfort 45 Headphones Not Charging

If your Bose QuietComfort 45 headphones are not charging, don't immediately assume the battery is dead.

Try these steps.

1. Try another USB-C cable. A damaged cable can make the headphone appear defective.

2. Try another charger. Use another compatible power source.

3. Inspect the charging port. Look for dust or physical damage. Do not force anything into the port.

4. Leave the headphones connected. If the battery is extremely depleted, give the headphones some time before assuming something is wrong.

5. Try resetting the headphones. Follow Bose's official reset procedure rather than random button combinations from unofficial websites.

Bose provides dedicated troubleshooting resources for the QC45.

Bose QuietComfort 45 Headphones Won't Turn On

  • Perform the official reset procedure
