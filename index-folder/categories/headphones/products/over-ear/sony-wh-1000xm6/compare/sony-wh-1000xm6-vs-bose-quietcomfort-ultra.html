Sony WH-1000XM6 vs. Bose QuietComfort Ultra Headphones (2nd Gen): Which Premium Headphones Should You Buy?

A comprehensive comparison of noise cancellation, sound quality, comfort, battery life, calls, connectivity, and everyday usability.

By Mahmoud Hamdy · YallaBuy · Last updated: October 15, 2026

The short answer

The Sony WH-1000XM6 and Bose QuietComfort Ultra Headphones (2nd Gen) are premium wireless headphones designed for listeners who want effective active noise cancellation (ANC), high-quality audio, and a more comfortable listening experience in noisy environments.

However, they approach that goal differently.

Sony emphasizes extensive sound customization, adaptive noise cancellation, communication features, and flexible everyday controls. Bose focuses on long-wearing comfort, effective noise cancellation, and an immersive listening experience, with additional features for USB-C audio and spatialized sound.

Neither headphone is automatically the better choice for everyone. The right decision depends on how you listen, how long you wear your headphones, which devices you use, and which features matter enough to justify the price.

1. Understanding the two headphones

Before comparing individual features, it helps to understand what these headphones are designed to do.

Sony WH-1000XM6

Sony's WH-1000XM6 is an over-ear wireless headphone with active noise cancellation, adaptive ambient sound, Bluetooth connectivity, customizable audio, and support for wired listening.

Its noise-cancellation system uses Sony's HD Noise Canceling Processor QN3 and an adaptive microphone system. Sony states that the system uses 12 microphones and adjusts noise cancellation according to environmental conditions and the way the headphones are worn.

The XM6 also supports LDAC, Sony's higher-bitrate Bluetooth audio codec, alongside SBC, AAC, and LC3. It can connect to multiple devices through Bluetooth multipoint, making it suitable for people who regularly switch between a smartphone and a computer.

These features make the XM6 more than a pair of headphones that simply blocks background noise. It is designed to provide considerable control over how audio is reproduced and how the headphones respond to their surroundings.

Bose QuietComfort Ultra Headphones (2nd Gen)

The second-generation Bose QuietComfort Ultra Headphones follow a similar overall concept but place particular emphasis on comfort, noise cancellation, and spatialized listening.

Bose includes its CustomTune technology, which adapts audio processing to the listener, alongside its noise-cancellation system and Bose Immersive Audio. The second generation also introduces USB-C digital audio, Cinema Mode, and improvements to battery life and standby behavior.

Its USB-C audio support is an important distinction. A USB-C cable can carry digital audio when the connected device and headphones support that functionality; USB-C charging alone does not establish that a headphone supports USB audio.

Bose also provides an analog connection, adjustable listening modes, and an application for managing settings.

Why the generation matters

The original Bose QuietComfort Ultra Headphones and the second generation are not interchangeable in every comparison.

The newer model adds features such as USB-C digital audio and Cinema Mode, while extending the advertised battery life with noise cancellation disabled. A comparison that uses the first generation's specifications to describe the second generation can therefore produce misleading conclusions.

When shopping, check the full product name and generation before comparing prices, specifications, or reviews.

2. Active noise cancellation: Which headphone blocks more noise?

Active noise cancellation is one of the main reasons to consider either of these models.

ANC works by using microphones to detect environmental sound and processing an opposing signal to reduce the noise reaching the listener. Its effectiveness depends on the noise's frequency, how it changes over time, the headphone's fit, and the quality of the seal around the ears.

This means ANC performance cannot be reduced to a single processor specification or a manufacturer's claim about the number of microphones.

Sony's noise-cancellation system

The WH-1000XM6 uses Sony's QN3 processor and an adaptive microphone system intended to adjust noise cancellation as the environment changes.

Sony also provides an Adaptive NC Optimizer, which accounts for factors such as environmental noise, atmospheric pressure, and wearing conditions. These capabilities are useful when moving between different environments, including offices, public transport, and aircraft cabins.

The practical objective is to maintain effective noise reduction without requiring the listener to adjust the headphones continually.

A well-functioning ANC system can make repetitive background sounds less distracting, allowing the listener to reduce music volume or work without constant interruptions.

However, stronger noise cancellation does not mean every sound disappears. Sudden impacts, nearby speech, and irregular noises can remain audible.

Bose's noise-cancellation system

Bose has a long-standing focus on noise cancellation, and the second-generation QuietComfort Ultra continues that approach.

Its system combines noise cancellation with listening modes that let users choose between quieter environments and greater awareness of their surroundings. The Aware mode, including ActiveSense when enabled, can respond to changing environmental sound rather than treating every situation identically.

This flexibility is useful when walking through a busy environment or listening in places where remaining aware of surrounding sounds matters.

Bose also uses CustomTune to adapt its audio processing to the listener. CustomTune should not be confused with a guarantee of identical noise cancellation or sound quality for every person.

What do independent comparisons say?

RTINGS' comparison of the Sony WH-1000XM6 and Bose QuietComfort Ultra Headphones (2nd Gen), updated in August 2026, identifies Sony as having a slight advantage in noise isolation, although the difference is relatively small.

That is a more useful conclusion than declaring one headphone the universal winner. Both belong to the premium ANC category, and the result can change with the noise being measured and the way the headphones fit.

Low-frequency noise versus human voices

Not all noise-cancellation performance is equally valuable in daily life.

Low-frequency sounds include the steady rumble of aircraft cabins, buses, trains, and air-conditioning systems. Human voices contain more complex frequencies and change continuously as people speak.

A headphone may perform extremely well against a steady engine but provide less isolation from nearby conversations. Even excellent ANC does not create complete silence.

For travelers, the most important factor may be the reduction of continuous transport noise. For someone working in a shared office, reducing conversations and intermittent sounds may be equally important.

Both models are appropriate candidates for these environments, but the relative advantage depends on the specific situation.

The role of fit

The earcups must form a reasonably consistent seal around the ears for effective isolation.

Glasses, hair caught beneath the cushions, head shape, and earcup positioning can affect that seal. A headphone that performs exceptionally well in a laboratory may deliver less consistent results for someone whose glasses create a gap between the cushions and their head.

This is one reason comfort and ANC should be evaluated together rather than as completely separate features.

Bose's more generously padded fit may appeal to some listeners, while others may prefer Sony's shape. Neither design guarantees a perfect seal for every person.

Verdict: Noise cancellation

Both headphones are excellent options for listeners who want to reduce background noise. Sony has a small advantage in some independent comparisons, but the gap is not large enough to make Bose an unsuitable alternative.

Choose Sony if you want a highly capable adaptive system and extensive controls. Choose Bose if its fit and listening modes suit you better. If possible, try both with your own glasses and in-ear fit preferences before deciding.

3. Sound quality: Sony's flexibility versus Bose's presentation

Sound quality is more difficult to rank objectively than a specification table might suggest.

Different listeners prefer different amounts of bass, different vocal presentations, and different levels of treble detail. The type of music also matters.

A bass-heavy electronic track places different demands on a headphone than a vocal recording, an acoustic performance, or a film soundtrack.

For this reason, a useful comparison should examine the default sound, the available adjustments, and the kinds of listening experiences each model supports.

Sony WH-1000XM6: A customizable sound profile

Sony provides a detailed equalizer through its companion application, giving listeners the ability to adjust bass, midrange, and treble to suit their preferences.

This is valuable because the default sound is only one part of the listening experience. Someone who finds the bass too strong can reduce it, while a listener who wants more low-frequency impact can make the opposite adjustment.

The XM6 supports LDAC, which can transmit higher-bitrate audio over Bluetooth when the source device and playback settings support it.

However, codec support alone does not establish that one headphone sounds better. The recording, the source device, the chosen codec, the headphone's tuning, and the listener's preferences all contribute to the final result.

For most users, a well-adjusted sound profile and a good recording are more meaningful than simply choosing the codec with the highest advertised bitrate.

Bose QuietComfort Ultra: A more immersive presentation

Bose combines CustomTune with adjustable EQ and Bose Immersive Audio.

Immersive Audio is designed to create a spatialized listening experience that can make audio feel more expansive than conventional headphone playback. Its appeal depends on the listener, the content, and the selected settings.

Some listeners enjoy the sense of space it creates, particularly when watching films or listening to music. Others prefer conventional stereo because they want the original presentation without additional spatial processing.

Bose also offers Cinema Mode on the second-generation model. This mode is intended to create a more cinema-like presentation, with particular attention to dialogue and the spatial character of video content.

These features add flexibility, but they should not be treated as automatic improvements to every recording.

Bass, vocals, and treble

The two headphones have different default sound characteristics.

RTINGS' measured comparison describes the Sony as having very emphasized bass and a comparatively balanced treble region, while the Bose has emphasized bass and more underemphasized treble.

Those descriptions refer to measured frequency-response characteristics, not a universal ranking of sound quality.

In practical terms, a listener who prefers a warmer presentation may appreciate either model. Someone who wants more control over the default tonal balance may prefer Sony's extensive equalizer.

A listener who likes Bose's spatial processing may find that it contributes more to their enjoyment than small differences in conventional frequency response.

Does LDAC make Sony the better headphone?

Not automatically.

LDAC can support higher-bitrate Bluetooth transmission than basic SBC in compatible configurations. But the actual benefit depends on the recording, the source, the selected connection settings, and whether the listener can perceive a difference.

Bluetooth audio quality also depends on more than the codec name. The headphone's acoustic design, signal processing, tuning, and recording quality remain important.

Bose's Snapdragon Sound support is another connectivity feature, but compatibility depends on the connected device and the particular audio technologies it supports.

Neither codec label should be used as a substitute for evaluating the complete listening experience.

Music genres and personal preferences

For electronic music and hip-hop, bass response and control may be particularly important. Both headphones offer substantial low-frequency presence, and EQ adjustments can help match the sound to individual tastes.

For acoustic music and vocal recordings, listeners may care more about vocal clarity, instrument separation, and how natural the midrange sounds.

For film soundtracks, Bose's spatialized listening features may appeal to people who want a more expansive presentation. Sony's sound customization may be more attractive to listeners who prefer to adjust the tonal balance themselves.

These are starting points for choosing, not guarantees that one model will perform better with a specific genre.

Verdict: Sound quality

There is no universal winner based on specifications alone. Sony offers a particularly flexible route to personalizing the sound, while Bose adds spatialized listening features that may appeal to people who watch films or enjoy a wider presentation.

If conventional stereo and EQ control are your priorities, begin with Sony. If Bose Immersive Audio and Cinema Mode are important to you, evaluate Bose with the content you actually watch and hear.

4. Comfort: A specification cannot tell the whole story

Comfort deserves as much attention as sound quality in this comparison.

Premium over-ear headphones are often used for several hours at a time. Small differences in headband pressure, earcup depth, cushion material, and weight distribution can become significant during a long flight or a full working day.

Sony's fit

The WH-1000XM6 weighs approximately 254 grams, according to Sony's published specifications.

Its design is intended to provide a secure over-ear fit, but the experience depends on the listener's ear shape and head size. Earcup depth and internal microphone placement may matter to people whose ears sit close to the internal surfaces.

Listeners with larger ears should pay particular attention to whether their ears touch the internal padding or other components.

The headband's pressure distribution also matters. Even a relatively light headphone can become uncomfortable if its clamping force or contact points do not suit the wearer.

Bose's fit

The QuietComfort Ultra Headphones (2nd Gen) weigh approximately 264 grams based on Bose's published product dimensions and weight information.

Bose emphasizes soft ear cushions, adjustable headband positioning, and a design intended for long listening sessions.

Its plush padding may be especially attractive to people who prioritize comfort on flights, during remote work, or while listening to music for extended periods.

However, softer cushions do not guarantee that the headphones will suit every head shape. Heat buildup, pressure around the ears, and the fit around glasses can still affect comfort.

What independent reviews indicate

SoundGuys' comparison of the Sony WH-1000XM6 with the original Bose QuietComfort Ultra found Bose more comfortable for extended wear, citing its padding and earcup design.

That comparison is useful for understanding the design differences, but it should not be presented as a direct test of the second-generation Bose model in every respect.

The newer model retains the general QuietComfort Ultra design philosophy, but individual fit remains personal.

Wearing headphones with glasses

Glasses introduce an additional challenge because their arms can create small gaps between the cushions and the head.

Those gaps may affect passive isolation and ANC performance. They can also create pressure points during extended wear.

Try both models with your usual glasses, if possible. Check whether the frames press against your temples, whether the cushions maintain a consistent seal, and whether the headphones remain comfortable after at least 15–20 minutes.

A quick fitting in a store may reveal obvious pressure problems, although a short test cannot fully reproduce several hours of use.

Verdict: Comfort

Bose is a strong starting point for people who prioritize plush padding and long-session comfort. Sony remains a reasonable choice, but its fit should be checked carefully if you have larger ears or are particularly sensitive to earcup pressure.

Comfort is sufficiently personal that it is worth trying both headphones before buying whenever possible.

Sony and Bose publish their maximum playback estimates under specified conditions. Actual runtime can change with volume, listening modes, wireless settings, environmental conditions, and battery age.

Everyday use

With ANC enabled, both headphones advertise up to 30 hours of playback. That makes them broadly comparable on the headline figure.

For someone listening for four hours each day, 30 hours would represent roughly seven and a half days of playback if the maximum figure were achieved consistently. In real use, the result may be lower.

For longer journeys, the difference between a full battery and a partially depleted one can be more important than a small difference in the maximum advertised runtime.

Quick charging

Sony's advertised quick-charge rate is especially useful when a short charging window is available.

Bose also offers fast charging, with up to three hours of playback from a 15-minute charge under its stated conditions.

These figures are not perfectly comparable unless charging conditions, settings, and playback requirements are identical. Nevertheless, Sony's published quick-charge claim is more aggressive.

Battery life without ANC

Bose advertises up to 45 hours with ANC turned off, compared with Sony's up to 40 hours.

This difference matters mainly to people who frequently use their headphones without active noise cancellation.

For listeners who keep ANC enabled most of the time, the equal advertised 30-hour figure is more relevant.

Can you charge while listening?

Bose specifically lists the ability to charge its second-generation QuietComfort Ultra Headphones while they are in use.

For the Sony model, check the manufacturer's instructions for the operating conditions you intend to use. Do not assume that every charging and playback combination behaves identically.

Verdict: Battery

The two models are tied on advertised battery life with ANC enabled. Sony has the more attractive stated quick-charge rate, while Bose advertises longer playback with ANC disabled and supports charging during use.

The better choice depends on whether you value quick top-ups or maximum runtime in a low-power listening mode.

6. Microphone quality and phone calls

A premium headphone can have excellent sound and noise cancellation but still be disappointing for calls.

Microphone performance depends on how well the system captures speech, suppresses unwanted background noise, and preserves the natural character of the speaker's voice.

The environment matters as well. A quiet home office, a windy street, and a crowded café present very different challenges.

Sony WH-1000XM6

Sony equips the XM6 with a microphone system and processing intended to improve voice pickup and reduce background noise.

Its beamforming and noise-reduction features are designed to help keep the speaker's voice intelligible when surrounding sounds compete for attention.

This can be useful for video meetings, voice calls, and remote work.

However, noise reduction involves trade-offs. Aggressive processing may suppress background sound while making a voice sound less natural, particularly in difficult conditions.

A microphone specification cannot establish how consistently a headphone will perform across every environment.

Bose QuietComfort Ultra (2nd Gen)

Bose also includes built-in microphones and processing intended to support clear calls.

The second-generation model is designed to improve the overall user experience, but microphone performance should be evaluated separately from noise cancellation for listening.

ANC microphones primarily help reduce sounds reaching the listener. Call microphones must capture the user's voice and distinguish it from environmental noise. These are related but different tasks.

Which is better for remote work?

RTINGS' current comparison suggests that Sony may be preferable for people who make frequent calls, although the difference is not necessarily dramatic and Bose remains capable.

That conclusion should not be interpreted as a guarantee that Sony will sound better in every meeting application or noisy environment.

If calls are central to your purchase decision, look for comparable microphone recordings made in quiet rooms, noisy offices, and windy outdoor conditions.

Also consider whether you will use the headphones with a laptop, a phone, or a separate microphone. For professional recording or streaming, a dedicated microphone may offer more consistent results than either headset's built-in system.

Verdict: Calls

Sony is the more compelling starting point if frequent calls and voice pickup are major priorities. Bose remains suitable for ordinary calls and meetings, but neither model should be chosen solely from its microphone specifications.

7. Connectivity, codecs, and wired listening

Connectivity affects more than whether the headphones can connect to a phone. It determines which audio formats are available, how easily the headphones switch between devices, and whether wired playback is possible.

Bluetooth multipoint on Sony

The WH-1000XM6 supports Bluetooth multipoint, allowing it to connect to multiple devices and switch between them according to supported behavior and settings.

For example, a user may listen to music from a computer while keeping a phone connected for incoming calls.

This is convenient for people who work across devices. However, multipoint behavior can depend on the connected devices, active applications, and audio-routing settings.

Bose Bluetooth connectivity

The second-generation QuietComfort Ultra Headphones use Bluetooth 5.4 and support Snapdragon Sound.

The benefits of particular codecs and advanced audio features depend on compatibility with the source device. Owning a headphone with a supported codec does not mean every phone or computer will use it automatically.

Before purchasing, check the specifications of your phone or laptop if you want to use a particular Bluetooth audio feature.

Sony LDAC

LDAC is Sony's higher-bitrate Bluetooth audio codec. It can be useful when the source device supports it and the connection is configured appropriately.

It does not guarantee that every listener will hear an improvement, nor does it make a compressed recording lossless.

Bluetooth transmission, source material, signal conditions, and headphone tuning all influence the result.

Bose Snapdragon Sound

Snapdragon Sound is a platform for compatible audio technologies. Its available features depend on the specific source device and implementation.

It should not be treated as a universal promise of lossless wireless playback with every smartphone.

If you are choosing between these models because of codec support, verify the precise compatibility of your phone rather than relying only on a logo or marketing description.

Wired listening

Sony supports a 3.5 mm analog connection.

Bose includes a 3.5 mm-to-2.5 mm analog audio cable, using the 2.5 mm connection on the headphone itself.

Both therefore offer an alternative to Bluetooth playback.

However, an analog cable does not automatically mean that the headphone operates passively in every mode or that every processing feature remains available without power. Check the relevant manufacturer's instructions for the operating mode you need.

USB-C charging versus USB-C audio

This distinction deserves special attention.

Sony's USB-C port is used for charging. Its specifications list analog wired listening through a stereo mini jack, and users should not assume that connecting the charging cable to a computer will provide USB digital audio.

The Bose second-generation model explicitly supports USB-C digital audio, including compatible 16-bit, 44.1 kHz or 48 kHz playback.

This can be useful for people who want a digital wired connection to a compatible computer or mobile device.

USB audio is not automatically the same as an audible improvement for every listener, but it provides an additional connection option.

Verdict: Connectivity

Sony is attractive for its multipoint support and LDAC compatibility. Bose offers a useful alternative for listeners who specifically want USB-C digital audio.

Choose based on the devices you own and the connection types you actually plan to use, not simply the newest Bluetooth version.

8. Spatial audio, listening modes, and customization

The Sony and Bose applications help turn the headphones into adjustable listening systems rather than fixed audio devices.

Sony's customization options

Sony offers an extensive set of listening controls, including equalization and ambient-sound adjustments.

These options let listeners tailor the sound and change how much outside noise they hear. Depending on the chosen settings, the headphones can be configured for focused listening or situations where environmental awareness is more important.

The main advantage is flexibility. Users who enjoy fine-tuning their audio have more opportunity to adjust the experience to their preferences.

The trade-off is complexity: more controls can take time to understand, and changing too many settings without a clear reason can make comparisons less meaningful.

Bose Immersive Audio

Bose Immersive Audio aims to create a spatialized presentation that feels more expansive than conventional headphone playback.

Its appeal is subjective. Some people enjoy the additional sense of space, while others prefer standard stereo because it feels more natural or faithful to the original mix.

Bose also provides Cinema Mode on the second-generation model, designed to enhance the experience of watching video.

These features can be valuable, but they are not a substitute for good source material or a guarantee that every recording will sound better.

Choosing between the two approaches

Sony is a natural candidate for listeners who like adjusting equalization and environmental sound settings.

Bose is particularly interesting for listeners who want spatialized playback and a more cinema-oriented mode.

Neither approach is objectively superior. The decision comes down to whether you want more direct control over sound or prefer specific listening features that are integrated into the Bose experience.

9. Build quality, portability, and everyday handling

Both headphones are designed as premium portable products, with folding mechanisms and protective carrying cases.

Their materials and moving parts should be considered in the context of everyday use rather than judged solely by appearance.

Sony's construction

Sony lists the WH-1000XM6 as weighing approximately 254 grams. The headphones use a folding design intended to make storage and travel more convenient.

The included carrying case helps protect the headphones during transport. The detachable analog cable also provides an alternative connection when needed.

As with any folding headphone, avoid forcing the hinges or storing the product under heavy objects. A carrying case reduces exposure to accidental pressure but cannot make the headphones indestructible.

Bose's construction

Bose uses plastic, aluminum, and protein-leather cushion materials in the second-generation QuietComfort Ultra Headphones.

Its foldable design is intended to support portability, while the included case protects the headphones during storage and travel.

The ear cushions are removable, which is useful when checking replacement-part availability and maintenance options.

Durability and repairability

Neither brand's premium positioning should be interpreted as proof that its headphones will last longer than the other model.

Long-term durability depends on handling, environmental conditions, battery aging, hinge use, and the availability of replacement parts.

Before buying, check the warranty terms in your country, the availability of replacement ear cushions, and the manufacturer's service arrangements.

For a product that may be used daily for several years, repair and support options can be as relevant as a small difference in weight.

Verdict: Design and portability

Both models offer portable folding designs and carrying cases. Bose uses aluminum in its construction and provides removable ear cushions; Sony offers a lightweight design with a detachable analog cable.

The more important consideration is whether the headphones feel secure, fold easily, and fit comfortably into your normal travel routine.

12. Who should skip both?

Premium ANC headphones are not the right purchase for every listener.

You may want to consider less expensive options if you rarely use noise cancellation, listen mainly at home, or do not need advanced wireless features.

You should also look elsewhere if you require a dedicated microphone for professional recording, a gaming headset with a specific low-latency connection, or a headphone designed for exercise and heavy exposure to sweat.

Neither model should be purchased on the assumption that premium pricing automatically makes it ideal for every use case.

Consider your actual listening habits, the devices you own, the comfort you need, and the features you will use frequently.

14. Final verdict: Sony WH-1000XM6 vs. Bose QuietComfort Ultra (2nd Gen)

The Sony WH-1000XM6 and Bose QuietComfort Ultra Headphones (2nd Gen) are both compelling choices for listeners seeking premium noise cancellation and everyday versatility.

Sony's strengths include extensive sound customization, LDAC support, multipoint connectivity, adaptive noise cancellation, and an attractive quick-charge specification.

Bose counters with a comfort-oriented design, spatialized listening features, Cinema Mode, and USB-C digital audio. Its advertised battery life without ANC is also slightly longer.

The differences are meaningful, but they do not establish a universal winner. A feature that matters greatly to one listener may be irrelevant to another.

If you frequently take calls, switch between devices, and enjoy adjusting your sound, the Sony WH-1000XM6 is a sensible starting point.

If you prioritize a comfortable fit for long listening sessions, enjoy spatialized audio, or want USB-C digital audio, the Bose QuietComfort Ultra Headphones (2nd Gen) deserves serious consideration.

Before buying, compare the current price of both models, check the exact generation, confirm compatibility with your devices, and try the fit if possible.

The better purchase is the headphone that solves your actual listening problems—not the one with the longest feature list.
