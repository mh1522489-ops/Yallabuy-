Quick Verdict

Buy it around $85–$100 if you commute or work at a desk and can live with a plastic build and some EQ tweaking.

Skip it if you want a folding travel headphone, the strongest ANC, strong calls in noisy places, or LDAC.

Look at the CH730N if the price gap shrinks, or if folding and longer battery would change how you use them.

The WH-CH720N is Sony's entry-level noise-cancelling over-ear headphone from February 2023. Independent reviewers largely agree on its profile: very light, long battery life, useful noise cancelling for the price, and a bass-heavy sound that you can tame in the app. They also agree on its weak spots: a plastic build, no folding mechanism, no carry case, and calls that range from average to adequate.

The more important news for a buyer in late 2026 is that Sony has launched a successor, the WH-CH730N, at a $179.99 US list price. That changes the question from "are these good?" to "are these good at today's price compared with the newer model and with other options?"

Buying context: the WH-CH720N has been succeeded

Sony announced the WH-CH730N on September 7, 2026. Sony's press release describes it as the updated mid-range model, with a foldable design, longer battery life, and improved call quality compared with its predecessor. Sony lists it at $179.99 in the US.

That does not make the WH-CH720N obsolete. Older headphones often become better buys when the successor arrives, because retailers clear stock and prices fall. We could not find an official Sony discontinuation notice for the WH-CH720N, and deal coverage in August and October 2026 shows it still on sale at Amazon. Availability can shift, so treat that as a snapshot.

A fuller comparison with the newer model appears later in this article.

What you actually get for the money

The WH-CH720N sits below Sony's flagship 1000X line. Notebookcheck reported at launch that Sony pointed to the same Integrated Processor V1 used for noise cancelling in the 1000X range, and described the model as Sony's lightest over-ear wireless headphone to date, in its launch report. The same report notes the savings: smaller 30 mm drivers and no support for high-bitrate codecs such as LDAC or aptX.

The practical feature list, according to Sony's specifications and the reviews cited here:

  • Active noise cancelling with an ambient mode, adjustable in the app. Trusted Reviews describes 20 adjustment levels.
  • Bluetooth multipoint, so one pair of headphones can stay connected to a phone and a laptop.
  • DSEE upscaling, which tries to improve compressed audio, and support for Sony 360 Reality Audio on supported streaming services.
  • A wired mode that keeps working even when the battery is flat, though SoundGuys notes the sound changes noticeably in that passive state.
  • Physical buttons on the right earcup, instead of touch controls.

What is missing is as informative as what is included. There is no folding hinge and no carry case, which What Hi-Fi, Trusted Reviews, and SoundGuys each flag. There is no wear detection, and no hi-res Bluetooth codec. At the price these headphones sell for in deals, those omissions are reasonable trade-offs. At the original list price, they are harder to excuse.

Design, build, and comfort

Weight and fit

At about 192 g, the WH-CH720N is light for a full-size over-ear headphone. Notebookcheck's pre-launch report listed the previous CH710N at 223 g, which puts the saving at roughly 14 percent.

Independent verdicts on comfort are positive with caveats. What Hi-Fi gave comfort full marks, citing padding on the headband and ear cups. Trusted Reviews found the clamp force light but secure and the earcups roomy for larger ears. RTINGS describes the over-ear fit as a bit more comfortable than the on-ear WH-CH520, in its comparison page. SoundGuys said the pads can heat up and feel sticky after about an hour, attributing it to the vinyl covering, and warned that glasses wearers might feel extra pressure.

Build quality: reviewers disagree

What Hi-Fi called the build simple but sturdy. Trusted Reviews said the recycled-plastic frame does not creak when flexed and survived several weeks in a rucksack, though it cannot hide being plastic. Expert Reviews listed a cheap feel among its cons, and SoundGuys described plasticky housings with raised seams and rough corners at the headband adjustment.

Those views can all be true at once. A frame can pass a short flex and scratch test and still feel inexpensive. Longevity is the open question, which the owner section below addresses.

The earcups swivel flat but the headphones do not fold, and there is no case. What Hi-Fi says the Earfun Wave Pro offers a sturdier feel and a fold at this level. All controls except power sit on the right earcup. SoundGuys and Trusted Reviews both found the buttons easy to mix up at first, and SoundGuys notes the button mapping cannot be changed in the app.

Noise cancelling

Reviewers agree on the shape of the result: effective on low-frequency rumble, weaker on voices, and a tier below Sony's flagships.

What was measured

SoundGuys, which bought its own unit and tested it over 10 days, reports around 20 dB of reduction for most lower-pitched noise, peaking near 28 dB at 80 Hz. It calls the isolation slightly below average and the ANC fine, adding that this would have been top-tier a few years earlier. Its product page lists an isolation and ANC score of 4.9 out of 10.

RTINGS, using Test Bench 1.8, reports overall attenuation of about 18.65 dB with ANC on, split roughly into 10.71 dB in the bass, 18.69 dB in the mids, and 28.26 dB in the treble. For common scenarios it reports about 10.91 dB for airplane noise, 11.77 dB for office noise, and 13.00 dB for street noise.

The two sets of numbers should not be read side by side. The labs use different equipment, frequency bands, and weighting, so "20 dB at low frequencies" and "10.71 dB in the bass band" describe different things. The safe reading is that both found meaningful reduction and neither found flagship-class results.

What reviewers heard

What Hi-Fi found the ANC effective at dampening road noise without silencing it, and said premium models such as the WH-1000XM5 and Bose QuietComfort 45 block more. Trusted Reviews found engine rumble and large crowds handled well, while individual voices can stay audible.

YallaBuy analysis

For a bus or train commute, the evidence suggests a clear gain over passive isolation alone. In a chatty open-plan office, expect a smaller effect. RTINGS' airplane figure suggests noticeable but modest reduction on a flight. If strong ANC is the main goal, Tom's Guide says the WH-1000XM5 can often be found on sale around $179 in its CH730N review. That is a different price tier, but it frames the choice.

Sound quality

Where the evidence agrees

Every review we read describes a bass-forward tuning. What Hi-Fi says the bass can become over-enthusiastic by default. Trusted Reviews describes a low-frequency boost with a lively but unbalanced character. SoundGuys measured exaggerated response between about 100 Hz and 400 Hz and raised treble between 5 kHz and 10 kHz, and describes vocals as slightly congested and treble that can tire the ears. RTINGS' comparison text calls the cheaper CH520 less bass-heavy out of the box, which implies the CH720N is the more bass-heavy of the two.

What Hi-Fi and SoundGuys both point to the same fix, the equalizer in Sony's app. What Hi-Fi turned the bass down a notch and preferred the result.

Where the evidence differs

The disagreement is about how good the sound is. What Hi-Fi gave five stars for sound and praised rhythm, drive, and detail for the price. SoundGuys' MDAQS model gave an overall predicted listener score of 3.3 out of 5 (timbre 3.2, distortion 2.9, immersiveness 4.7), and its product page places that in the bottom quarter of over-ears scored this way. Trusted Reviews sits between, enjoying the sound but wanting more detail.

The methods explain much of the gap. What Hi-Fi uses a human panel and scores relative to price. MDAQS is a model-predicted average-listener score on an absolute scale. A bassy tuning that a panel finds fun can score lower in a model that rewards tonal accuracy.

YallaBuy analysis

For rock, hip-hop, pop, and electronic music at moderate volume, the default tuning is likely to feel energetic, and What Hi-Fi's testing suggests it suits those genres. For acoustic, classical, or vocal-led music, the bass boost and congested vocals SoundGuys describes may bother you unless you use the EQ. Plan on adjusting it.

Wired listening has a catch. SoundGuys found the sound matches wireless when plugged in and powered on, but in passive mode with a flat battery it significantly cuts treble and boosts lows and mids.

Battery life

Battery is the cleanest case of manufacturer claim, independent tests, and owner feedback pointing the same way.

Manufacturer claim. Sony rates music playback at up to 35 hours with ANC on and up to 50 hours with it off.

Independent tests.

  • SoundGuys measured 40 hours 11 minutes with ANC on in its standard battery test.
  • RTINGS measured about 38.4 hours of continuous battery life in its comparison data.
  • Trusted Reviews ran a streaming drain test and saw the battery fall 10 percent after four hours at about 50 percent volume, which extrapolates to roughly 40 hours.

The three tests used different volume levels, sources, and methods, so the numbers are not directly comparable. Still, all three came in above Sony's 35-hour figure, which suggests the rating is conservative for typical use. Real life will vary with volume, codec, and temperature.

Owner feedback. Best Buy reviewers repeatedly mention long battery life, and a CDW reviewer reported about a week of use at 5 to 10 hours per day. Those are individual accounts, but they fit the lab data.

YallaBuy analysis. As an illustration, not a measured result: someone listening about three hours a day on a 35 to 40 hour battery might charge roughly once every 11 to 13 days. For most commuters, that is a real convenience. The advantage over rivals depends on the comparison. Sony's newer CH730N claims up to 50 hours, and the WH-CH520 measured at 52.8 hours in RTINGS' data, so the CH720N is long-lasting rather than category-leading.

Quick charging helps in a pinch. SoundGuys reports Sony's claim that three minutes of charging gives about an hour of playback. We did not find an independent measurement of that figure.

Calls and microphone

Call quality is the area where reviewers are least aligned.

  • Trusted Reviews found the performance average: the other person heard it fine in quiet places but struggled in noisier ones.
  • Expert Reviews listed sub-par microphone quality as a con.
  • SoundGuys called the mic quite good for a headphone microphone, with some background attenuation, some keystrokes getting through, and wind noise filtered particularly well.

A reader vote on SoundGuys' page, which is an informal poll and not a controlled test, put the microphone at 12 percent awful or bad, 42 percent okay, and 46 percent good or perfect. Owner comments echo the split. One Best Buy reviewer praised the sound but called the microphone subpar, and one SoundGuys community owner said the mic distorted slightly with a loud speaking voice while calling everything else better than expected.

YallaBuy analysis. The evidence supports "adequate, with limits." For occasional calls in a quiet room or a video meeting, expect it to work. For regular calls from a busy street or open office, the reports suggest you may want to consider a model with a stronger microphone reputation. Sony says the newer WH-CH730N improves call quality, though independent verdicts on that claim are still thin, and What Hi-Fi's review notes the newer model's ANC struggles with outside human speech.

Connectivity and codecs

Sony's specification page lists Bluetooth 5.2 with the SBC and AAC audio formats and a range of about 10 m. Multipoint lets the headphones stay connected to two devices. Trusted Reviews found the connection stable with only a couple of blips in busy train stations, and SoundGuys reported a stable connection throughout its test, though it had trouble establishing the first connection with an iPhone.

The LDAC conflict

One source says these headphones support LDAC and most others say they do not. SoundGuys' review text and its product specification list LDAC, AAC, and SBC. By contrast, Sony's own specification page lists only SBC and AAC, and What Hi-Fi's tech specs, Trusted Reviews, Expert Reviews, and Notebookcheck's launch report all say there is no LDAC support.

The weight of evidence, including the manufacturer's own page, points to SBC and AAC only. The SoundGuys listing appears to be an error, although we cannot confirm why it differs. If LDAC matters to you, for example because you use an Android phone and a high-bitrate music service, do not buy this model expecting it.

YallaBuy analysis. AAC works well with Apple devices, as SoundGuys notes. On Android, the headphones will fall back to AAC or SBC. Most casual listeners will not hear a dramatic difference, but this is a real gap for people who want the best wireless quality and who have the source files to benefit from it.

App and software

Sony's companion app, called Headphones Connect in the reviews cited here, controls the EQ, noise cancelling and ambient levels, listening modes, DSEE, 360 Reality Audio setup, and firmware updates. SoundGuys notes that using all features requires creating an account. Because the EQ is the best fix for the bass-heavy tuning, the app is close to essential, so people who dislike sharing data with apps will get a reduced experience.

One Best Buy reviewer (3 stars) said the app is required for full noise cancelling. We could not verify that, and the other reviews we read describe ANC working from the buttons, with the app adding finer control. It is a single owner report. We also found no information on how long Sony will provide firmware updates for this 2023 model.

What owners report

Owner feedback can reveal problems that short reviews miss, but it skews toward strong experiences, good or bad. Treat these as patterns in reports, not statistics.

Positive patterns

Best Buy's summary says customers are positive about noise cancelling, comfort, sound, battery life, price, light weight, and connectivity. The listing shows well over 2,800 reviews, but many reviewers disclose promotional consideration or a sweepstakes entry, which makes the average rating a weaker signal. There are also long-term positive accounts, such as an Adorama reviewer who described almost three years of daily use, including gym use and accidental drops, with only a small scuff and a wrinkle in the headband pad.

Hinge, swivel, and slider failures

The most consistent complaint involves moving plastic parts breaking.

  • iFixit hosts at least two separate question threads on the WH-CH720N, one about a broken hinge or swivel and one about a headband slider. In the hinge thread, one owner said it broke a second time and that a replacement hinge cost about $32.
  • A Best Buy reviewer reported the rotating piece broke after eight months.
  • A CDW reviewer said two units each lost an earpiece at the headband connection and that Sony declined a remedy because it does not cover accidental or physical damage. That is a single account, with Sony's side not shown.
  • A Slickdeals commenter in August 2026 said the hinge broke after about a year.
  • A Walmart reviewer reported four years of daily use before the plastic began to break down.

These are multiple independent reports from different sites, so calling hinge and swivel breakage a recurring owner complaint is justified. They do not establish a failure rate. People with broken headphones are more likely to post in repair forums and retailer reviews, and the three-year account above shows long life is possible. The fair conclusion is a potential durability concern of unknown frequency.

An Adorama reviewer also reported static in the left earpiece shortly after purchase, which we treat as an isolated report.

Independent reactions to the newer model are mixed on value. Tom's Guide scored it 4 out of 5 overall and praised its sound, ANC, comfort, and battery, but rated price 2.5 out of 5 and called the US pricing hard to defend. It still said it would choose the newer model over the older one for its improvements. What Hi-Fi tested it at £100 / $180 / AU$230 and noted that its ANC struggles with outside human speech. SoundGuys scored it 7.8 out of 10 and listed somewhat weak ANC and odd tuning among its dislikes. SoundGuys' CH720N score is 7.1 out of 10, but the two were scored at different times and the CH720N rating was revised in March 2025, so they are not a clean head-to-head.

Should existing owners upgrade? This looks like a moderate, situational upgrade. Foldability and battery matter most to travelers. For a commuter whose CH720N works fine, the evidence does not suggest an upgrade at $179.99 is worthwhile. An owner with a broken hinge may find a repair or a discounted replacement more economical.

Which should a new buyer choose? At roughly $85 to $100 against $179.99, the CH720N is the better value for most buyers who do not need folding. If the gap narrows to a small amount, the newer model's design and battery claims become more compelling. These are YallaBuy's judgments from the evidence, and we have tested neither model.

Who should buy the WH-CH720N

  • A student or commuter who wants real noise reduction, a light headphone for long days, and does not want to spend flagship money.
  • Someone who charges rarely and values battery life over other specs.
  • A person who uses both a phone and a laptop and wants one headphone to stay connected to both.
  • A listener who likes bass-forward music and is willing to spend a few minutes in the app adjusting the EQ.
  • A buyer who sees the price near $85 to $100 and accepts that the build is plastic.
  • A user who wants a wired fallback for flights or a flat battery.

Who should skip it

  • Frequent flyers who want a folding headphone with a protective case, since neither is included.
  • Listeners who want a balanced, accurate sound without EQ work, because every review describes the default as bass-heavy.
  • Anyone for whom strong ANC against voices and airplane cabin noise is the main goal.
  • Heavy callers who work from noisy places, given the average to sub-par microphone verdicts from two reviewers.
  • Android users who specifically want LDAC or another high-bitrate codec.
  • Buyers who are nervous about hinge durability and do not want any risk of that failure, given the recurring owner reports.
  • Anyone who would pay close to the $179.99 list price, where better-equipped options enter the picture.

Value for money

Two questions need separating: is the WH-CH720N a good product, and is it a good purchase today?

As a product, independent reviewers rate it well for its tier. What Hi-Fi gave it five stars and repeated awards, Trusted Reviews called it a reliable all-rounder, and SoundGuys recommended it with 7.1 out of 10. The lab data supports strong battery life and meaningful, if not flagship-grade, noise reduction.

As a purchase, price decides. At roughly $85 to $100, the mix of ANC, multipoint, long battery life, and a wired option is hard to match among name-brand headphones. At $179.99, the case weakens: Tom's Guide notes the flagship XM5 has sold for about that price on sale, and the CH730N lists at that price with a fold and a longer claimed battery. There is also ownership risk. If a hinge fails outside warranty, owners report paying for parts or replacing the headphones, which is easier to accept at under $100 than at $180.

Final verdict

The WH-CH720N is good value at the prices it has reached in 2026 and poor value at its list price. Sources agree on its strengths: light weight, battery life above Sony's own rating in three independent tests, ANC that helps on commutes, and flexible connections. They also agree on its weaknesses: a plastic build with owner-reported breakage, bass-heavy tuning that needs the app, ANC behind premium models, average calls, no folding or case, and no high-bitrate codec.

YallaBuy's conclusion, from the evidence and not from testing:

  • Buy it around $85 to $100 if you commute or work at a desk and can live with a plastic build and some EQ tweaking.
  • Skip it if you want a folding travel headphone, the strongest ANC, strong calls in noisy places, or LDAC.
  • Look at the CH730N if the price gap shrinks, or if folding and longer battery would change how you use them.
