Who Should NOT Buy the Sony WH-1000XM5?

By Mahmoud Hamdy · Founder & Editor
Editorial Transparency

YallaBuy has not personally tested this product. This article is based on Sony's official specifications, independent testing from specialized outlets, and recurring patterns found in user reviews on platforms such as Adorama and community forums. The source of each claim is noted directly next to it.

Quick Verdict

The Sony WH-1000XM5 is a strong noise-cancelling headphone in its category, and it has held up well since its 2022 launch. However, it is not the right choice for every buyer. The most significant concern, based on repeated user reports, is a hinge that can crack or fail, combined with the loss of the full-fold design that previous generations offered.

You may want to avoid it if you:
  • Travel frequently and need headphones that fold down compactly
  • Prefer not to worry about long-term durability
  • Have a smaller head or wear glasses and need a lighter clamping fit
  • Want the newest noise-cancelling technology available today
It may still suit you if you:
  • Want strong noise cancellation at a lower price than the newer XM6
  • Use it mainly at home or in the office rather than while traveling constantly
  • Don't mind handling it with a bit more care than usual
  • Prioritize call quality for meetings and calls

Who Was This Headphone Designed For in the First Place?

Sony markets the WH-1000XM5 as using two dedicated processors (the QN1 chip and the Integrated Processor V1) to control eight microphones for active noise cancellation, with up to 30 hours of battery life with ANC enabled Manufacturer Claim. These are official company figures, not independent lab results, but they give a reasonable sense of the intended use case: long flights, noisy commutes, open-plan offices, and extended listening sessions where clean audio and clear calls matter.

Given that positioning, the XM5 is primarily built for people who spend significant time in moderately noisy environments and need clear calls. That doesn't mean it's the best fit for everyone, which is where this guide comes in.

The Reasons This Headphone Might Not Be Right For You

1. A Recurring Hinge Durability Problem

This is the most-discussed concern with the WH-1000XM5. A reader survey conducted by SoundGuys with more than 2,000 respondents found that around 47% experienced some form of failure, with the headband/hinge breaking being the single most common issue, reported by roughly 24% of respondents Independent Test/Survey. Similar complaints appear repeatedly in user reviews on Adorama, where several buyers described the hinge cracking within months of normal use, without any reported drop or unusual pressure Customer Feedback.

It's worth being precise about what this evidence does and doesn't show: these are user reports and a reader survey, not a controlled statistical study covering every unit sold, so they don't establish an official failure rate. That said, the same complaint recurring across multiple independent sources — a testing publication's survey and separate customer reviews on a retail platform — moves this from an isolated complaint into a pattern worth taking seriously YallaBuy Analysis. If you handle your gear roughly, or plan to keep these headphones for several years, this is a point that deserves real consideration before buying.

2. It No Longer Folds Flat

Unlike the XM4 and XM3 before it, Sony moved away from a fully collapsible design in the XM5 Manufacturer Specification, replacing it with a flatter form that only allows the ear cups to swivel, not the headband to fold inward. A number of users on forums like Slickdeals and GSMArena viewed this as a step backward for portability Customer Feedback. Notably, Sony itself reversed course for the next generation: coverage from Notebookcheck indicates the WH-1000XM6 returns to a foldable hinge design, seemingly as a direct response to feedback on the XM5 Independent Tech Publication.

If your travel bag has limited space, or you're stuffing the case into a crowded backpack daily, this design change could be a genuine practical drawback rather than a minor inconvenience.

3. Fit May Not Work for Every Head Shape

Independent testing from TechGearLab found the fit can be uncomfortable for people with smaller heads, and noted that the soft ear cushions compress easily over time, sometimes leading to sweat buildup during long sessions Independent Test. The team also observed that the XM5's shallower, less clamping ear pads compared with the XM4 can reduce seal consistency for certain face shapes, which may affect isolation. These are qualitative observations from one testing team rather than a comprehensive fit study across many users, but they suggest comfort is not uniform for everyone.

4. Touch Controls May Degrade Over Time

One user reviewing the headphones on Adorama described the touch-sensitive controls becoming inconsistent after extended use, speculating that moisture or repeated pressure on the touch panel was the cause Individual Customer Report. This is a single report, and there isn't currently enough evidence to call it a common issue, but it's worth noting as a possible long-term annoyance for some owners.

5. A Newer Generation (XM6) Exists, and the Price Gap Has Narrowed

Sony launched the next-generation WH-1000XM6 at an official list price of $449.99 Manufacturer's Official Launch Price, featuring a newer QN3 noise-cancelling processor, 12 microphones instead of 8, and a return to a fully foldable design. As WH-1000XM5 pricing has dropped repeatedly during sales events, the price gap between the two generations can shrink significantly during promotions — which matters most to buyers who want the latest technology and aren't working with a tight budget.

What About the Strengths? (For Balance)

In fairness, the WH-1000XM5 isn't a poor product — quite the opposite. It continues to hold strong ratings on SoundGuys even after the XM6's release Independent Rating. Noise cancellation quality, call clarity from its eight-microphone array Manufacturer Claim, and relatively light weight (approximately 250g according to Sony's official specs Manufacturer Specification) make it a genuinely competitive option in its class. One reviewer who tested it specifically as a gaming headset found no noticeable audio lag over a wired connection, and rated its microphone performance on par with dedicated gaming headsets in the same price range Independent Test.

In other words, the strengths are real and documented — they just don't cancel out the durability and portability concerns raised above. The right call depends on your own priorities as a buyer.

Alternatives Worth Considering

If your main concerns are hinge durability and foldability, it's worth widening your search before committing. The Bose QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds Gen 2, for example, use a completely different form factor (in-ear rather than over-ear), which eliminates the hinge problem entirely — though it comes with its own trade-offs in battery life and long-wear comfort that deserve separate research. For buyers who want to stay within the same over-ear category, moving up to the newer WH-1000XM6 is the more logical choice if budget allows, given the return to a foldable design and the claimed improvements to the noise-cancelling processor.

To be clear, this isn't a firm recommendation of any specific alternative — it's an invitation to weigh your own priorities (price, comfort, portability, and durability) before settling on any single option.

Final Verdict

The Sony WH-1000XM5 is a solid product by the standards of its category, but it isn't the best fit for every buyer. If long-term durability and genuine foldability for frequent travel are high on your list, you may have a better experience with an alternative like the Bose QuietComfort Ultra, or by moving directly to the newer WH-1000XM6 if your budget allows. On the other hand, if your priorities are strong noise cancellation and good call quality at a lower price than the newest generation, and you don't mind handling the headphones with a bit more care, the WH-1000XM5 remains a reasonable option today.

The goal of this guide isn't to talk anyone out of buying it — it's to give you a realistic, unbiased picture of this specific product's documented weak points, so your decision is based on complete information rather than manufacturer marketing or early-launch review hype.
